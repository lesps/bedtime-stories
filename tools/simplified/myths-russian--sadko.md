---
title: Sadko and the King of the Sea
basedOn: the Novgorod bylina (sung epic) of Sadko, following the outline of it that Isabel F. Hapgood gives in A Survey of Russian Literature (1902)
workId: russian-sadko
order: 6
---

Long ago, in the great city of Novgorod, there lived a harp player called Sadko. He had no gold and no treasure. All he had was his harp. He went to the grand feasts of the merchants and the lords, and played for them, and made everyone merry.

But one day nobody invited Sadko to a feast. And nobody invited him the next day, or the day after that. So Sadko was sad, and he went down to the shore of Lake Ilmen, and sat by the water, and played his harp all day long.

On the third day, the Water King himself rose out of the lake.

"Thank you, Sadko," said the Water King. "All this time you have been playing, my guests down in the deep have been feasting and dancing to your music. Now I will reward you. Go back to Novgorod. Tomorrow you will be invited to a feast. When the merchants start boasting about how rich they are, make a bet with them. Bet your own head against all their shops and everything in them, that in Lake Ilmen there are fish with fins of gold."

So Sadko went back to Novgorod. The next day he was invited to a feast, and the merchants began to boast, just as the Water King had said. "I'll make a bet with you," said Sadko. "In Lake Ilmen there are fish with fins of gold."

And the merchants bet their shops against Sadko's head.

So they all went down to the lake, and cast a silken net into the water. And the Water King sent up the fish, and when they pulled in the net, there were fish in it with fins of pure gold.

So Sadko won the bet, and he became one of the richest merchants in Novgorod. He built himself a palace of white stone, wonderfully decorated. And now he held feasts of his own.

At one of his feasts, Sadko boasted, "I am so rich that I can buy up everything for sale in all of Novgorod. And if I can't, I will pay thirty thousand in gold."

So the next day Sadko began to buy. He bought and bought. But as fast as he bought, more goods kept flowing into the city. At last Sadko saw that he could never buy up everything. He was wise enough to give up, and he paid his thirty thousand.

Then he built thirty-three red ships, shaped like dragons, and loaded them with goods from Novgorod. And he sailed away down the river Volkhov, across Lake Ladoga, down the river Neva, and out onto the open sea.

For twelve years Sadko sailed the seas. Then one day, in the middle of the sea, his ships stopped still. The waves splashed, and the wind whistled in the sails, but the ships would not move.

"It must be the King of the Sea," said Sadko. "For twelve years we have sailed on his seas, and never paid him anything. He wants his share."

So they threw barrels of red gold into the sea, and barrels of pure silver, and barrels of round pearls. But still the ships would not move.

"Then the King of the Sea must want one of us," said Sadko. "Let each man make himself a lot, and throw it into the sea. Whoever's lot sinks must go down to the King of the Sea."

So they all threw their lots into the sea. And all the other lots floated, but Sadko's lot sank. He tried again with a lot as light as a hop flower, and it sank. He tried with a lot of heavy steel, and all the other lots floated, and his sank again.

"Then I am the one the King wants," said Sadko. He took his harp, and an icon of Saint Nicholas, the protector of travellers, and bowls full of treasure. And he climbed down onto a plank of oak, and floated there on the waves. And at once the ships sailed off, flying over the water like black ravens.

Then Sadko sank down, down to the bottom of the sea, and found himself in the palace of the King of the Sea.

"Play for me, Sadko," said the King. So Sadko played, and the King of the Sea began to dance, and all the sea maidens and sea people danced with him, wilder and wilder.

But the Queen of the Sea whispered to Sadko, "Break your harp, Sadko! Up above, the King's dancing is stirring up the waves, and they are crashing on the shore and doing terrible damage."

So Sadko broke his harp, and the dancing stopped.

"Now, Sadko," said the King, "you must stay here and marry one of my sea maidens. Choose a wife."

And again the Queen whispered to him, "Let them all go past, and choose the very last one."

So nine hundred sea maidens walked past Sadko. He waited, and at the very end came a little maiden called Chernava. And Sadko chose her. If he had chosen any of the others, he would never have seen the world above again, but would have stayed for ever in the blue sea.

The King of the Sea held a great wedding feast. And when it was over, Sadko fell into a deep sleep.

When he woke up, he was lying on the bank of the river Chernava, near Novgorod. And there, racing up the river Volkhov towards him, were his thirty-three red ships.

So Sadko went home to his palace, and his wife in Novgorod. He built two churches, to give thanks. And he never went roaming again, but lived happily ever after in his own city.
