---
title: Sosruko, the Child of the Stone
basedOn: the Nart sagas of the Circassian peoples of the northern Caucasus, which collectors began writing down in the nineteenth century, in the story of how the hero Sosruko was born from a stone and tempered by the smith Tlepsh
workId: circassian-sosruko-stone
order: 1
---

Long ago, in the mountains of the Caucasus, there lived the Narts, a people of mighty heroes. And the wisest of all the Narts was a woman called Satanaya. She knew everything that would happen, and the Narts came to her for advice.

One day Satanaya went down to the river to wash clothes. On the other side of the river, a shepherd was watching his flock. When he saw how beautiful Satanaya was, he fell in love with her. But the river was wide and deep, and he could not cross it. And something wonderful happened. The shepherd's love flew across the river and struck a great stone that lay on the bank beside Satanaya.

Satanaya, who knew everything, understood at once. "A child will be born from this stone," she said. So she took the stone home with her, and kept it safe, and watched over it.

Day by day, the stone grew warmer, and bigger. And after nine months, Satanaya could hear something moving inside it.

Then she went to Tlepsh, the great smith of the Narts, who could forge anything out of iron. "Tlepsh," she said, "come and open this stone. There is a child inside it."

So Tlepsh came with his hammer, and struck the stone, and it split open. And inside was a baby boy. But he was not an ordinary baby. His body was made of hot, glowing metal, red-hot like iron in the forge, so hot that sparks flew from him.

"This child must be tempered," said Tlepsh, "like a sword, to make him hard and strong."

So Tlepsh took the baby in his great iron tongs, holding him by the knees. And he dipped him into cold water, again and again, until the hot metal hissed and steamed and cooled, and the baby's body became as hard as the finest steel.

Everywhere the water touched him, he became so hard that no sword or arrow could ever hurt him. But where the tongs had gripped his knees, the water could not reach. So his knees stayed soft, like any other person's. That was his only weak place.

Satanaya named the baby Sosruko, and brought him up as her own son. And he grew up to be the boldest and cleverest of all the Nart heroes. He was not the biggest or the strongest of them, but nobody could ever outwit him.

Many stories are told about Sosruko: how he won fire for the Narts when they were freezing, how he beat giants and sly enemies with his cleverness, and how he was always the first to help his people. But that is how he began, as a red-hot child of the stone, hardened in the water by Tlepsh the smith.
