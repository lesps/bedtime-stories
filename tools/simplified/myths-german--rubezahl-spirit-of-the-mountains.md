---
title: Rübezahl, Spirit of the Mountains
basedOn: the legend as Johann Karl August Musäus told it in his Volksmärchen der Deutschen (1783), and later folk tales of the Giant Mountains
workId: german-rubezahl
order: 12
---

High in the Giant Mountains, between Silesia and Bohemia, there lives a mountain spirit. Deep under the mountains he is the lord of the gnomes and of all the treasures of the earth. But sometimes he comes up into the daylight to see what people are doing, and then he can take any shape he likes: a woodcutter, a traveller, a raven or a stone. People call him Rübezahl. But you had better not say that name when he can hear you. This is how he got it.

Long ago, the mountain spirit came up into the world after a very long time underground, and wandered through the forests. One day he heard laughter, and he saw a king's daughter, the Princess Emma, playing with her maidens by a cool pool in the woods. She was so lovely that the spirit fell in love with her at once. And because he was a spirit, and did not understand people at all, he did not think to ask her. He made the ground open, and he carried the princess down to his palace deep under the mountains.

The palace was full of wonders, halls of crystal and silver, and gardens where jewels grew like flowers. The spirit gave Emma everything she asked for. But she was lonely. She missed her father and her friends, and the young prince, Ratibor, whom she was going to marry. And she cried and cried.

So the spirit brought her a basket of turnips from his fields, and a little magic wand. "Touch a turnip with this," he said, "and it will turn into anyone you wish."

Emma touched a turnip, and there was her best friend! She touched more, and there were all her maidens, and her little dog, and her cat. For a while she was happy. But after a few days she saw that her friends were growing pale and thin and wrinkled. And soon they all shrivelled up and turned back into dried-up old turnips. For the magic only lasted as long as the turnips were fresh.

"Please bring me more turnips," said Emma. But the basket was empty. So the spirit planted a whole field of turnips for her. And while they grew, Emma thought of a plan.

When the field was full of ripe turnips, she said to the spirit, "Before I can be happy here, I must know exactly how many turnips there are in that field. Please go and count them for me, every single one, and don't make a mistake."

So the spirit hurried off to count them. He counted and counted, but he lost his place, and he had to start all over again. And again. And while he was busy counting, Emma picked a turnip, touched it with the wand, and turned it into a swift horse. She jumped on its back and galloped away, up out of the mountain, all the way home to her father and to Prince Ratibor.

When the spirit had counted every last turnip, he hurried back to tell Emma the number. But she was gone! He raced after her, but she had already crossed the edge of his mountains, where his magic could not reach. And from far away he heard her laughing and calling him a name. "Rübezahl! Turnip-Counter!"

The mountain spirit was so angry and so ashamed that he stormed back into the mountains. Thunder rolled, and rocks came crashing down the slopes. And ever since then, he hates to hear that name. If anyone calls out "Rübezahl!" in the mountains, a storm may suddenly blow up, or the traveller may lose his way in the fog.

But the spirit had learned something about people. After that, he spent his time watching them, in one shape or another. And the old stories say that he helps the poor and the honest, and plays tricks on the greedy and the proud. A poor woman gathering firewood might find that her bundle of sticks has turned to gold. A rich man who cheated people might find that his money has turned to stones.

So if you ever walk in the Giant Mountains, be kind and be honest. You never know whether the old woodcutter you meet on the path is really the spirit of the mountains.
