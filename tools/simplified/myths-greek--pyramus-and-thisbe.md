---
title: Pyramus and Thisbe

Long ago, in the great city of Babylon, two families lived side by side. In one house lived a young man named Pyramus. In the other lived a young woman named Thisbe. They grew up as neighbors, and as they grew, they fell in love.

But their parents would not let them marry. They would not even let them meet.

Between the two houses was a stone wall, and in the wall was a tiny crack. No one else had ever noticed it. But Pyramus and Thisbe found it. Every evening they stood on either side of the wall and whispered to each other through the crack.

"Oh, wall," they said, "why must you keep us apart?" Then each of them kissed their own side of the wall goodnight.

At last they made a plan. One night, when everyone was asleep, they would sneak out of the city. They would meet at an old stone monument in the fields, under a white mulberry tree beside a cool spring.

That night, Thisbe slipped out first, with a veil over her face. She came to the mulberry tree and sat down to wait.

Then, in the moonlight, she saw a lioness come to the spring to drink. Its mouth was red from its last meal. Thisbe jumped up and ran to hide in a cave. As she ran, her veil fell off.

The lioness drank. On its way back to the woods, it found the veil, sniffed it and tore it with its bloody mouth. Then it padded away.

Soon Pyramus arrived. He saw the lion's footprints in the sand. Then he found Thisbe's veil, torn and stained red. His face went white.

"Oh, Thisbe," he cried. "A lion has taken you, and it is my fault for asking you to come here alone." His heart was broken. He lay down under the mulberry tree, and there he died. His blood ran into the roots of the tree, and the white mulberries turned dark red.

After a while, Thisbe crept out of the cave. She was still shaking, but she wanted to tell Pyramus how she had escaped. When she came to the tree, the berries were red, and she wondered if she was in the wrong place.

Then she saw Pyramus lying on the ground. She ran to him and held him and called his name. For one moment he opened his eyes and looked at her. Then they closed forever.

Thisbe saw her torn veil, and she understood what had happened. "We could not be together in life," she whispered. "So we will be together now." Her heart broke too, and she lay down beside him under the tree and died.

When their parents found them, they were very sorry. At last they understood how much the two had loved each other. They laid Pyramus and Thisbe to rest together, side by side.

And from that day on, the mulberries on that tree, and on every mulberry tree, have turned dark red when they are ripe, to remember the two young people who loved each other so much.
