---
title: Idun's Golden Apples
adaptedFrom: blackwell-prose-edda--iduna-and-her-apples

namesNote: Blackwell spells Idun's name “Iduna” and the giant's name “Thjassi.”
---

The goddess Idun had golden apples. They were very special apples. Whenever the gods began to grow old, they took a bite of Idun's apples, and they became young again. That is how the gods stayed young and strong forever.

One day, Odin, Loki and another god named Hoenir went on a journey. They grew hungry, and when they came to a valley with a herd of oxen in it, they took one and put it in a pot over the fire to cook.

They waited, and then they lifted the lid. The meat was still raw. They waited some more, and lifted the lid again. Still raw! It would not cook at all.

Then they heard a voice above them. Up in an oak tree sat a huge eagle.

"If you let me have my share of the meat," said the eagle, "I'll make it cook."

"All right," said the gods. And at once the meat was cooked. But the eagle swooped down and grabbed a leg and both shoulders, the very best parts!

Loki was furious. He grabbed a big stick and whacked the eagle on the back. But the stick stuck fast to the eagle's back, and Loki's hands stuck fast to the other end of the stick! The eagle flew up into the air, dragging Loki along behind him, bumping over rocks and crashing through trees, until Loki thought his arms would be pulled off.

It was not really an eagle at all. It was a giant named Thiazi, wearing an eagle's feathers.

"Please! Let me go!" begged Loki.

"I'll let you go," said the giant, "but only if you promise to bring me Idun and her golden apples."

Loki promised. And he went back to his friends, all bruised and scratched.

When they got home to Asgard, Loki went to see Idun. "Idun," he said, "I found a tree in the forest with apples on it that are even better than yours. Bring your apples and come and compare."

Idun believed him. She took her box of apples and went with Loki into the forest. And as soon as they got there, the giant swooped down in his eagle feathers, snatched up Idun and her apples, and carried her off to Giantland.

Back in Asgard, without Idun's apples, the gods began to grow old. Their hair turned grey. Their faces grew wrinkled. Their backs began to bend. Soon they were very old indeed.

"Where is Idun?" they asked. "What has happened to her?" And soon they found out that, as usual, Loki was to blame.

"Bring her back," they told Loki, "or you'll be very sorry!"

So Loki borrowed Freyja's cloak of falcon feathers, and he flew to Giantland. He was lucky. Thiazi was out at sea, fishing. Loki found Idun, changed her into a little sparrow, held her in his claws and flew away as fast as he could.

But when Thiazi came home and found Idun gone, he put on his eagle feathers and came flying after them. The eagle was big and fast, and he was catching up!

The old, grey gods stood on the walls of Asgard, watching Loki the falcon flying toward them with the little sparrow in his claws, and the huge eagle right behind. Quickly they piled up heaps of wood shavings on top of the walls. As soon as Loki flew safely over, they set the shavings on fire.

The eagle was flying too fast to stop. He flew right into the flames, and his feathers caught fire, and down he fell inside the walls of Asgard. And that was the end of the giant Thiazi.

Then Idun changed back into herself. She gave each of the gods a golden apple. And as soon as they took a bite, their hair grew thick and golden, their wrinkles disappeared, their backs straightened, and they were young and strong again.
