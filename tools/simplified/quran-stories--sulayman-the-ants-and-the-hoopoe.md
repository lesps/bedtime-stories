namesNote: From the twenty-seventh chapter of the Qur'an, called The Ant. Sulayman is the Arabic name of Solomon, and Dawud of David. Muslims honour them both as prophets, and say “peace be upon him” after a prophet's name. Allah is the Arabic word for God. A hoopoe is a bird with a crest of feathers on its head. The jinn are beings that Allah made, which people usually cannot see.

Allah gave knowledge to Dawud and to his son Sulayman. And they said, "Praise be to Allah, who has favoured us above many of His servants who believe!"

Sulayman took Dawud's place. And he said, "O people! We have been taught the language of the birds, and we have been given plenty of everything. This is surely a clear gift from Allah."

One day Sulayman's armies were gathered before him: armies of jinn, and of people, and of birds, all marching in their ranks. When they came to the Valley of the Ants, an ant called out, "O ants! Go into your homes, so that Sulayman and his armies don't crush you without even noticing!"

Sulayman smiled, and laughed at what she said. And he prayed, "My Lord, help me to be thankful for the kindness You have shown to me and to my parents, and to do good things that will please You. And let me be one of Your good servants."

Then Sulayman looked among the birds. "Why can't I see the hoopoe?" he said. "Is he missing? I will punish him hard, unless he brings me a clear reason why he is away."

But the hoopoe was not long in coming. "I have found out something that you do not know," he said. "I have come to you from Sheba with true news. I found a woman ruling over the people there. She has been given plenty of everything, and she has a mighty throne. But I found her and her people bowing down to the sun instead of Allah. Satan has made the things they do look good to them, and turned them away from the right path. So they do not worship Allah, who brings out what is hidden in the heavens and the earth, and who knows what you hide and what you show. Allah! There is no god but Him, the Lord of the mighty Throne."

"We shall see whether you are telling the truth," said Sulayman, "or whether you are a liar. Take this letter of mine, and drop it down to them. Then move away, and see what answer they give."

So the letter came to the Queen of Sheba. "O chiefs!" she said. "A noble letter has been dropped down to me. It is from Sulayman, and it says: 'In the name of Allah, the Most Kind, the Most Merciful. Do not set yourselves up against me, but come to me, and give yourselves to Allah.'"

"O chiefs," she said, "tell me what I should do. I never decide anything unless you are here with me."

"We are strong, and we are mighty fighters," they said. "But it is for you to command. So think about what you will tell us to do."

"When kings march into a town, they ruin it," said the queen, "and they bring its proudest people down low. That is what they do. So I will send them a present instead, and see what answer my messengers bring back."

When her messenger came to Sulayman, Sulayman said, "What! Do you want to help me with riches? What Allah has given me is better than what He has given you. No, it is you who are so pleased with your gift. Go back to them! We will come to them with armies they cannot stand against, and we will drive them out of their land, brought low."

Then Sulayman said, "O chiefs! Which of you will bring me her throne, before they come to me and give themselves to Allah?"

A mighty one of the jinn said, "I will bring it to you before you can get up from your seat. I am strong enough for this, and you can trust me."

But one who had knowledge of the Scripture said, "I will bring it to you in the blink of an eye!" And when Sulayman saw the throne standing in front of him, he said, "This is from the goodness of my Lord, to test me, to see whether I will be thankful or ungrateful. Whoever is thankful, it is for his own good. And whoever is ungrateful, it only does himself harm. For my Lord needs nothing, and He is generous."

Then Sulayman said, "Change the look of her throne, and we will see whether she finds the right way, or not."

So when the queen came, she was asked, "Is your throne like this?"

"It is as if it were the very same one!" she said.

And Sulayman said, "We were given knowledge before her, and we had already given ourselves to Allah." For the things she used to worship instead of Allah had held her back, because she came from a people who did not believe.

Then she was told, "Come into the hall." When she saw the floor, she thought it was a pool of water, and she lifted the hem of her dress. But Sulayman said, "It is a hall, made smooth with glass."

And the queen said, "My Lord! I have done wrong to myself. Now, with Sulayman, I give myself to Allah, the Lord of all the worlds."
