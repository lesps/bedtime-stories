The Sheriff of Nottingham was very cross. He had tried and tried to catch Robin Hood, and everyone in Nottingham was laughing at him. So he rode all the way to London, with a fine troop of soldiers, to complain to King Henry.

"Your Majesty," he said, "in Sherwood Forest there lives a bold outlaw called Robin Hood. He shoots your deer, and he robs your people on the highways!"

"I have heard of him," said the King. "He is a saucy rascal, but a merry one. But what do you want me to do about it? You come here with all these soldiers, and you can't catch a band of outlaws in your own county? You are my Sheriff. Think of a plan yourself, and don't trouble me again. And if you can't make people obey my laws, you won't be my Sheriff for long!"

The Sheriff rode home very worried, thinking and thinking. Then suddenly he slapped his leg. "I have it!" he cried. "Robin Hood is so bold, he often comes right into Nottingham. If I hold a great archery contest, with a wonderful prize, he won't be able to stay away. And then I'll catch him!"

So he sent messengers all over the country to announce a grand shooting match. The prize was an arrow made of pure gold.

When Robin heard about it, he called his merry men together. "Lads," he said, "the Sheriff is holding a shooting match, and the prize is a golden arrow. I'd like one of us to win it. What do you say?"

Then young David of Doncaster spoke up. "Master, don't go! I heard from a friend, who heard it from one of the Sheriff's own men, that the whole contest is a trap to catch you."

"You are a wise lad," said Robin. "But shall we let people say that the Sheriff frightened Robin Hood away? No. We must be as clever as he is. Some of you dress up as friars, some as farmers, some as tinkers or beggars. And I will shoot for the golden arrow."

The day of the contest was bright and gay. Benches were set up beside the town wall for the lords and ladies, and the poor people sat on the grass. The best archers in all England had come. There was Gilbert of the Red Cap, the Sheriff's own archer, and old Adam of the Dell, who had beaten a famous archer long ago. And the Sheriff arrived on his white horse, dressed in purple velvet with a gold chain around his neck.

The Sheriff looked carefully at all the archers. But nobody was dressed in Lincoln green, like Robin's men. "I'll see him when only ten are left," he said to himself. "He's sure to be one of the best ten."

They all shot, and it was wonderful shooting. Then the best ten shot again. Six of them were famous archers. Two came from Yorkshire. One was a tall stranger in blue, who said he came from London. And the last was a ragged stranger in scarlet, with a brown beard and a patch over one eye.

"Do you see Robin Hood among them?" the Sheriff asked one of his soldiers.

"No, Your Worship," said the soldier. "One of the Yorkshiremen is too tall and the other too short. Robin's beard is yellow, and that ragged beggar's beard is brown, and he's blind in one eye. And Robin's shoulders are much broader than the man in blue's."

"Then he is a coward," said the Sheriff angrily, "and he didn't dare to come."

Now only three archers were left: Gilbert of the Red Cap, old Adam of the Dell, and the ragged stranger in scarlet. Nobody in the crowd cheered for the stranger. They laughed at his patched clothes and his one eye.

Gilbert shot, and his arrow landed a finger's width from the center. The ragged stranger shot so quickly that nobody had time to take a breath, and his arrow landed even closer. Then Adam shot, and his arrow landed beside the stranger's.

They shot again, and again the stranger's arrow was the best. Then they shot for the third and last time. Gilbert took careful aim, and his arrow landed right beside the very center. Everyone cheered so loudly that the flags shook.

"Well done, Gilbert!" cried the Sheriff. "Now, you ragged fellow, let's see you beat that!"

The stranger stood perfectly still while you could count to five. Then he drew his bow and let the arrow fly. It flew so straight that it knocked a feather off Gilbert's arrow, and landed right in the very center of the target.

Nobody said a word. Then old Adam shook his head and put his arrows away. "I've been shooting for forty years," he said, "but I'll shoot no more today. No one can beat that stranger."

The Sheriff came down and gave the stranger the golden arrow. "What is your name?" he asked.

"People call me Jock of Teviotdale," said the stranger.

"Jock, you are the best archer I ever saw, much better than that coward Robin Hood, who didn't dare show his face today. Come and work for me, and I'll give you fine clothes and good food and plenty of money."

"No," said the stranger roughly. "I'll be my own man. No one in England will be my master."

"Then go away!" shouted the Sheriff, shaking with anger.

That evening, deep in Sherwood Forest, a strange crowd gathered under the great greenwood tree: friars, tinkers, beggars and farmers. And in the middle sat a man in ragged scarlet, with a patch over one eye, holding a golden arrow. He took off the patch and pulled off the scarlet rags, and underneath he was dressed in Lincoln green. "These things come off easily," he laughed, "but the walnut juice I used to stain my yellow beard brown won't come out so fast!" It was Robin Hood himself!

How his merry men laughed! But after the feast, Robin took Little John aside. "I'm vexed," he said. "I heard the Sheriff call me a coward who didn't dare show his face. I want him to know who really won his golden arrow."

"Leave it to me and Will Stutely," said Little John.

That night the Sheriff sat at dinner in his great hall. "I really thought Robin Hood would come today," he said. "I didn't think he was such a coward. But who was that rude beggar? There was something about him..."

Just then, something fell clattering among the dishes. It was a blunt arrow, with a little roll of paper tied to it, and it had come flying in through the window. The Sheriff unrolled the paper and read it, and his face went red with rage. This is what it said:

"Now heaven bless Your Grace this day, say all in sweet Sherwood, for you gave the prize away to merry Robin Hood."
