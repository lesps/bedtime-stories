On a hill in an old country, there are two trees growing side by side: an oak and a linden. This is the story of how they got there.

One evening, Zeus, the king of the gods, and his son Hermes decided to visit the earth in disguise. They dressed up as two tired, dusty travelers and went from house to house in a village, knocking on doors.

"Please," they said, "may we come in and rest?"

At every house, the answer was no. Doors slammed in their faces. Some people did not even get up to answer.

At last they came to the very smallest house of all. It was a tiny cottage with a straw roof. In it lived an old woman named Baucis and her husband, Philemon. They had lived there together their whole lives. They were poor, but they were happy, because they had each other.

"Come in, come in!" said Philemon, opening the door wide.

Baucis set out a bench with a cushion for the guests. She blew on the embers of the fire to warm it up. Philemon went to the garden and picked a cabbage. Baucis took down a piece of bacon that had been hanging from the ceiling for a special occasion.

While the soup cooked, they chatted with their guests to make them feel at home. Baucis wiped the table with sweet-smelling herbs. One table leg was too short, so she slid a flat stone under it to make it steady. Then she set out olives, radishes, cheese and eggs, with an old clay jug of wine. For dessert there were apples, grapes and a honeycomb. Everything was simple, but it was served with kind faces and warm hearts.

As they ate, Baucis noticed something strange. Every time she poured wine from the jug, it filled itself right back up again.

Baucis and Philemon looked at each other in fright. These were no ordinary travelers. They fell to their knees. "Forgive us," they said, "for such a poor little supper."

They had one old goose, who guarded their cottage. They thought they should cook it for their important guests. But the goose was too quick for them. It flapped and honked around the room, and the old couple chased it until they were out of breath. At last the goose ran and hid between the two guests' feet.

"Don't hurt the goose," laughed the guests. "We are gods. Your neighbors have been cruel, and they will be punished. But you two will be safe. Come with us, up the hill."

Leaning on their walking sticks, Baucis and Philemon climbed the hill. When they reached the top, they turned around. The whole village had disappeared under a lake. Only their own little cottage was left standing.

As they watched, their cottage changed. Its wooden posts turned into marble columns. Its straw roof became gold. It was a beautiful temple.

"Good people," said Zeus, "what would you like? Ask for anything."

Baucis and Philemon whispered together for a moment. Then Philemon said, "Let us take care of this temple for the rest of our lives. And since we have always been together, let us leave the world at the same moment, so that neither of us has to be alone."

Their wish was granted. They looked after the temple for many years. Then one day, when they were very, very old, they were standing together on the steps. Baucis saw leaves sprouting from Philemon. Philemon saw leaves sprouting from Baucis. Bark began to grow around them.

"Goodbye, dear wife," said Philemon.

"Goodbye, dear husband," said Baucis.

And they turned into two trees, an oak and a linden, growing side by side, with their branches touching. They are still there today, on top of the hill.
