Soon after he became king, Arthur fought a hard battle against the tallest knight in all the land. Arthur fought bravely, but the other knight was too strong for him, and he would have lost his life if Merlin had not used his magic to put the knight into a deep sleep.

Then Merlin took Arthur to a wise hermit who knew all about healing, and in three days all Arthur's wounds were better. They thanked the hermit, and rode on together.

"I have no sword," said Arthur.

"Be patient," said Merlin. "I will find you one soon."

After a while they came to a big lake. And in the middle of the lake, Arthur saw an arm rising up out of the water, and in its hand it held a sword.

"Look," said Merlin. "That is the sword I told you about."

Then Arthur saw a lady walking across the water towards them.

"That is the Lady of the Lake," said Merlin. "If you ask her politely, she will give you the sword."

So when the lady came near, Arthur greeted her and said, "Lady, please tell me, whose is that sword that the arm is holding up out of the water? I wish it were mine, because I have lost my own sword."

"That sword is mine, King Arthur," said the Lady of the Lake. "And I will give it to you, if you will promise to give me a gift whenever I ask for one."

"I promise," said Arthur. "I will give you whatever you ask."

"Then get into that boat," said the lady, "and row out to the sword, and take it, and its sheath too. I will ask for my gift when the time comes."

The sword was called Excalibur.

So Arthur and Merlin tied up their horses and got into the boat, and rowed out to the middle of the lake. Arthur took hold of the sword by its handle, and the arm sank back down under the water. And they rowed back to the shore with the sword.

As they rode on, Arthur kept looking at his beautiful new sword. Merlin saw him and smiled.

"Which do you like best," he asked, "the sword or the sheath?"

"The sword, of course," said Arthur.

"Then you are not very wise," said Merlin. "The sheath is worth ten of the sword. As long as you wear it, you will never lose a drop of blood, however badly you are hurt."

Then they rode into the town of Carlion, and Arthur's knights gave them a joyful welcome. "It's good to serve a king who is ready to risk his life like any ordinary man," they said.
