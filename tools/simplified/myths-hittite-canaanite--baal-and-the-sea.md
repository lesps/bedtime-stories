---
title: Baal and the Sea
basedOn: the Ugaritic poem of Baal and Yam (clay tablets from Ugarit, about 1300 BC)
workId: canaanite-baal-yam
order: 4
---

Long ago, on the coast of the land of Canaan, people told stories about Baal, the god of storms and rain, who rode on the clouds.

At that time, El, the old father of the gods, loved his son Yam, the god of the sea, and he made Yam a king.

One day, Yam sent two messengers to the mountain where the gods were feasting. The gods saw the messengers coming, and they were afraid. They put their heads down on their knees, and hid their faces.

But Baal stood up. "Gods!" he said. "Why have you put your heads down? Lift up your heads! I will answer Yam's messengers."

The messengers came before El, and they did not bow. "This is what Yam, our master, says," they said. "Give up Baal, and all his helpers! Hand him over to Yam, so that he can be Yam's servant."

And El, the father of the gods, gave in. "Baal shall be your servant, Yam," he said.

Baal was furious. He grabbed his weapons to strike the messengers. But the goddesses Anat and Astarte caught hold of his arms and held him back, because it is wrong to strike a messenger.

"Then I will fight Yam myself," said Baal.

Now the craftsman of the gods, Kothar-wa-Khasis, the clever smith, came to Baal. "Listen, Baal, rider of the clouds," he said. "Now you will strike your enemy. Now you will win the kingdom that lasts for ever." And Kothar made two magic clubs for Baal, and gave them names.

The first club he named Chaser. "Chaser," said Kothar, "chase Yam from his throne! Fly from Baal's hand like an eagle, and strike Yam on the shoulders!"

So the club flew from Baal's hand like an eagle, and it struck Yam on the shoulders. But Yam was strong, and he did not fall.

Then Kothar made the second club, and he named it Driver. "Driver," he said, "drive Yam from his throne! Fly from Baal's hand like an eagle, and strike Yam on the head, between the eyes!"

So the second club flew from Baal's hand like an eagle, and it struck Yam on the head, between the eyes. And Yam staggered, and his knees gave way, and he fell to the ground.

Then the goddess Astarte called out, "Baal, rider of the clouds! Yam is beaten! Baal shall be king!"

And so Baal, the god of storm and rain, the rider of the clouds, became king.
