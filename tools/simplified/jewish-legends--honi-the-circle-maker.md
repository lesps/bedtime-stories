---
title: Honi the Circle-Maker
basedOn: the Babylonian Talmud, tractate Ta'anit 23a (written down about AD 500)

Long ago, in the land of Israel, there was a year when no rain fell. Spring came, and still there was no rain. The fields were dry, the wells were low, and the people were afraid that nothing would grow.

So they went to a wise and holy man called Honi. "Pray for us," they said, "so that the rain will come."

Honi prayed. But no rain came.

Then Honi took his stick, and he drew a circle on the ground, and he stood inside it. And he spoke to God. "Master of the world," he said, "Your children have turned to me, because they think I am like one of Your own household. I promise by Your great Name that I will not move from this circle until You have mercy on Your children."

And it began to rain, just a little. A few drops fell, here and there.

"That is not what I asked for," said Honi. "I asked for rain that will fill the wells and the ditches and the caves."

Then the rain came down in a storm, with drops so big that each one could have filled a bucket. People were afraid that the whole world would be washed away.

"That is not what I asked for either," said Honi. "I asked for rain of goodness, and blessing, and kindness."

Then the rain fell just as it should, gently and steadily. And it rained and rained, until the people had to climb up to the Temple Mount, the high hill in Jerusalem, to get away from the water.

"Honi," they said, "now you must pray for the rain to go away!"

"I have been taught that we must not pray for too much good to go away," said Honi. "But bring me an offering of thanks." So they brought him an offering of thanks, and Honi prayed. And a wind blew, and the clouds scattered, and the sun came out. And the people went out into the fields, and gathered the mushrooms that had sprung up after the rain.

Then the great teacher Simeon ben Shetah sent a message to Honi. "You should not have talked to God like that, as if you could make Him do what you wanted. If you were anyone else, I would scold you. But what can I do with you? You are like a child who keeps asking his father for things, and his father gives him what he asks for."

And Honi is remembered to this day as Honi the Circle-Maker.
