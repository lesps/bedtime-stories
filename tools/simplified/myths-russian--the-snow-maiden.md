---
title: The Snow Maiden
basedOn: the Russian folk tale of Snegurochka, the snow girl, in the version Alexander Afanasyev collected (1855–1863), which Magnus's translation leaves out
workId: russian-snow-maiden
order: 7
---

Once upon a time there lived an old man called Ivan and an old woman called Marya. They had lived together happily for many years, but they had no children, and that made them sad.

One winter's day, when deep snow had fallen, the children of the village ran out to play. They threw snowballs, and slid on their sledges, and built a snowman. Ivan and Marya sat by the window and watched them.

"Wife," said Ivan, "let's go out and make ourselves a little girl out of snow."

So they went out into the garden and began. They made her body, and her arms and legs, and on top they put a snowy head. Ivan shaped her little nose and chin, and made two little holes for her eyes. And as he finished, the little girl began to breathe. Her lips turned pink, and her eyes opened, and she smiled at them. Then she shook the snow from her arms and legs, and stepped out of the snowdrift, a real living girl.

Ivan and Marya were overjoyed. They took her into their house and called her Snegurochka, the Snow Maiden.

The Snow Maiden grew, not by the day, but by the hour. Soon she was a tall girl. She had skin as white as snow, eyes as blue as forget-me-nots, and long fair hair down to her waist. She was clever and kind and gentle, and all the village children loved her. And Ivan and Marya loved her more than anything in the world.

All winter the Snow Maiden was happy. But when spring came, and the sun grew warm, and the snow began to melt, she grew sad. She stayed in the shadows, and hid from the sun.

"Are you ill, my darling?" asked Marya.

"No, Grandmother, I'm well," said the Snow Maiden. But she grew quieter and sadder every day.

Then summer came, and the flowers bloomed, and the village girls came to call for her. "Come with us to the woods, Snow Maiden," they said. "We'll pick flowers and berries, and sing and dance."

The Snow Maiden did not want to go. But Marya said, "Go along, my darling, and enjoy yourself with your friends." So the Snow Maiden went with them.

In the woods, the girls picked flowers and made garlands and sang songs. And when evening came, they gathered sticks and built a bonfire. Then they began to jump over the fire, one after another, laughing.

"Come on, Snow Maiden," they cried. "It's your turn!"

So the Snow Maiden ran, and jumped over the fire. But as she jumped, she melted away into a little white cloud. The cloud floated up into the sky, higher and higher, and was gone.

The girls turned round. "Snow Maiden! Where are you?" they called. "Aa-oo! Aa-oo!" But only the echo answered them from the woods.

The Snow Maiden had gone.
