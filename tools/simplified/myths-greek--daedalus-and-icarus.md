Daedalus was the cleverest inventor in the world. He built the great maze called the Labyrinth for King Minos of Crete, a maze so twisty that anyone who went in could never find the way out.

But later, Daedalus made King Minos angry. The king locked Daedalus and his young son, Icarus, in a high tower.

Daedalus found a way out of the tower. But they were still stuck on the island. King Minos watched every ship that came and went, so they could not escape by sea.

"Minos rules the land and the sea," thought Daedalus. "But he does not rule the sky. We will escape through the air."

So he began to collect feathers. He collected little feathers and big feathers. He laid them in rows, from the smallest to the largest, the way feathers grow on a bird's wing. He tied the big feathers together with thread and stuck the small ones on with wax. Then he bent them gently into a curve, like real wings.

Icarus played nearby while his father worked. He chased the feathers when the wind blew them away. He pressed his thumb into the soft yellow wax. He did not know that he was playing with the thing that would decide his fate.

At last the wings were ready. Daedalus put a pair on his own arms, flapped them, and rose into the air. Then he came down and made a second pair, just for Icarus.

As he tied them onto his son's arms, Daedalus said, "Listen carefully, Icarus. Fly in the middle. Don't fly too low, or the spray from the sea will make your feathers heavy. And don't fly too high, or the heat of the sun will melt the wax. Stay close to me, and you will be safe."

He kissed his son. His hands were shaking a little. Then they flapped their wings and rose up into the sky.

It was wonderful. Below them, farmers looked up from their plowing and fishermen looked up from their boats. They thought the two flying figures must be gods.

They flew past island after island. Icarus began to feel bold. Flying was the most exciting thing he had ever done. He forgot his father's warning. He flew higher and higher, up toward the shining sun.

The sun grew hot. The wax on his wings began to soften. Then it melted, and the feathers began to drop away, one by one. Icarus flapped his arms, but there were no feathers left to hold the air. He fell down, down, down into the blue sea below, calling for his father.

"Icarus!" cried Daedalus. "Icarus, where are you?"

Then he saw the feathers floating on the water.

Daedalus wished he had never made the wings. He laid his son to rest on a nearby island, and he named the island Icaria. The sea where Icarus fell is still called the Icarian Sea.

People still tell this story to remember two things: how wonderful it is to fly, and how important it is to listen when someone who loves you tells you to be careful.
