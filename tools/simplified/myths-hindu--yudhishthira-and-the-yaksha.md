title: Yudhishthira and the Yaksha
adaptedFrom: ganguli-mahabharata--yudhishthira-and-the-yaksha

namesNote: From the third book of the Mahabharata. A yaksha is a powerful nature spirit. Nakula and Sahadeva are the sons of King Pandu's second wife, Madri; Yudhishthira, Bhima and Arjuna are the sons of his first wife, Kunti. Dharma, the lord of justice, is Yudhishthira's father.
---

Long ago, the five Pandava brothers were living in exile in the forest. One day they chased a deer that had run off with a holy man's fire-sticks, but it got away from them. They were tired and very thirsty, so they sat down to rest under a tree.

"Nakula," said Yudhishthira, the eldest brother, "climb this tree and look around. Can you see any water?"

Nakula climbed the tree. "I can see trees that grow beside water," he said, "and I can hear cranes calling. There must be water over there."

"Go and fetch some," said Yudhishthira.

So Nakula went, and soon he came to a clear lake, full of cranes. He was just about to drink when a voice spoke out of the sky. "Don't do it, child! This lake is mine. Answer my questions first, and then you can drink."

But Nakula was too thirsty to listen. He drank the cool water, and fell down dead.

When Nakula did not come back, Yudhishthira sent his twin brother Sahadeva to find him. Sahadeva found his brother lying dead by the lake. He heard the same voice, but he was so thirsty that he drank the water too, and fell down dead.

Then Yudhishthira sent Arjuna. When Arjuna heard the voice, he shot arrows all over the sky, to hit whoever was speaking. "Why go to all that trouble?" said the voice. "Answer my questions, and then drink." But Arjuna drank, and fell down dead.

Then Yudhishthira sent Bhima, the strongest of them all. And Bhima did the same, and fell down dead too.

At last Yudhishthira went to look for his brothers himself. He came to the beautiful lake, and there he found them, all four, lying on the ground. He wept, and wondered what had happened. There was not a mark on them, and no footprint anywhere. "Who could have done this?" he thought.

As he stepped into the water, he heard the voice. "I am a crane, and I eat little fish. It was I who killed your brothers. If you don't answer my questions, you will be the fifth to die. Don't drink, child! This lake is mine. Answer my questions first."

"No bird could have done this," said Yudhishthira. "Who are you?"

"I am not a bird," said the voice. "I am a yaksha." And Yudhishthira saw him: a huge yaksha, as tall as a palm tree, blazing like fire, sitting in a tree.

"I won't take what belongs to you," said Yudhishthira. "Ask me your questions, and I will answer them as well as I can."

So the yaksha asked, and Yudhishthira answered.

"What makes the sun rise?"

"Brahma makes the sun rise."

"What is heavier than the earth? What is higher than the sky? What is faster than the wind? And what are there more of than blades of grass?"

"A mother is heavier than the earth. A father is higher than the sky. The mind is faster than the wind. And our thoughts are more than the blades of grass."

"What does not close its eyes when it sleeps? What does not move after it is born? What has no heart? And what grows bigger all by itself?"

"A fish does not close its eyes when it sleeps. An egg does not move after it is born. A stone has no heart. And a river grows bigger all by itself."

"Who is the friend of someone far from home? Who is the friend of someone who is ill?"

"The friend of someone far from home is the person travelling with him. The friend of someone who is ill is the doctor."

"What is the best thing to gain? What is the best kind of happiness?"

"The best thing to gain is good health. The best kind of happiness is being content with what you have."

"What, if you give it up, makes people like you? And what, if you give it up, makes you happy?"

"Pride, if you give it up, makes people like you. And greed, if you give it up, makes you happy."

"What enemy can never be beaten?"

"Anger is an enemy that can never be beaten."

The yaksha asked many more questions, and Yudhishthira answered them all. At last the yaksha asked, "What is the most wonderful thing in the world?"

"Every day, creatures go to the land of the dead," said Yudhishthira, "and yet the people who are left behind think that they will live for ever. What could be more wonderful than that?"

"You have answered all my questions well," said the yaksha. "So I will let one of your brothers live. Choose which one."

Yudhishthira looked at his brothers. "Let Nakula live," he said.

"Why Nakula?" asked the yaksha. "Bhima is as strong as ten thousand elephants, and you all depend on Arjuna. Why choose Nakula, who is only your half-brother?"

"My father had two wives, Kunti and Madri," said Yudhishthira. "I am Kunti's son, and I am alive. Let Madri have a son alive too. I love both my mothers the same, and I want to be fair to them both. Never to hurt others is the highest goodness, and I will always try to do what is right."

"Because you think that never hurting others matters more than gain or pleasure," said the yaksha, "all your brothers shall live!"

And at once the four brothers stood up, and their hunger and thirst were gone.

"Who are you really?" asked Yudhishthira. "You can't be just a yaksha. Are you a friend? Are you my father?"

"I am your father," said the yaksha. "I am the lord of justice. I came to test you, and I am very pleased with you. It was I who took the holy man's fire-sticks, in the shape of a deer. Now ask me for gifts."

"Then first," said Yudhishthira, "let the holy man have his fire-sticks back, so that he can keep his fire burning."

"So be it," said the lord of justice. "Ask again."

"We have lived in the forest for twelve years," said Yudhishthira, "and now the thirteenth year has come, when we must live in hiding. Let no one recognise us this year."

"So be it," said the lord of justice. "Ask again."

"May I always be able to conquer greed and foolishness and anger," said Yudhishthira, "and may my heart always be set on kindness and truth."

"You are like that already," said his father. "But so be it." And he vanished.

Then the five brothers went back to their forest home, and gave the holy man back his fire-sticks.
