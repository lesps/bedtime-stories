Deep under the earth, among the rocks, lived a dwarf called Alviss. His name means All-Wise, because he knew so much. And somehow, while Thor was away from home, Alviss had been promised that he could marry Thor's daughter.

One evening, Alviss came to Thor's hall to take his bride home. "Get the benches ready for the wedding!" he said happily. "Now my bride will come home with me!"

But Thor was home now. He looked at the dwarf. "Who are you?" he asked. "Why is your nose so pale? You look as if you've been sleeping with dead things all night. You're not the right sort to marry my daughter."

"My name is Alviss," said the dwarf. "I live under the earth, beneath the rocks. I have come for my bride. A promise is a promise."

"I'll break it, then," said Thor. "I'm her father. I wasn't home when that promise was made, and I say no."

"Who are you, to say no?" asked the dwarf.

"I am Thor," said Thor. "And you will not marry my daughter without my say-so."

"Then I want your say-so," said Alviss.

Thor thought for a moment. Then he had an idea.

"Very well," said Thor. "You may have her, clever guest, if you can answer all my questions. You say you are wise. Tell me everything I want to know about all the worlds."

"Ask away," said Alviss proudly. "I have traveled through all nine worlds, and I know everything."

"Tell me then," said Thor, "what is the earth called, in each of the worlds?"

"People call it earth," said Alviss. "The gods call it the field. The Vanir call it the way. The giants call it the evergreen. The elves call it the growing one. And the high gods call it clay."

"And what is the sky called, in each of the worlds?"

"People call it sky," said Alviss. "The gods call it the roof of light. The Vanir call it the wind-weaver. The giants call it the world above. The elves call it the fair roof. And the dwarfs call it the dripping hall."

"And the moon?"

"People call it the moon. The gods call it the bright one. In the land of the dead they call it the whirling wheel. The giants call it the hurrier. The dwarfs call it the shiner. And the elves call it the counter of years."

On and on Thor went. He asked about the sun and the clouds, the wind and the calm, the sea and the fire, the forest and the night, the grain in the fields and the ale in the cups. And the clever dwarf had an answer for every single one. He knew the sun's names, and the sea's names, and the night's names in every world. He talked and talked and talked, all night long. He was enjoying himself very much.

At last Thor said, "I have never met anyone who knew so many old things. But I have tricked you, little dwarf. You are above the ground at dawn! Look, the sun is shining into the hall!"

Alviss had been so busy showing off how clever he was that he had forgotten all about the time. And dwarfs who live under the earth must never be caught by the sun. As the first sunbeam came through the door and touched him, the old stories say, the clever dwarf turned into stone.

And that is how Thor kept his daughter at home, not by fighting, but by being even cleverer than the All-Wise.
