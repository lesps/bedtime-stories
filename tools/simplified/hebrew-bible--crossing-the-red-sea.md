When the king of Egypt was told that the children of Israel had gone, he and his servants changed their minds. "What have we done?" they said. "We have let Israel go, and now they will not work for us any more!"

So Pharaoh got his chariots ready, and took his army with him: six hundred of his best chariots, and all the other chariots of Egypt, with captains over them all. And the Lord made Pharaoh's heart hard, and he chased after the children of Israel. The Egyptians came after them with all Pharaoh's horses and chariots and horsemen and soldiers, and caught up with them where they were camped beside the sea.

When the children of Israel looked up and saw the Egyptians marching after them, they were terrified, and they cried out to the Lord. They said to Moses, "Weren't there enough graves in Egypt, that you brought us out here to die in the wilderness? Why did you bring us out of Egypt? We told you to leave us alone! It would have been better to work for the Egyptians than to die out here."

"Don't be afraid," Moses said to the people. "Stand still, and see how the Lord will save you today. The Egyptians you see today, you will never see again. The Lord will fight for you. All you have to do is keep still."

Then the Lord said to Moses, "Tell the children of Israel to go forward. And you, lift up your staff, and stretch out your hand over the sea, and divide it, so that the children of Israel can walk through the middle of the sea on dry ground."

Then the angel of God, who had been going in front of the camp of Israel, moved and went behind them. And the pillar of cloud moved from in front of them and stood behind them, between the camp of Egypt and the camp of Israel. On one side it was cloud and darkness, but on the other side it gave light all through the night. So neither camp came near the other all night long.

Moses stretched out his hand over the sea, and all that night the Lord drove the sea back with a strong east wind, and made the sea into dry land. The waters were divided, and the children of Israel walked into the middle of the sea on dry ground, with the waters standing like a wall on their right and on their left.

The Egyptians chased after them, all Pharaoh's horses and chariots and horsemen, right into the middle of the sea. But just before morning, the Lord looked down on the Egyptian army from the pillar of fire and cloud, and threw them into confusion. He made the wheels come off their chariots, so that they could hardly drive. "Let's get away from Israel!" the Egyptians cried. "The Lord is fighting for them against Egypt!"

Then the Lord said to Moses, "Stretch out your hand over the sea, so that the waters come back over the Egyptians and their chariots and their horsemen."

Moses stretched out his hand over the sea, and as morning came, the sea went back to its place. The waters came back and covered the chariots and the horsemen, all of Pharaoh's army that had followed them into the sea. Not one of them was left.

But the children of Israel had walked on dry ground through the middle of the sea, with the waters like a wall on their right and on their left. So that day the Lord saved Israel from the Egyptians. And when the people saw the great thing the Lord had done, they feared the Lord, and they believed in the Lord, and in His servant Moses.
