Thor's wife, Sif, had the most beautiful long golden hair. But one night, just for mischief, Loki crept in while she was asleep and cut it all off. Snip, snip, snip! In the morning, poor Sif had no hair at all.

When Thor saw what had happened, he was furious. He grabbed Loki and was about to break every bone in his body.

"Wait! Wait!" squealed Loki. "I'll make it right! I'll get the dwarfs to make her new hair, out of real gold, that will grow just like her own!"

So Loki went down to the dwarfs who live under the ground. He went to the sons of Ivaldi, who were wonderful smiths, and they made the golden hair. And while they were at it, they made two more treasures: a ship called Skidbladnir, and a spear called Gungnir.

Loki should have gone home then. But he could never leave well enough alone. He went to another dwarf, named Brokk.

"I bet my head," said Loki, "that your brother Sindri can't make three treasures as good as these."

"We'll see about that," said Brokk.

So they went to Sindri's forge. Sindri put a pigskin into the fire. "Work the bellows," he told Brokk, "and don't stop blowing, not for a moment, until I take it out." And he went out.

As soon as he was gone, a fly landed on Brokk's hand and bit him. (Many people think that fly was really Loki in disguise, trying to spoil the work.) But Brokk kept on blowing. When Sindri came back and pulled the thing out of the fire, it was a boar with bristles of shining gold.

Next Sindri put gold into the fire. "Keep blowing," he said, "and don't stop till I come back." This time the fly landed on Brokk's neck and bit twice as hard. But Brokk kept on blowing. And out of the fire came a golden ring.

Last of all, Sindri put iron into the fire. "This time," he said, "if you stop blowing even for a moment, everything will be ruined."

The fly landed right between Brokk's eyes and bit his eyelids, so hard that he could not see. For just one moment Brokk let go of the bellows to swat the fly away. Then he grabbed them again. Sindri came back and said, "That was close! It was nearly spoiled." And he pulled out of the fire a great iron hammer.

Then Brokk and Loki took all the treasures up to Asgard, and the gods sat down to judge them. Odin, Thor and Frey would decide who had won.

First Loki gave out his treasures. To Odin he gave the spear Gungnir, which would never miss what it was thrown at. To Thor he gave the golden hair for Sif, and as soon as Sif put it on, it grew onto her head and was her own hair, just as beautiful as before. To Frey he gave the ship Skidbladnir. It always had a good wind, wherever it was going, and yet it could be folded up like a handkerchief and carried in a pocket!

Then Brokk gave out his treasures. To Odin he gave the golden ring, called Draupnir. Every ninth night, eight more rings just as heavy would drip from it. To Frey he gave the golden boar, which could run through the air and over the sea faster than any horse, and its bristles glowed so brightly that it was never dark wherever it went.

And to Thor he gave the hammer. Its name was Mjollnir. "You can hit anything with it, as hard as you like," said Brokk, "and it will never break. If you throw it, it will never miss, and it will always fly back to your hand. And if you want, it will shrink so small that you can carry it inside your shirt." There was only one little fault: its handle was a bit short, because Brokk had stopped blowing for that one moment.

The gods decided that the hammer was the best treasure of all, because it would protect them from the giants. So Brokk had won the bet, and Loki's head belonged to him!

"Catch me first!" said Loki, and he ran away in his magic shoes, which could carry him through the air and over the sea. But Thor caught him and brought him back.

Brokk was about to take his prize. But clever Loki said, "You can have my head, but not my neck! You won the head, but you didn't win the neck, and you mustn't touch my neck!"

Brokk could not argue with that. So instead, he took a sharp awl and a leather thong and sewed Loki's lips together, so that Loki could not talk his way out of trouble for a while. Loki soon pulled the stitches out again. But that was the day Thor got his hammer, and from then on, Thor and his hammer kept the gods safe from the giants.
