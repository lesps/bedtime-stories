---
title: Ara the Beautiful and Queen Shamiram
basedOn: Movses Khorenatsi's History of the Armenians (traditionally dated to the 400s AD), book 1, chapter 15
workId: armenian-ara
order: 2
namesNote: Shamiram is the Armenian name of Semiramis, a legendary queen of Assyria. The Aralez were spirits like dogs, who were said to heal fallen heroes by licking their wounds.
---

Long ago, Armenia was ruled by a king called Ara, and he was so handsome that people called him Ara the Beautiful.

Far to the south, in Assyria, there ruled a queen called Shamiram. For many years she had heard about Ara's beauty, and she longed to marry him. But while her husband, King Ninos, was alive, she did nothing. When Ninos died, she sent messengers to Ara with gifts. She begged him to come to her city of Nineveh, and marry her, and rule with her over all her lands. Or if he would not marry her, she begged him at least to come and visit, and go home again with rich presents.

But Ara would not go. Again and again she sent messengers, and again and again he sent them away. At last Shamiram grew angry. She gathered her army and marched into Armenia. "Take Ara alive," she told her generals. "Don't let him be killed!" But in the battle, Ara the Beautiful fell, and he was killed. The place where he fell was called the plain of Ara.

Shamiram found Ara's body among the fallen, and had it carried to a high room in her palace. "I will tell the Aralez to lick his wounds," she said, "and he will live again." For the Aralez were spirits like dogs, who could bring fallen heroes back to life by licking their wounds.

But Ara did not wake up. So Shamiram had him buried in secret. Then she dressed one of her men in Ara's clothes, and sent word through the land: "The Aralez have licked Ara's wounds, and brought him back to life!" And the Armenians believed it, and stopped fighting.

Shamiram made Ara's son ruler of Armenia, though he was only twelve years old, and she named him Ara, after his father. She stayed in Armenia for a while, and by the shore of Lake Van she built a beautiful city, with gardens and canals and a palace on the rock. It is still called Shamiramakert, the city of Shamiram.
