---
title: Dhruva, the Pole Star
adaptedFrom: dutt-vishnu-purana--dhruva-the-pole-star
order: 6
namesNote: From the Vishnu Purana. Dhruva means "fixed" or "steady", and in Sanskrit the Pole Star, which never moves in the sky, is called Dhruva.
---

Long ago there was a king called Uttanapada, and he had two wives. His favourite wife, Suruchi, had a son called Uttama, whom the king loved very much. His other wife, Suniti, whom he loved less, had a son called Dhruva.

One day Dhruva saw his brother Uttama sitting on their father's knee on the throne, and he wanted to climb up there too. But because Suruchi was there, the king did not lift him up. "Child," said Suruchi, "why do you want what you cannot have? You were not born from me. You may be the king's son, but this throne is for my son only. Don't you know that you are only Suniti's child?"

Dhruva left his father and ran to his mother, his lips trembling with anger. Suniti took him on her lap. "Who has made you angry, my child?" she said. And Dhruva told her everything Suruchi had said.

Suniti sighed, and her eyes filled with tears. "Suruchi is right," she said. "Your luck is not good, my child. But don't be sad. Be good and kind, and friendly to every living creature, and good fortune will come to you, as water flows down to the low ground."

"Mother," said Dhruva, "your words cannot comfort me. But I will try so hard that I win the highest place in the whole world. I do not want anything that someone else gives me. I will win a place by myself that even my father never had."

So Dhruva left his mother's house and the city, and went into a forest, and there he found seven great sages sitting on deerskins. He bowed to them. "I am Dhruva, the son of King Uttanapada and Suniti," he said. "I am not happy with the world, and I have come to you."

"You are only a boy of four or five," said the sages. "Why are you unhappy?" And Dhruva told them what his stepmother had said.

"How fiery he is," said the sages to each other, "even though he is only a child! Tell us, prince, what do you want to do?"

"I don't want riches, or a kingdom," said Dhruva. "I want to reach a place that no one has ever reached before. Tell me how."

One after another, the sages told him: "Worship Vishnu. If Vishnu is pleased with you, nothing is impossible." And they taught him a prayer to say: "Om, I bow to Vasudeva."

So Dhruva went to the holy forest of Madhuvana, on the banks of the river Yamuna, and there he began to pray to Vishnu. He thought of nothing else, until Vishnu filled his whole heart. He prayed so hard that the earth could hardly hold him. When he stood on his left foot, half the earth bent down, and when he stood on his right foot, the other half sank. When he stood on his toes, the whole earth shook, with all its mountains and rivers.

The gods were alarmed, and they tried to stop him. One of them took the shape of his mother, Suniti, and stood before him, weeping. "My son," she said, "stop this! You are only five years old. This is the time for you to play. If you don't stop, I will die in front of you!" But Dhruva was thinking only of Vishnu, and he did not even see her. Then demons came rushing at him, with terrible weapons and flaming faces, and jackals howled around him, and goblins with the faces of lions and camels and crocodiles shrieked at him. But Dhruva saw and heard nothing but Vishnu in his heart.

Then the frightened gods went to Vishnu. "Stop this boy," they begged. "We don't know whose throne he wants."

"He does not want anyone's throne," said Vishnu. "Go home, and don't worry. I will give him what he wants."

Then Vishnu, in his form with four arms, came to Dhruva. "Bless you, son of Uttanapada," he said. "I am pleased with you. Ask me for a gift."

Dhruva opened his eyes, and saw Vishnu standing before him, and he bowed down and praised him. Then he said, "Lord, you know everything in my heart. My stepmother said to me, 'This throne is not for you.' So I ask you for a place higher than any other, which holds up the whole world, and which will last for ever."

"You shall have it," said Vishnu. "You will have a place above the three worlds, higher than the sun and the moon and all the planets and stars, and higher even than the seven sages. All the stars will turn around you. And your mother, Suniti, will shine near you as a bright star."

So Dhruva rose up into the sky. And there he still shines, the Pole Star, which never moves, while all the other stars turn around him.
