namesNote: Budge spells Anubis as Anpu and the monster's name as Amemit (often Ammit); the Hall of Maati is the Hall of Truth. Ani was a real scribe, whose Book of the Dead is in the British Museum.

The people of ancient Egypt believed that when someone died, their soul went on a long journey to the kingdom of Osiris, the king of the dead. But before they could enter it, they had to pass a test. Their heart was weighed.

This is the story of how a scribe called Ani was tested, as it was painted on his Book of the Dead.

Ani came into a great hall, called the Hall of Truth. At the far end, on a throne, sat Osiris, the king of the dead, and behind him stood the goddesses Isis and Nephthys. Along one side of the hall sat a row of great gods, who were the judges.

In the middle of the hall stood a great pair of scales. On top of the scales sat a baboon, the friend of Thoth. Anubis stood beside the scales, to watch the pointer. Thoth, the scribe of the gods, stood ready with his pen, to write down what happened.

And behind them crouched a monster called Ammit, the Eater of the Dead. She had the head of a crocodile, the front legs of a lion, and the back legs of a hippopotamus. If a heart failed the test, Ammit would gobble it up.

Ani's heart was put in one pan of the scales. In the other pan was a feather, the feather of Truth.

While his heart was being weighed, Ani spoke to it. "O my heart," he said, "my heart that I had from my mother! Don't stand up against me. Don't tell any lies about me in front of the great god."

The scales swung, and then they were still. The heart and the feather balanced exactly.

Then Thoth spoke to the gods. "Listen! The heart of Ani has been weighed, and it is true. No wickedness has been found in him. He did not steal from the temples. He did not cheat. He did not say unkind things about people when he was on earth."

And all the gods answered, "What Thoth says is true. Ani has done no wrong. The Eater of the Dead shall not have him. Let him come before Osiris, and let him have food and a home in the Fields of Offerings for ever."

Then Horus, the son of Isis, took Ani by the hand and led him to his father Osiris. "I have brought Ani to you," said Horus. "His heart is good. It has come safely through the weighing. Let him have bread and beer, and let him live for ever with your followers."

And Ani knelt before Osiris. "Here I am, Lord," he said. "There is no wrong in me. I have not told a lie on purpose. Let me be one of your followers."

And Osiris welcomed him. And so Ani passed into the kingdom of Osiris, to live there for ever.
