title: Krishna and the Serpent Kaliya

namesNote: From the Vishnu Purana. Vraja is the land of the cowherds where Krishna grew up, and the Yamuna is its river. Balarama is Krishna's elder brother; Garuda, the king of the birds, is the enemy of all serpents.
---

When Krishna was a boy, growing up among the cowherds of Vraja, he went one day into the forest with the other cowherd boys, without his brother Balarama. He wore a garland of wild flowers, and he wandered down to the banks of the river Yamuna, where the waves sparkled with foam.

There Krishna came to a pool where the terrible serpent Kaliya lived. The pool was boiling with his poison. The great trees on its banks had withered, and when the wind blew spray from the water onto the birds, they were scorched.

"So the wicked serpent Kaliya lives here," Krishna thought. "I beat him once, and drove him out of the ocean, and now he has poisoned the river Yamuna, so that the thirsty cows and cowherds cannot drink. I came down to the earth to punish the wicked. I will climb this kadamba tree, and jump into the pool."

So Krishna tied his clothes tightly around him, and climbed the tree, and jumped boldly into the serpent's pool. The whole pool heaved, and the poisoned spray splashed onto the trees far away, and set them on fire. Krishna slapped his arms loudly, to dare the serpent to come out.

Up came Kaliya, the king of the serpents. His eyes were the colour of copper, and his hoods were flaming with poison. All around him were other poisonous snakes, and hundreds of serpent women wearing glittering earrings. They coiled themselves around Krishna, and bit him with their fiery fangs.

When the other boys saw Krishna in the pool with the snakes all around him, they ran back to the village crying. "Krishna has jumped into Kaliya's pool, and the serpent is eating him up! Come and see!"

The cowherds and their wives ran to the river, with Krishna's mother, Yasoda, stumbling along in front, and with Balarama and Krishna's father, Nanda. There they saw Krishna in the water, wrapped in the coils of the snakes, not moving at all. Nanda and Yasoda could only stare. And the women wept. "We will go into the pool with Yasoda," they said. "What is the day without the sun, or the night without the moon? What is our village without Krishna? We will not go home without him." And then they cried, "Look! He is still smiling at us, even with the snakes all around him!"

Then Balarama saw Nanda staring, and Yasoda almost fainting, and he called out to his brother. "Krishna, you are the lord of all the gods! Why are you acting like an ordinary boy? You have played at being a child long enough. Now beat this dreadful snake!"

When Balarama reminded him who he really was, Krishna smiled, and shook himself free of the snakes. He grabbed Kaliya's middle head with both hands, and bent it down, and stood on it. And he danced on the serpent's head. Every time Kaliya tried to lift a head, Krishna stamped it down again with his dancing feet, until Kaliya grew weaker and weaker.

Then the serpent's wives begged Krishna for mercy. "Now we know who you are," they said. "You are the lord of all things. Even the gods cannot praise you enough. Please, have pity on this poor creature. You are so great, and he is so weak. Give us back our husband!"

And Kaliya himself, very tired, begged Krishna to forgive him. "Snakes are crooked by nature," he said. "You made me a snake, so I behaved like one. Now you have taken away my strength and my poison. Spare my life. Tell me what to do."

"You must not stay here any longer," said Krishna. "Go to the sea, with all your family. Garuda will not hurt you when he sees the marks of my feet on your head."

So Krishna let Kaliya go. The serpent bowed to him, and went away to the ocean with all his wives and children and servants. The cowherds hugged Krishna as if he had come back from the dead, and they wept for joy. And when they saw that the water of the river was clean again, they sang Krishna's praises, and he went home with them to the village.
