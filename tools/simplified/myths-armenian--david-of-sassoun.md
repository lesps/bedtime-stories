---
title: David of Sassoun
basedOn: the Armenian folk epic Daredevils of Sassoun, first written down from the storyteller Krpo by Garegin Srvandztiants in 1873
workId: armenian-david-sassoun
order: 4
namesNote: Sassoun is a mountain land in old Armenia, and Msr is Egypt; its Melik is its king. The epic was sung by village storytellers, and there are many versions of it.
---

Long ago, in the mountains of Sassoun, there lived a mighty hero called Mher. When Mher died, his son David was only a little boy, and he was taken far away to the land of Msr, to be brought up in the house of its king, the Melik of Msr. But the Melik was afraid of how strong the boy was growing, and at last he sent David home to Sassoun, to his uncle Ohan.

David was so strong that nobody knew what to do with him. So Uncle Ohan made him the village calf-herd. Every morning David drove the calves up into the mountains to graze, and every evening he drove them home again. But one evening he came down from the mountains driving not only the calves, but every wild creature he had met on the way: wolves and foxes, bears and hares, deer and wild goats, all in one great herd. "Open your doors!" he shouted. "Here come your calves!" The villagers bolted their doors in fright, and the wild animals ran all over the village until David chased them back to the mountains.

One day David found out that his father's things were still kept in Sassoun: Mher's great horse, Kurkik Jalali; the Lightning Sword, which could cut through anything; and the Cross of Battle, which Mher had worn on his right arm to keep him safe. So David put the Cross of Battle on his arm, and buckled on the Lightning Sword, and leaped onto Kurkik Jalali's back.

Now every year the Melik of Msr sent his men to Sassoun to collect taxes, and every year the people of Sassoun had to pay him gold and grain and cattle, and they were poor because of it. When the tax collectors came again, David met them on the road. "Go back and tell your Melik," he said, "that Sassoun pays nothing to anyone." And he chased them all the way out of the land.

When the Melik heard, he was furious. He gathered a huge army and marched on Sassoun. The people were terrified, but David saddled Kurkik Jalali and rode out to meet the army all alone. "Bread and wine, and the living Lord!" he cried, which was the battle cry of the heroes of Sassoun. And he charged into the army like a storm, and scattered it in every direction, until the soldiers threw down their weapons and ran home.

Then David rode to the Melik's own tent. "Come out and fight me yourself," he called, "and leave these poor soldiers alone."

The Melik came out. "Very well," he said. "We will each take three blows. I will strike first."

David agreed, and he stood still while the Melik struck at him with his heavy club. The Melik struck once, and the dust rose up like a cloud, and the people of Sassoun cried out that David must be dead. But when the dust cleared, there was David, still standing. "Strike again," he said. The Melik struck a second time, and a third, with all his strength. But each time the dust cleared, David was still standing.

"Now it's my turn," said David.

The Melik was so afraid that he ran and hid in a deep pit. His men covered it with forty buffalo hides, and on top of the hides they piled forty millstones. But David drew the Lightning Sword, and struck one mighty blow. The sword cut through the forty millstones, and through the forty buffalo hides, and that was the end of the Melik of Msr.

Then David rode home to Sassoun on Kurkik Jalali, and the people came out to meet him with music and dancing. And from that day on, Sassoun paid taxes to no one.
