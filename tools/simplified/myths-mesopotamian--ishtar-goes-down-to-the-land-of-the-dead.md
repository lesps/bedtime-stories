Ishtar was the Queen of Heaven. And once she went down to the land of the dead, to look for Tammuz, whom she had loved when she was young.

"I spread out my hands like a bird," she said. "I go down, down to the house of darkness, the house that no one ever leaves, along the road from which no one ever comes back. There is no light there. The people there eat dust and mud. They are covered with feathers, like birds, and they live in the dark. And dust lies thick over the door."

When Ishtar came to the gate of the land of the dead, she called to the gatekeeper. "Keeper, open your gate and let me in! If you don't open it, I will smash the door and break the bolts, and I will let all the dead out, until there are more dead than living!"

"Wait," said the gatekeeper. "First I must ask the Queen of the Dead." And he went to Ereshkigal, the Queen of the Dead, and told her that Ishtar, the Queen of Heaven, had come.

Ereshkigal was very angry. She thought of all the people who had died because of Ishtar. "Let me weep for the strong men who have left their wives behind," she said, "and for the young women who have lost their husbands, and for the only son who was taken away before his time."

Then she gave a stern order. "Go, keeper, open the gate to her. Treat her by the old rules, the same as everyone else who comes here."

So the gatekeeper opened the gate, and Ishtar began to go down. There were seven gates, one after another. At the first gate, the keeper took her crown. At the second gate, he took her earrings. At the third gate, he took her necklace of precious stones. At the fourth gate, he took the jewels from her breast. At the fifth gate, he took her jewelled belt. At the sixth gate, he took the bracelets from her hands and feet. And at the seventh gate, he took her robe.

At every gate, Ishtar asked, "Why are you doing this to me?" And every time the keeper said, "That is the command of the Queen of the Dead."

At last, after a long, long way down, Ishtar stood before Ereshkigal with nothing left at all. But she was still proud, and Ereshkigal could not make her humble. So Ereshkigal told Namtar, the demon of sickness, to make Ishtar ill all over, from head to foot.

And while Ishtar was shut up in the land of the dead, nothing on earth would grow, and no young ones were born.

Then Papsukkal, the messenger of the gods, hurried to Shamash, the sun god, to tell him what had happened. Shamash went to his father Sin, the moon god, and to Ea, the god of the deep waters. And Ea made a new creature, called Asushunamir, to rescue Ishtar. He gave Asushunamir the power to pass through all seven gates of the land of the dead.

When Asushunamir gave Ereshkigal his message, she beat her breast and bit her thumb. She was furious, and she cursed him. "May you be shut up in the great prison!" she said. "May the rubbish from under the city be your food, and the city drains be your drink! May you live in a dark dungeon, and may your children be hungry and thirsty!"

But she had to do what the great gods said. So she said to Namtar, "Give Ishtar the water of life, and bring her to me."

Then Ishtar was led back up through the seven gates. At every gate, she was given back the thing that had been taken from her: her robe, her bracelets, her belt, her jewels, her necklace, her earrings and her crown.

Then Namtar said to her, "You have not paid a ransom to the Queen of the Dead for letting you go. So turn back to her, for the sake of Tammuz, whom you loved when you were young. Pour the shining water of life over him, and dress him in fine clothes, and put a crystal ring on him."

And Ishtar wept for Tammuz, and beat her breast. "O Tammuz, my only brother," she cried, "the one who gave me a crystal ring and a bracelet of emeralds! Let all the men and women who mourn come together and weep for him."
