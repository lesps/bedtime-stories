---
title: Bhima Meets Hanuman
adaptedFrom: ganguli-mahabharata--bhima-meets-hanuman
order: 27
namesNote: From the third book of the Mahabharata. Bhima and Hanuman are brothers because both are sons of the Wind god. Arjuna is Bhima's brother, and Duryodhana is their cousin and enemy.
---

Long ago, the five Pandava brothers were living in exile, and they came to the mountain Gandhamadana, high in the Himalayas. There the wind blew a wonderful golden lotus to their queen, Draupadi, and she asked Bhima, the strongest of the brothers, to bring her more.

So Bhima set off up the mountain. He crashed through the forest like an elephant, roaring like thunder. He blew his great conch shell, and slapped his arms with his hands, and the caves of the mountain echoed. The lions woke up in their dens and roared, and the elephants trumpeted with fear.

Now Hanuman, the great monkey hero, lived on that mountain. He heard all the noise, and he knew that it was Bhima, his own brother, for they were both sons of the Wind god. The path that Bhima was taking led up to heaven, where no human being could go. So, to keep Bhima safe, Hanuman lay down right across the narrow path, in a grove of banana trees, and dozed. He yawned, and thumped his long tail on the ground, and the sound of it echoed like thunder all over the mountain.

When Bhima heard the noise, the hair on his body stood on end, and he went to see what it was. There, lying on a rock among the banana trees, he found a huge monkey, glowing like fire, with a coppery face and bright eyes and a long, hairy tail held up like a flag. Bhima strode up to him and gave a great shout.

Hanuman opened his eyes a little, and looked at Bhima. "I'm not well," he said, "and I was having a lovely sleep. Why did you wake me? A sensible man like you should be kind to all creatures. Who are you, and where are you going? You can't go any further. This is the path of the gods. People can't go this way. Stay here, and eat some fruit, and then go home."

"Who are you?" said Bhima. "I am Bhima, a son of King Pandu, and a son of the Wind god. Let me pass!"

"I am only a monkey," said Hanuman, "and I won't let you pass. You had better go back."

"Get up and let me by," said Bhima, "or you'll be sorry."

"I'm too ill to get up," said Hanuman. "If you must go on, jump over me."

"The Spirit of God lives in every body," said Bhima, "and so I won't jump over you. If it weren't for that, I would jump over you, and the mountain too, just as Hanuman leaped over the ocean."

"Who is this Hanuman, who leaped over the ocean?" asked the monkey. "Tell me about him."

"He is my brother," said Bhima proudly. "He is the great monkey hero of the Ramayana, who leaped across the ocean in one jump to find Rama's queen. And I am just as strong as he is. So get up, and let me pass, or I'll make you!"

"I am too old to get up," said Hanuman. "Be kind to me. Just move my tail out of the way, and go on."

Bhima smiled to himself. "I'll grab this weak old monkey by the tail," he thought, "and throw him out of the way." So he took hold of the tail with his left hand. But he couldn't move it. Then he took hold of it with both hands, and pulled with all his might. His eyebrows went up and his eyes rolled, his face screwed up into wrinkles and he dripped with sweat. But he still couldn't lift the tail.

At last Bhima gave up. He stood in front of the monkey, very embarrassed, and bowed with his hands joined. "Forgive me for my rude words," he said. "Who are you, who look like a monkey? Are you a god?"

"I am Hanuman," said the monkey. "I am the son of the Wind god." And he told Bhima how he had helped Rama to find Sita, and leaped across the ocean, and burned the city of Lanka, and how Rama had built a bridge across the sea, and defeated the wicked Ravana, and brought Sita home. "And I asked Rama for a gift," said Hanuman, "that I might live for as long as people tell the story of his deeds. That is why I am still here. This is the path of the gods, and people cannot pass this way. That is why I stopped you, so that no one would hurt or curse you. But the lake you are looking for is that way."

Bhima was overjoyed. He bowed to Hanuman. "How lucky I am!" he said. "I have met my elder brother! Now please, show me the shape you had when you leaped across the ocean."

"No one can see that shape now," said Hanuman. "Times have changed since those days." And he told Bhima about the four ages of the world, and how goodness grows less in each one.

But Bhima would not give up. "I won't go away until I have seen it," he said.

So Hanuman smiled, and began to grow. He grew bigger and bigger, until he was as tall as the Vindhya mountains, and covered the whole grove. He shone like the sun, like a golden mountain, and Bhima had to close his eyes.

"This is as much as you can bear to see," said Hanuman. "But I can grow as big as I like."

"I have seen it," said Bhima, trembling. "Please make yourself smaller again! I can't look at you. With you beside him, why did Rama need to fight Ravana himself? You could have destroyed Lanka all by yourself."

"You are right," said Hanuman. "Ravana was no match for me. But if I had killed him, Rama would not have won the glory. So I left him for Rama."

Then Hanuman made himself small again, and hugged Bhima, and at once all Bhima's tiredness went away, and he felt stronger than ever before. Hanuman had tears in his eyes. "Go home now, brother," he said, "and remember me sometimes. But don't tell anyone that I live here. Meeting you has reminded me of Rama. Ask me for a gift. Shall I go and fight your enemies, Duryodhana and his brothers?"

"Just be our friend," said Bhima. "With you to help us, we will beat all our enemies."

"Then I will help you," said Hanuman. "When you go into battle, I will sit on the flag on your brother Arjuna's chariot. And when you roar, I will roar with you, and frighten your enemies."

Then Hanuman showed Bhima the way to go, and vanished.
