namesNote: Punt was a faraway land, probably on the coast of Africa, where the Egyptians went to buy incense and perfumes. A cubit is about half a metre.

A long time ago, an Egyptian nobleman came home from a long voyage. One of his servants, a sailor, said to him, "Be glad, master! We're home again, safe and well, and not one man is missing. Now go and tell the king all about our journey."

But the nobleman did not want to listen to his chatter. "Be careful what you say," he told him.

"Then let me tell you something that happened to me," said the sailor.

And this is the sailor's story.

"Once, I was sailing to the king's mines. Our ship was a hundred and fifty cubits long, and forty cubits wide, and it had a hundred and fifty of the best sailors in Egypt, who had seen heaven and earth, and whose hearts were braver than lions. They said that there would be no storm.

"But as we came near the land, a great wind blew up, and the waves rose eight cubits high. I grabbed hold of a piece of wood. But the ship sank, and all the other sailors were lost. Not one was left.

"For three days the waves carried me, all alone. Then a wave threw me up onto an island. I crawled under a bush, into the shade. Then I got up to look for food. And I found figs and grapes, and all kinds of good vegetables, berries and grain, melons, fish and birds. There was nothing that the island did not have. I ate until I was full, and I lit a fire, and I made an offering to the gods.

"Suddenly I heard a noise like thunder. I thought it was a great wave. The trees shook, and the ground trembled. I uncovered my face, and I saw a huge serpent coming towards me. He was thirty cubits long, with a beard more than two cubits long. His body was covered in gold, and his colour was a beautiful deep blue.

"He reared up in front of me, and I threw myself flat on my face. And he said, 'Who brought you here, little one? Who brought you to this island? Tell me quickly, or I will make you vanish like a flame!'

"Then he picked me up gently in his mouth, and carried me to his home, and set me down without a scratch. And he asked me again, 'Who brought you here, little one, to this island in the middle of the sea?'

"So I told him all about our ship and the storm, and how all the others were lost, and how a wave had thrown me onto his island.

"'Don't be afraid, little one,' said the serpent. 'Don't look so sad. If you have come to me, it is God who has saved you. He has brought you to this island of plenty, where there is everything good. You will stay here for four months. Then a ship will come from your own country, with sailors you know, and you will go home with them.

"'I live here with my brothers and my children. There are seventy-five of us serpents. And if you are brave, and wait patiently, you will hold your children in your arms again, and hug your wife, and see your own home again.'

"I bowed down before him. 'I will tell the king of Egypt all about you,' I said. 'I will bring you precious oils and perfumes and incense from the temples. I will send you ships full of all the treasures of Egypt.'

"But the serpent smiled at that. 'You haven't got much in the way of perfumes,' he said. 'All you have is ordinary incense. But I am the Prince of the land of Punt, and I have all the perfumes in the world. And when you leave this place, you will never see this island again. It will sink beneath the waves.'

"And just as he had said, after four months a ship came. I climbed a tall tree to look at it, and I ran to tell the serpent. But he already knew.

"'Goodbye, little one,' he said. 'Go home, and see your children again, and let your name be honoured in your town.'

"Then he gave me wonderful gifts: perfumes, and sweet-smelling woods, and incense, and elephant tusks, and baboons and monkeys, and all kinds of precious things. I loaded them all onto the ship, and I bowed down, and I thanked God for him.

"'In two months you will be home,' said the serpent, 'and you will hold your children again.' And I went down to the ship, and on the shore I bowed once more to the Prince of the island, and to everyone who lived there.

Then the sailor said to his master, "So when we come to the king's palace, take me with you to the king. Let me tell him my story, and show him the gifts I brought. It's good to listen to people. And as I was told long ago: be wise, and you will be honoured."
