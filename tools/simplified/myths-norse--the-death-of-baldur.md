Baldur was the son of Odin and his wife, Frigg, and he was the best and kindest of all the gods. He was so beautiful that light seemed to shine from him. Everyone loved him.

But one night, Baldur began to have terrible dreams, dreams that his life was in danger. He told the other gods, and they were very worried.

So his mother, Frigg, went all over the world, and she asked everything in it to make a promise never to hurt Baldur. Fire and water promised. Iron and every other metal promised. Stones and the earth, trees and sicknesses, animals and birds, poisons and snakes, every one of them promised that they would never hurt Baldur.

Now Baldur was safe! And the gods made up a game. Baldur would stand in the middle, and the other gods would throw things at him, sticks and stones, even spears and axes. And nothing would hurt him! Everything just bounced off. The gods thought it was a wonderful game.

But Loki did not like it. He was jealous of Baldur, and he wanted to spoil things. So he disguised himself as an old woman and went to visit Frigg.

"What are the gods doing over there?" asked the old woman.

"They are throwing things at Baldur," said Frigg, "and nothing can hurt him. Everything in the world has promised me."

"Everything?" asked the old woman. "Has every single thing promised not to hurt Baldur?"

"Well," said Frigg, "there is one little plant, growing on an oak tree west of Valhalla. It is called mistletoe. It seemed too young and small to ask, so I didn't bother."

The old woman hurried away. Loki threw off his disguise, went straight to the oak tree and pulled up the mistletoe. Then he went back to where the gods were playing their game.

Baldur's brother, Hodur, was standing at the edge of the crowd, not playing. Hodur was blind.

"Why aren't you throwing anything at Baldur?" asked Loki.

"Because I can't see where he is," said Hodur. "And I have nothing to throw."

"You should join in, like everyone else," said Loki. "Here, take this little stick. I'll show you which way to throw it."

So Hodur took the sprig of mistletoe, and Loki guided his hand, and Hodur threw it. The mistletoe flew straight at Baldur. And it was the one thing that had never promised. It struck him, and Baldur fell down dead.

The gods could not speak. They could not move. They were too shocked even to cry. Then, at last, they all burst into tears. And Odin was the saddest of all, because he knew how great a loss this was.

Then Frigg asked, "Who will ride down to the land of the dead, to ask Hel, its queen, to let Baldur come home?"

Baldur's brave brother Hermod said he would go. He borrowed Odin's eight-legged horse, Sleipnir, and rode for nine nights through deep, dark valleys, until he came to a golden bridge over a roaring river, and at last to the gates of Hel's land. Sleipnir leaped right over the gates. And inside, Hermod found his brother Baldur, sitting in the place of honor.

Meanwhile, the gods held Baldur's funeral. They laid him on his great ship, with all his treasures. His wife, Nanna, was so heartbroken that she died of grief, and she was laid beside him. Odin put his magic golden ring, Draupnir, on the ship too. Then the ship was set on fire and pushed out to sea, burning brightly.

Down in the land of the dead, Hermod begged Hel to let Baldur go home. "Everyone is weeping for him," he said.

"We shall see if that is true," said Hel. "If everything in the world, living and not living, weeps for Baldur, then he may go home. But if even one thing refuses to weep, then he must stay here with me."

Before Hermod left, Baldur gave him the golden ring, Draupnir, to take back to Odin, so that Odin would remember him.

The gods sent messengers all over the world, asking everything to weep for Baldur. And everything did! People and animals wept. The earth and the stones and the trees wept, and so did all the metals. That is why, when frosty things come into a warm room, they get wet, as if they are crying.

But then the messengers found a giantess sitting in a cave. Her name was Thokk. "Please weep for Baldur," they said, "so that he can come home."

"I won't weep for Baldur," said Thokk. "Let Hel keep what she has."

And so Baldur had to stay in the land of the dead. People say that the giantess was really Loki in disguise, spoiling things one more time.

But the old stories say that Baldur will not stay there forever. After the end of the world, when a new green world rises up from the sea, Baldur will come back.
