The Holy Grail was the holy cup that Jesus used. It was the holiest thing in the world, and long ago, the stories say, it was brought to Britain. But because of all the wrong things people did, it was hidden away, and only now and then did it appear, carried by angels, or floating in a heavenly light.

One Pentecost, all the knights of the Round Table were sitting at supper in Camelot. Suddenly they heard a great noise like thunder, so loud that it seemed the roof would fall in. And through the thunder came a sunbeam, seven times brighter than the brightest day, and every knight's face shone more beautifully than ever before. Nobody could speak. Then the Holy Grail floated into the hall, covered with a cloth of white silk, so that no one could see it, or who was carrying it. The hall was filled with sweet smells, and in front of every knight appeared the food he liked best. Then the Grail was gone, and nobody knew where.

When they could speak again, Sir Gawain stood up. "Tomorrow," he said, "I will set out to look for the Holy Grail, and I will not come back until I have seen it, or until a year and a day have passed." And many of the other knights made the same promise.

But King Arthur was very sad. "Gawain, Gawain," he said, with tears in his eyes, "you have broken up the finest company of knights the world has ever seen. Once they leave, they will never all sit at the Round Table together again, for many of them will not come back. And I have loved them as much as my own life."

Among the knights that night was the youngest of them all, Sir Galahad, the son of Sir Lancelot. The next morning, a hundred and fifty knights rode out of Camelot on the quest, while the people wept in the streets, and the king was too sad to speak. And at sunrise they all went their separate ways.

Galahad had no shield. After four days he came to an abbey, where the monks showed him a shield as white as snow, with a red cross in the middle. "No knight may carry this shield," they said, "unless he is the best knight in the world. Anyone else who takes it will come to harm." Another knight, King Bagdemagus, tried to carry it away. But he had not gone two miles before a strange knight in white armour knocked him off his horse and took the shield from him. "Take this shield to Sir Galahad," said the White Knight, "for no one else may carry it." So Galahad hung the white shield round his neck, and rode on.

Sir Lancelot was also looking for the Grail. One night he came to an old chapel in the forest. Through the broken door he could see an altar, and a great silver candlestick shining with light. He longed to go in, but he could not. So he lay down to sleep outside, by a stone cross.

As he lay there, half asleep and half awake, a sick knight came by, carried in a litter. "Oh, Lord," the sick knight prayed, "when will the Holy Grail come to me, and make me well?" And at once the shining candlestick and the Holy Grail appeared beside the cross, though nobody had carried them there. The sick knight crawled to the Grail on his hands and knees, and prayed, and he was made well. Then the Grail went back into the chapel. Lancelot wanted to follow it, but he could not move, because of all the wrong things he had done in his life.

When Lancelot woke up properly, a voice spoke to him. "Lancelot," it said, "you are harder than stone. Go away from this holy place." And Lancelot wept, because he knew that he was not good enough to find the Grail.

Meanwhile, Sir Percivale was having adventures of his own. Once, when he had lost his horse, a strange woman lent him a huge black horse. It galloped so fast that in less than an hour it had carried him four days' journey, and it would have plunged into a roaring river if Percivale had not pulled on the reins. Then Percivale made the sign of the cross. At once the horse threw him off, and dashed into the water, howling, and the water seemed to burn. It had been an evil spirit, not a horse at all!

The next morning, Percivale found himself on a wild mountain by the sea. There he saw a serpent carrying off a lion cub, and a great lion fighting the serpent. Percivale drew his sword and helped the lion, and killed the serpent. And the lion was so grateful that it rubbed against him like a dog, and that night it lay down at his feet, and they slept side by side.

Then there was Sir Bors. He promised a holy man that he would live a good, clean life, and eat nothing but bread and water until he had sat at the table of the Grail. And he kept his promise. At last a voice told him to go down to the sea. There he found a ship hung with white silk, and as soon as he stepped aboard, it sailed away as fast as flying. When Bors woke up the next morning, there was Sir Percivale, asleep on the same ship! The two friends were overjoyed to see each other. "All we need now is Galahad," said Percivale.

And soon Galahad came too, led by a young lady who turned out to be Percivale's sister. They sailed to a strange land, and there they found another ship, with no one on board. On a bed in the middle of the ship lay a wonderful sword. Its handle was made of the bones of two magic creatures, and whoever held it would never grow tired. "Only the best knight in the world can hold this sword," said Percivale's sister. Percivale tried to take hold of it, and could not. Bors tried, and could not. But Galahad gripped it easily, and Percivale's sister fastened it round his waist.

Then the four companions had many adventures together. And in the end, Percivale's sister gave her life to save another lady. As she had asked, they laid her in a boat covered with black silk, and let the wind carry it away.

Later, Sir Lancelot found that same boat, and stepped on board, and it carried him away across the water. And one day a young knight rode up to the shore and came on board too.

"What is your name?" the young knight asked. "My heart goes out to you."

"My name is Lancelot," he said.

"Then you are my father," said the young knight. It was Galahad. He knelt down and asked for his father's blessing, and they hugged each other with great joy. For half a year they sailed together on that boat, and they were very happy. But one day a knight in white armour came to the shore, leading a white horse. "Galahad," he said, "you have been with your father long enough. Come, and go on with your quest."

So Galahad kissed his father goodbye. "Dear father," he said, "I don't know if I will see you again until I have seen the Holy Grail." And a voice said, "They will not see each other again in this world." So they said goodbye for ever. Lancelot went back to Camelot, where he found that more than half of the knights of the Round Table had been lost on the quest.

Galahad rode on, and he found Percivale and Bors again. They came to the castle of King Pelles, and there Galahad healed the Maimed King. Then the three knights went down to the sea, and there was a ship waiting for them, and in it was a silver table, and on the table stood the Holy Grail, covered in red silk. And it carried them far away, to a city called Sarras.

At the gate of the city sat an old man who had not walked without crutches for ten years. "Help us carry this table," said Galahad. "I can't walk," said the old man. "Never mind that," said Galahad. "Get up, and try." And the old man stood up, and he was well again!

But the king of Sarras did not believe what they told him about the Holy Grail, and he threw them into a deep dungeon. Even there, they were not unhappy, because the Grail came to them in a vision and comforted them. After a year, the king fell ill. Before he died, he asked them to forgive him, and they did. Then a voice told the people of the city to make the youngest of the three knights their king. So Galahad became the king of Sarras.

He had a golden chest made, set with jewels, to hold the silver table and the Holy Grail, and every day he and his two friends knelt in front of it and prayed.

A year later, on the very day he had been crowned, Galahad came to pray as usual. And this time he saw a holy man kneeling in front of the Grail, surrounded by angels. "Come, Galahad," said the holy man, "and see what you have longed to see." Galahad trembled, and looked, and saw the Holy Grail at last.

"Thank you, Lord," said Galahad, "for now I have seen what I have wanted to see for so long. Now I do not wish to live any longer, if it pleases you."

He kissed Percivale and Bors goodbye. "Give my love to my father, Sir Lancelot," he said. Then he knelt down to pray. And as he prayed, his soul left his body, and Percivale and Bors saw angels carry it up to heaven. Then a hand came down from heaven, and took the Holy Grail, and carried it away. And since that day, nobody has ever seen the Holy Grail again.

Percivale and Bors were very sad, and so were all the people of the city. Percivale went to live as a hermit, and a little over a year later he died too. Then Bors went home to Camelot, and told King Arthur everything that had happened, and the king's clerks wrote it all down. And Bors gave Sir Lancelot his son's message.
