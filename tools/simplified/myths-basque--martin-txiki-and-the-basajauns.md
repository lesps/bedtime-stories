---
title: Martin Txiki and the Basajauns
basedOn: the Basque legend of Martin Txiki (Little Martin), who stole the secret of wheat from the Basajauns, the wild men of the woods, as it was told in the Basque villages and written down by folklorists in the twentieth century
workId: basque-martin-txiki
order: 2
---

Long ago, the people of the Basque Country did not know how to grow wheat. They had no bread to eat. The only ones who knew the secret of wheat were the Basajauns, the wild men of the woods.

The Basajauns were tall and hairy and enormously strong, and they lived in caves high up in the mountains. They kept their wheat in a great heap in their cave, and they guarded it carefully. They would never give a single grain of it away.

But there was a clever young man called Martin Txiki, which means Little Martin. And Martin Txiki made up his mind to get some of that wheat.

So one day he climbed up the mountain to the Basajauns' cave. There, in the middle of the cave, was the great golden heap of wheat.

"Good day, Basajauns," said Martin. "I hear that you are great jumpers. I bet I can jump higher than any of you. Shall we have a contest? We could jump over that heap of wheat."

The Basajauns laughed. "You, little man? Jump better than us? Very well, let's see!"

So the Basajauns jumped, one after another, right over the heap of wheat, without even touching it.

Then it was Martin Txiki's turn. He took a run, and jumped, and fell, plop, right into the middle of the heap of wheat. "Oh dear," he said. "I'm not as good a jumper as I thought." And he climbed out again, and said goodbye.

But Martin had fallen into the wheat on purpose. His shoes were full of grains of wheat! As soon as he was out of the cave, he started to run.

The Basajauns soon saw what he had done. One of them threw his axe after Martin. But Martin was already far away down the mountain, and the axe missed him.

When he got home, Martin shook the grains of wheat out of his shoes, and planted them. But he didn't know the right time to plant them, and his wheat did not grow well.

So one day he crept back up the mountain, and hid near the Basajauns' cave, and listened. And he heard one of the Basajauns singing a song to himself:

"When the leaves come out, plant the maize. When the leaves fall, sow the wheat."

Then Martin knew. He waited until the leaves fell from the trees in the autumn, and he sowed his wheat. And the next summer it grew tall and golden, and he had wheat enough to make bread, and to give seeds to all his neighbours.

And that is how the Basque people learned to grow wheat, thanks to clever Martin Txiki, who stole the secret from the Basajauns.
