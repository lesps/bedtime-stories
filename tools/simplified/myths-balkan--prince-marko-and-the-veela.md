---
title: Prince Marko and the Veela
adaptedFrom: petrovitch-serbian--prince-marko-and-the-veela
order: 3
namesNote: Petrovitch spells the names Milosh, Mirotch and Raviyoyla; in Serbian they are Miloš, Miroč and Ravijojla. A veela (Serbian vila) is a mountain fairy, and a blood brother (pobratim) is a friend who has sworn to be a brother.
---

Early one morning, Prince Marko and his friend Milosh were riding together over the beautiful mountain of Miroch. They loved each other like brothers, for they had sworn to be blood brothers.

As they rode along, Marko began to feel sleepy, nodding on the back of his horse Sharatz. "Sing me a song, brother," he said, "to keep me awake."

"I would love to sing for you, Marko," said Milosh. "But last night I met the veela Raviyoyla, the fairy of the mountain. And she told me that if she ever heard me singing on this mountain again, she would shoot me with her arrows."

"Don't be afraid of the veela," said Marko. "Not while I'm alive, and while I have Sharatz and my six-edged club. Sing, brother!"

So Milosh began to sing. He sang a beautiful song about their brave and good ancestors, the kings who had ruled the land long ago, and how each one of them had built a church. The song was so lovely that it lulled Marko to sleep.

But the veela heard the song too. And she began to sing along with Milosh, trying her very best to show that she could sing better than he could. But Milosh had a magnificent voice, and he sang better than the veela. That made her so angry that she took her bow and shot two arrows at Milosh, and wounded him.

"Oh, Marko, my brother!" cried Milosh. "The veela has shot me! Didn't I tell you I mustn't sing on this mountain?"

Marko woke up at once. He jumped down from the saddle and tightened Sharatz's straps. Then he hugged his horse and whispered in his ear, "Sharo, my fast one, you must catch the veela for me now! If you do, I'll give you horseshoes of pure silver and gold, and a silk cloak down to your knees, with silk tassels down to your hooves, and I'll braid your mane with gold thread and pearls. But if you don't catch her, I don't know what I'll do!"

Then Marko leapt onto Sharatz and galloped after the veela. She was already flying over the mountain top. When Sharatz saw her, he leapt forward, as high as three lances and as far as four lances with every bound. In a moment he had caught up with her. The frightened veela flew up towards the clouds. But Marko threw his great club after her, and it struck her between her white shoulders, and she fell to the ground.

"Why did you shoot my brother?" cried Marko. "You had better bring him healing herbs, or it will be the worse for you!"

"For God's sake, brother Marko," begged the veela, "spare me, and I'll go through the mountain and gather herbs to heal your friend!"

Marko was always moved when someone asked him something for God's sake. So he let the veela go. And she went off to gather the herbs, but she always stayed close enough to hear when Marko called her.

When she had gathered the herbs, the veela brought them to Milosh and healed his wounds. And his voice was not only as good as before, it was even better, and his heart was stronger than ever. Then the two blood brothers rode on together over the mountains.

And when the veela went back to her sisters, she warned them, "Listen, my sisters! As long as Prince Marko and his horse Sharatz are alive, don't ever shoot at any hero in the mountains. You wouldn't believe what I've been through today! I'm amazed I'm still alive!"
