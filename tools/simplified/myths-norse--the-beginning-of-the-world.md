In the very beginning there was no sand and no sea, no earth below and no sky above. There was only a great, empty gap, yawning and dark, called Ginnungagap.

To the north of the gap lay Niflheim, a land of freezing fog and ice. To the south lay Muspell, a land of fire, so bright and hot that no one could go there unless they had been born there. At its edge sat a fire giant named Surt, holding a flaming sword.

Out of Niflheim flowed icy rivers. They poured into the empty gap and froze, layer upon layer of ice and frost. But sparks and hot winds blew in from Muspell. Where the warm air met the ice, the ice began to melt and drip. And from the drips, life began. The melting ice took the shape of an enormous giant. His name was Ymir, and he was the first of the frost giants.

Out of the melting frost came a cow, too. Her name was Audhumla. Four rivers of milk flowed from her, and Ymir drank them and grew.

The cow had nothing to eat but the ice, so she licked the frosty, salty stones. On the first day, as she licked, a man's hair appeared out of the ice. On the second day, a head appeared. On the third day, a whole man stepped out. He was handsome and strong, and his name was Buri.

Buri had a son, and that son married a giant's daughter, and they had three sons: Odin, Vili and Ve. Odin was the greatest of them, and he would become the father and ruler of all the gods.

But Ymir and his frost giant children were wicked and wild. So the three brothers fought Ymir and defeated him. When the great giant fell, so much water poured out of him that it flooded the land, and nearly all the frost giants were drowned. Only one, named Bergelmir, escaped with his wife in a little boat, and all the later giants came from them.

Then Odin and his brothers did something amazing. They dragged Ymir's huge body into the middle of the empty gap, and they made the world out of it.

His flesh became the earth. His bones became the mountains. His teeth became the rocks and pebbles. His hair became the trees. The water that had poured out of him became the sea, which they put all around the earth like a ring.

They lifted up his skull and made it into the sky, arching over the earth like a great bowl. At each of its four corners they put a dwarf to hold it up, and the dwarfs were named East, West, North and South. Then they caught the sparks flying out of Muspell and set them in the sky to be the stars, and gave each one its place, so that there would be day and night, and seasons, and years.

The giants were given the far-off shores at the edge of the sea to live on. But to keep the middle of the world safe from them, the brothers built a wall around it. They made the wall out of Ymir's eyebrows, and they called the land inside it Midgard, the Middle Garden. And they threw Ymir's brains up into the sky, where they became the clouds.

One day the three brothers were walking along the seashore, and they found two pieces of wood, two logs lying on the sand. Out of them they made the first man and the first woman. Odin gave them life and breath. Vili gave them understanding and the power to move. Ve gave them faces and speech, hearing and sight. The man was called Ask, which means Ash Tree, and the woman was called Embla. They went to live in Midgard, and every person in the world comes from them.

Last of all, in the very middle of everything, the gods built a city for themselves called Asgard. And in Asgard, Odin had a high seat called Hlidskjalf. When he sat on it, he could look out over the whole world and see everything that was happening.
