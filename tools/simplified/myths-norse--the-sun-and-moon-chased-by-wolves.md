title: The Sun and Moon Chased by Wolves

Long ago there was a giant who had a daughter called Night. Like all her family, she was dark. Night married three times, and her last husband was one of the gods. Their son was called Day, and he was as bright and beautiful as his father's family.

Odin, the father of the gods, took Night and her son Day, gave each of them a horse and a chariot, and set them in the sky. Every day and night they ride around the world, one after the other.

Night rides first, on a horse called Frost-Mane. Every morning he wets the earth with the foam that drips from his bit, and that is where the dew comes from.

Day rides behind her, on a horse called Shining-Mane. His mane shines so brightly that it lights up all the sky and all the earth.

Now, there was once a man named Mundilfari, who had two children. They were so beautiful and bright that he named his son Moon and his daughter Sun. That was a very proud thing to do, and the gods were annoyed. So they took the two children and put them up in the sky to work. They made Sun drive the horses that pull the chariot of the sun, which the gods had made out of sparks from the land of fire. Her horses are called Early-Waker and All-Swift. Under their shoulders the gods put bellows full of cool air, to keep them from getting too hot.

Moon guides the moon across the sky, and he decides when it grows and when it shrinks. Once he took two children up from the earth to help him. They were a boy and a girl named Bil and Hjuki, and they were carrying a pail of water between them on a pole. If you look carefully at the moon, they say, you can still see them there, following him.

But why do the sun and the moon move so fast across the sky? Because they are being chased!

Two wolves are hunting them. One wolf, called Skoll, runs after the sun, and the sun has to hurry to stay ahead of him. Another wolf, called Hati, runs ahead of the sun, chasing the moon. These wolves were born to a giantess who lives in an iron forest east of Midgard, the mother of many wolves.

The strongest of all her wolves is called Moon-Hound. He grows fat on the lives of people when they die. And the old stories say that at the very end of the world he will catch the moon and swallow it, and the sun will grow dark, and wild winds will howl.

But that day has not come yet. So the sun and moon keep running across the sky, just ahead of the wolves, every single day and night.
