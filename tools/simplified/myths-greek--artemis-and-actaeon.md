Actaeon was a young prince who loved to hunt. One hot morning he was out in the mountains with his friends and his pack of hunting dogs. At noon, when the sun was at its highest, he said, "We've hunted enough for today. Let's rest in the shade, and hunt again tomorrow."

His friends lay down under the trees. But Actaeon wandered off by himself to explore.

Nearby was a secret valley, full of tall pine trees. It belonged to Artemis, the goddess of the hunt and of the moon. At the end of the valley was a cool cave with a spring of clear water. When Artemis was tired from hunting, she came there to bathe in the pool, and no one was ever allowed to see her.

That day, Artemis was in the cave with her nymphs. One held her bow and arrows. One combed her hair. Others poured water from big jugs.

Just then, Actaeon came wandering in.

The nymphs screamed. They rushed to stand in front of the goddess and hide her. But Artemis was taller than all of them, and Actaeon saw her. Her face turned red with anger.

She had no arrows in her hand. So she scooped up some water and splashed it in his face.

"Now go," she said, "and tell everyone that you have seen Artemis, if you can."

At once, antlers sprouted from Actaeon's head. His neck grew long. His ears grew pointed. His hands and feet became hooves, and his skin was covered in a spotted coat. Actaeon had turned into a deer.

He was frightened, and he ran. He ran faster than he had ever run before. He came to a stream and saw his reflection in the water. He tried to cry out, "Oh no, what has happened to me?" But no words came out.

Then he heard barking. His own hunting dogs had caught his scent. They came racing through the trees, the whole pack, howling with excitement. They did not know he was their master. To them, he was only a deer.

Actaeon ran over rocks and through the woods, the same woods where he had chased so many deer himself. He wanted to shout, "Stop! It's me, Actaeon! Don't you know me?" But he could not speak.

His friends heard the dogs and came running. "Actaeon!" they called. "Come and see, the dogs have found a stag!" They looked all around for him, and wondered why he wasn't there.

The dogs caught up with the deer at last. And that was the end of Actaeon.

Some people said that Artemis had been too harsh. Others said she had every right to keep her secret pool private. The old Greeks told this story as a warning: some places belong to the gods, and it is wise to respect them.
