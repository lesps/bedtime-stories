namesNote: From the first book of the Mahabharata. The bard Sauti tells this story; Suka is Vyasa's son, and Sanjaya is the king's charioteer who tells the story of the great battle.

Long ago, the wise poet Vyasa made up the Mahabharata, the greatest of all poems. It held everything: the stories of gods and heroes and kings, the holy teachings, the sun and the moon and the stars, the ages of the world, the holy rivers and mountains and forests, the art of war, and all the peoples of the earth and their languages. But Vyasa made it up only in his mind, and when it was finished, he wondered how he could ever teach it to his pupils.

Then Brahma, the creator of the world, came to him in person. Vyasa was amazed. He bowed with his palms together, and gave Brahma a seat, and sat down beside him, smiling with joy.

"Lord Brahma," said Vyasa, "I have made a poem with everything in it. But there is no one on earth who can write it down."

"There will never be another poem as great as yours," said Brahma. "Think of Ganesha, and ask him to write it for you." And then Brahma went back to his own home.

So Vyasa thought of Ganesha, the god who clears away all difficulties. And as soon as Vyasa thought of him, Ganesha was there.

"Ganesha," said Vyasa, "please write down the poem I have made, as I say it to you."

"I will," said Ganesha, "but only if my pen never stops for a single moment."

"Very well," said Vyasa. "But you must not write anything until you understand it."

Ganesha agreed. He said the holy word "Om", and he began to write, and Vyasa began to speak. And just for fun, every now and then Vyasa made a verse so knotty and difficult that it was hard to understand.

Sauti the storyteller knew eight thousand eight hundred of these knotty verses, and so did Vyasa's son Suka, and perhaps Sanjaya too. But to this day nobody can untangle them. Even Ganesha, who knows everything, had to stop and think for a moment. And while Ganesha was thinking, Vyasa made up many more verses.

That is how the Mahabharata was written down.
