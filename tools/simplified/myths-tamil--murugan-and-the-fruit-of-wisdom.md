livingTradition: Hindus
namesNote: Murugan, the god of the Tamil hills, is the god the rest of India calls Kartikeya or Skanda. "Pazham nee" is Tamil for "You are the fruit". In the Shiva Purana's telling of this story, the prize is being married first.
---

Long ago, on Mount Kailasa, the great god Shiva and the goddess Parvati lived with their two sons. One was Ganesha, with his elephant head and his big round belly. The other was young Murugan, the brave warrior, who rode on a peacock.

One day the wandering sage Narada came to visit them, and he brought a wonderful gift: a golden mango. "This is the fruit of wisdom," he said. "Whoever eats it will know everything there is to know. But it must not be cut or shared. It must be eaten whole, by one person."

Shiva and Parvati looked at their two sons. Each of them wanted the fruit, and they loved them both the same. So Shiva said, "We will have a race. Whoever goes all the way around the world and comes back here first shall have the fruit."

At once Murugan jumped onto his peacock, and away he flew. Over the mountains and the forests he went, over the rivers and the deserts, over the seas and the islands, all the way around the wide world.

But Ganesha did not move. He sat and thought. "I am big and slow," he said to himself, "and the world is very wide. But what is the whole world, really?"

Then he got up. He put his hands together and bowed to his mother and father. And he walked slowly all the way around them, and bowed again.

"I have been around the world," he said. "My mother and father are my whole world."

Shiva and Parvati smiled, and they gave Ganesha the fruit of wisdom.

When Murugan came flying home on his peacock, tired and dusty, he found that his brother had already won the prize. Murugan was hurt and angry. "I went all the way around the world," he said, "and I have nothing!" So he took off his fine clothes and his jewels, and left Mount Kailasa. He went far to the south, to a green hill in the land of the Tamils, and stood on top of it alone, dressed only in a simple cloth, with a staff in his hand, like a holy man.

Shiva and Parvati followed him there, for they loved him dearly. They found him standing on the hill, and Parvati put her arms around him.

"My son," said Shiva, "why are you sad about a fruit? You don't need any fruit of wisdom. Pazham nee! You yourself are the fruit. You are wisdom itself."

And that is why the hill is called Palani, from "Pazham nee", to this day. Murugan stayed on the hill, and people still climb the steps to the top to visit him there. And the Tamil people call him their own god, the god of the hills.
