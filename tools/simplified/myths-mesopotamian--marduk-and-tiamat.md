---
title: Marduk and Tiamat
adaptedFrom: budge-babylonian-creation--the-seven-tablets-of-creation
order: 1
namesNote: This is the Babylonian story of creation, told on seven clay tablets. Budge spells the names Tiâmat and Apsû, and calls Ea by his other name, Nudimmud.
---

In the very beginning there was no sky and no land. There was only Apsu, a great dark mass of water, wild and muddled and without any order at all.

After a very long time, the first gods came into being. Then, after ages and ages, came Anshar, the lord of the hosts of heaven, and Kishar, the lady of the hosts of the earth. And after another long time came the great gods: Anu, the god of the sky, and Ea, the wise god of the waters, and many others.

As soon as the gods appeared, order came into the world. Things began to have their proper places and their proper ways.

But Apsu hated order. He liked everything muddled and wild. So he went to his wife, the great monster Tiamat, and they made a plan together to destroy the gods and their order. Tiamat had wings and scales and terrible claws, and a body like a huge serpent. She was the mother of everything, and she kept the Tablet of Destinies, which held the fate of the world.

But Ea, the wise god, knew a spell, a pure and holy spell. He went out and spoke it, and with it he overcame Apsu and Apsu's messenger, Mummu. And so the wild waters were given edges that they could not pass.

When Tiamat heard that Apsu was dead, she was filled with fury. "I will have my revenge on the gods!" she cried.

She gathered an army of monsters: the Viper and the Snake, the Whirlwind and the Storm, the Raging Dog, the Scorpion-Man, the Fish-Man and the Horned Beast. She made a god called Kingu their captain. She fastened the Tablet of Destinies on his chest and said, "Whatever you command will happen."

When Ea heard about Tiamat's army, he sat down in sorrow. He did not feel strong enough to fight her. So he went to Anshar, and Anshar was so troubled that he bit his lips.

Anshar sent Anu, the sky god, to try to calm Tiamat. But when Anu came near and saw her muttering in her rage, he was so frightened that he turned and ran away.

Then Ea brought his son Marduk to meet Anshar. Marduk was young and bright and strong, like the sun. "I will fight Tiamat," said Marduk. "But first the gods must make me their champion."

So all the gods gathered for a great feast. They ate bread and drank sweet wine, and they grew merry. Then they gave Marduk a wonderful power: whatever he commanded would happen at once.

Marduk wanted to test his new power. He pointed at a robe and said, "Disappear!" And it disappeared. Then he said, "Come back!" And it came back. Then the gods bowed to him as their king, and gave him a throne and a sceptre.

Then Marduk armed himself. He took a bow, a spear and a club. He filled himself with fire and set lightning before him. He took a great net to catch Tiamat, and he set the four winds around it so that she could not escape. He made new winds and storms to help him, and he took a thunderbolt in his hand. Then he climbed into his chariot, the Storm, pulled by four horses, and rode out to meet Tiamat.

When Kingu saw Marduk coming, he trembled and staggered and could not stand. And all of Tiamat's monsters were too frightened to move.

But Tiamat was not frightened. She shouted at Marduk and tried to put a spell on him. Her spell did nothing. Marduk threw his net over her. He sent a great wind into her mouth, so that she swelled up. Then he threw his spear, and Tiamat split apart, and that was the end of her.

Her monsters tried to run away, but the four winds were all around them. Marduk caught all eleven in his net. Then he took the Tablet of Destinies from Kingu, sealed it with his own seal, and fastened it on his own chest.

Next Marduk made a clever plan. He split Tiamat's great body into two halves. Out of one half he made the dome of the sky. Out of the other he made the home of Ea, over the deep waters. Then he gave each of the old gods a proper place in heaven.

But the gods were not happy. "There is nobody to look after our temples," they said, "and nobody to bring us gifts."

So Marduk made another clever plan. "I will make people," he said, "out of blood and bone."

He called the gods together. "Who started the war against us?" he asked.

"It was Kingu," said the gods. So they tied Kingu up and took him to Ea. And out of Kingu's blood, Ea made the first people, to look after the gods.

The Anunnaki gods were so grateful to Marduk that they built him a great temple in the city of Babylon. They made all the bricks themselves. When it was finished, Marduk gave each god a place in the temple, just as he had given each one a place in the sky. And the gods sang a long song of praise to Marduk, and called him by fifty great names, and agreed that he was king of them all.
