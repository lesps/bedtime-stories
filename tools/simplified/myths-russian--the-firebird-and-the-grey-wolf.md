Once upon a time, in a certain kingdom, there lived Tsar Vyslav, and he had three sons: Dmitri, Vassili and Ivan. The tsar had a wonderful garden, and in it grew his favourite apple tree, which bore apples of pure gold.

But every night the Firebird came flying into the garden. She had wings of gold, and eyes that shone like crystal, and she would sit in the apple tree and pick the golden apples, and fly away with them.

The tsar was very upset. "My sons," he said, "whichever of you can catch the Firebird alive shall have half my kingdom now, and all of it when I die."

The first night, Dmitri sat under the apple tree to watch. But he fell asleep, and never saw the Firebird come. The second night, Vassili watched, but he fell asleep too. The third night, Ivan watched. He sat for one hour, and two, and three. Then suddenly the whole garden lit up, as if by many fires. The Firebird flew down into the apple tree and began picking apples. Ivan crept up so quietly that he caught her by the tail! But he couldn't hold on. She pulled free and flew away, and all that was left in his hand was one feather.

That feather was so bright that when it was carried into a dark room, the room shone as if it were full of candles. The tsar was delighted, and kept the feather safe. But from then on, the Firebird never came to the garden again.

"My sons," said the tsar, "go out into the world and find the Firebird, and bring her to me alive."

Dmitri and Vassili set off. They were jealous of their little brother, because he had got the feather. Ivan wanted to go too. His father begged him to stay, because he was so young. But at last he let him go.

Ivan rode on and on, until he came to a stone pillar in a green field. On it was written: "Whoever goes straight on will be hungry and cold. Whoever goes to the right will stay safe and well, but his horse will die. Whoever goes to the left will die himself, but his horse will be safe." Ivan thought, "I can always find another horse," and he turned to the right.

On the third day, an enormous grey wolf leapt out at him. "Didn't you read on the pillar that your horse would die?" said the wolf. And it killed Ivan's horse, and ran off.

Ivan cried for his horse, and went on on foot. He walked all day, until he was so tired he could hardly stand. Then the Grey Wolf came back. "I'm sorry, Ivan," it said. "You are tired from walking, and it was I who killed your good horse. Climb on my back, and tell me where you want to go."

So Ivan climbed onto the wolf's back and told him he was looking for the Firebird. And the wolf ran faster than any horse, until at nightfall they came to a stone wall.

"Climb over this wall," said the wolf. "In the garden on the other side is the Firebird, in a golden cage. Take the bird, but don't touch the cage, or you'll be caught."

Ivan climbed over the wall and found the Firebird in her golden cage. He took out the bird. But then he thought, "What will I carry her in?" And he picked up the cage too. At once there was a great crashing and clanging all through the garden, for the cage was tied to wires. The guards woke up, and caught Ivan, and took him to their master, Tsar Dolmat.

"Aren't you ashamed of yourself, young man, stealing like that?" said Tsar Dolmat angrily. "If you had asked, I would have given you the Firebird. But I'll forgive you, if you'll do something for me. Go to the land of Tsar Afron, and bring me his horse with the golden mane. Then I will give you the Firebird. If not, I will tell the whole world that you are a thief."

Ivan went back to the Grey Wolf, very sad. "Why didn't you listen to me?" said the wolf. "Never mind. Climb on my back."

The wolf ran as fast as an arrow to the kingdom of Tsar Afron. At night they came to the royal stables. "Go in and take the golden-maned horse," said the wolf. "But don't touch the golden bridle on the wall."

Ivan went in and took the horse. But then he saw the golden bridle, and it was so beautiful that he took it down. At once there was a great crashing, for the bridle was tied to strings. The grooms woke up and caught him and took him to Tsar Afron.

"Is that how an honourable knight behaves?" said Tsar Afron. "I would have given you the horse if you'd asked. But I'll forgive you, if you bring me the Princess Yelena the Beautiful. I have loved her for a long time, but I can't win her. If you won't, I'll tell the whole world you are a thief."

Ivan went back to the wolf, crying. "Why didn't you listen to me?" said the wolf. "Never mind. Climb on my back."

The wolf ran to the kingdom of Yelena the Beautiful, and hid by the golden fence around her garden. That evening, the princess came out to walk in the garden with her ladies. The wolf leapt over the fence, caught her up, and carried her off to where Ivan was waiting under a green oak. "Quick, climb on!" said the wolf. And he ran off with them both, so fast that nobody could catch them.

But as they rode along together, Ivan and Yelena fell in love. And when they came to Tsar Afron's kingdom, Ivan began to cry. "How can I give her away," he said, "when I love her with all my heart?"

"I have done a lot for you, Ivan," said the wolf, "and I will do this too. I will turn myself into Yelena the Beautiful. Give me to Tsar Afron, and take the golden-maned horse. When you are far away, think of me, and I will come."

The wolf struck the ground and turned into a princess, exactly like Yelena the Beautiful. Ivan took this false princess to Tsar Afron, who was overjoyed, and gave Ivan the golden-maned horse. And Ivan rode away on it, with the real Yelena.

After three days, the false princess asked Tsar Afron if she could go for a walk in the fields, and he let her. Meanwhile Ivan, riding along with Yelena, had forgotten all about the wolf. Then he remembered. "Oh, where is my Grey Wolf?" And at once the Grey Wolf was standing beside him on the road.

When they came near Tsar Dolmat's kingdom, Ivan said, "Dear Grey Wolf, do me one more favour. Turn yourself into a golden-maned horse. For I don't want to give this one away."

So the wolf turned himself into a golden-maned horse, and Ivan rode him to Tsar Dolmat. Tsar Dolmat was so pleased that he held a feast for two days. Then he gave Ivan the Firebird in her golden cage. And Ivan rode off with Yelena, the Firebird, and the real golden-maned horse.

The next day, Tsar Dolmat went riding on his new horse. But the horse threw him off, turned back into the Grey Wolf, and ran after Ivan.

At last they came back to the place where the wolf had killed Ivan's horse. "I have served you faithfully," said the Grey Wolf. "Now you have a horse of your own. Goodbye." And he ran off, and Ivan cried to see him go.

Ivan and Yelena rode on. When they were nearly home, they lay down under a tree to rest from the heat of the sun, and fell fast asleep.

Just then, Ivan's brothers came by. They had travelled all over the world and found nothing. When they saw their sleeping brother, with a princess, a golden-maned horse and the Firebird, they were filled with envy. And they killed him. Then they woke Yelena, and threatened her, and made her promise to tell their father that they had won everything. Vassili took Yelena to marry her, and Dmitri took the horse and the Firebird.

Ivan lay there for thirty days. Then the Grey Wolf found him. Just then a raven flew down with her two young ones. The wolf caught one of the young ravens. "Please let my child go!" cried the mother raven.

"I will," said the wolf, "if you fly far away and bring me the water of death and the water of life."

The raven flew off, and after three days she came back with two little bottles. The wolf sprinkled Ivan with the water of death, and his wounds closed up and he was whole again. Then he sprinkled him with the water of life, and Ivan stood up and said, "Oh, how long I've slept!"

"You would have slept for ever without me," said the wolf. "Your brothers killed you, and today Vassili is marrying your princess. Quick, climb on my back!"

The wolf carried Ivan home. When he walked into the palace, there was Yelena sitting at the wedding feast beside Vassili. But as soon as she saw Ivan, she jumped up and kissed him. "This is my true bridegroom, Ivan," she cried, "not that wicked man at the table!"

Then she told the tsar the whole truth. The tsar was so angry that he threw Dmitri and Vassili into prison. And Ivan married Yelena the Beautiful, and they loved each other so much that they could not bear to be apart for a single minute.
