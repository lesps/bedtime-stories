---
title: The Ten Plagues
adaptedFrom: jps-1917--the-ten-plagues
order: 11
namesNote: From chapters 7 to 12 of Exodus. Jewish families remember this story every spring at the festival of Passover.
---

Moses and his brother Aaron went to Pharaoh, the king of Egypt, just as the Lord had told them. Aaron threw down his staff in front of Pharaoh and his servants, and it became a snake. But Pharaoh called for his wise men and magicians, and they threw down their staffs too, and with their secret magic they also became snakes. Then Aaron's staff swallowed up all of theirs. But Pharaoh's heart was hard, and he would not listen.

So the Lord said to Moses, "Go to Pharaoh in the morning, when he goes down to the river. Say to him, 'The Lord, the God of the Hebrews, says: Let My people go, so that they may worship Me in the wilderness.'"

Pharaoh would not listen. So Aaron lifted his staff and struck the water of the river, and all the water in the river turned to blood. The fish died, the river smelled terrible, and the Egyptians could not drink its water. They had to dig beside the river to find water to drink. But the magicians did the same thing with their secret magic, and Pharaoh's heart was hard, and he would not listen.

Seven days later, the Lord sent Moses to Pharaoh again. "Let My people go," said Moses. But Pharaoh would not. So Aaron stretched out his staff over the waters of Egypt, and frogs came up and covered the land. There were frogs in the houses, frogs in the bedrooms, frogs on the beds, frogs in the ovens and in the bowls of dough!

Pharaoh sent for Moses and Aaron. "Pray to the Lord to take the frogs away," he said, "and I will let the people go." So Moses prayed, and the frogs died. The people gathered them up in heaps, and the land stank. But when Pharaoh saw that the trouble was over, he made his heart hard again, and would not let the people go.

Then Aaron struck the dust of the earth with his staff, and the dust turned into gnats, crawling on people and animals all over Egypt. The magicians tried to make gnats with their secret magic, but they could not. "This is the finger of God," they told Pharaoh. But Pharaoh's heart was hard, and he would not listen.

Next the Lord sent great swarms of flies into Pharaoh's palace and into all the houses of Egypt. But in the land of Goshen, where the children of Israel lived, there were no flies at all. Pharaoh said, "Go, make your offerings to your God, only do not go very far away." But as soon as the flies were gone, Pharaoh made his heart hard again, and would not let the people go.

Then a terrible sickness came upon the horses and donkeys and camels and cattle and sheep of Egypt, and the cattle of Egypt died. But not one animal belonging to the children of Israel died. Still Pharaoh would not let the people go.

Then Moses threw handfuls of soot from a furnace up into the air in front of Pharaoh. It became fine dust over all the land, and painful sores broke out on the people and the animals of Egypt. Even the magicians had sores, and could not stand before Moses. But Pharaoh would not listen.

"Tomorrow," said Moses, "the Lord will send a hailstorm, worse than any there has ever been in Egypt. Bring your animals in from the fields!" Some of Pharaoh's servants feared the word of the Lord, and brought their servants and animals indoors, but others did not. Then Moses stretched out his staff towards heaven, and the Lord sent thunder and hail, and fire flashing among the hail. The hail struck down everything in the fields, and broke every tree. Only in the land of Goshen, where the children of Israel were, there was no hail.

"I have sinned this time," Pharaoh told Moses. "The Lord is right, and I and my people are wrong. Pray to the Lord to stop the thunder and the hail, and I will let you go." So Moses went out of the city and spread out his hands to the Lord, and the thunder and hail stopped. But when Pharaoh saw that the storm was over, he made his heart hard again.

"How long will you refuse to humble yourself?" Moses and Aaron asked Pharaoh. "Let the people go, or tomorrow the Lord will bring locusts." Pharaoh's own servants begged him, "Let the people go! Don't you know that Egypt is ruined?" But Pharaoh would only let the men go, and not the children. So Moses stretched out his staff over Egypt, and an east wind blew all day and all night, and in the morning it brought the locusts. They covered the ground until it was dark with them, and they ate every plant and every fruit that the hail had left. Not one green thing was left in all the land of Egypt.

"I have sinned against the Lord your God, and against you," said Pharaoh. "Forgive me just this once, and pray that the Lord will take this away." So Moses prayed, and the Lord sent a strong west wind that blew all the locusts into the Red Sea. But Pharaoh's heart was hard, and he would not let the people go.

Then Moses stretched out his hand towards heaven, and thick darkness came over all the land of Egypt for three days. People could not see each other, and no one left their place for three days. But all the children of Israel had light in their homes.

Pharaoh called Moses. "Go," he said, "and take your children too, but leave your flocks and herds behind."

"Our animals must come with us too," said Moses. "Not one hoof shall be left behind."

But Pharaoh's heart was hard. "Get out!" he said. "Never come to see me again. The day you see my face, you will die."

"As you say," said Moses. "I will never see your face again."

Then the Lord said to Moses, "I will bring one more plague on Pharaoh and on Egypt. After that he will let you go. He will drive you out." And Moses told Pharaoh, "This is what the Lord says: At midnight every firstborn in the land of Egypt will die, from the firstborn of Pharaoh on his throne to the firstborn of the servant girl at the mill, and the firstborn of the animals too. There will be a great cry all over Egypt, such as there has never been before. But not even a dog will growl at the children of Israel. Then all your servants will come and bow down to me and say, 'Go!' And after that, I will go." And Moses left Pharaoh in great anger.

At midnight, it happened. Every firstborn in the land of Egypt died, from Pharaoh's own firstborn son to the firstborn of the prisoner in the dungeon. Pharaoh got up in the night, and all his servants and all the Egyptians, and there was a great cry in Egypt, for there was not a house where someone had not died.

That night Pharaoh called for Moses and Aaron. "Get up and go," he said, "you and the children of Israel. Go and worship the Lord, as you asked. Take your flocks and your herds, and go. And bless me too."

The Egyptians hurried the people out of the land, for they said, "We will all be dead." So the children of Israel took their dough before it had time to rise, wrapped in cloths on their shoulders. They asked the Egyptians for silver and gold jewellery and for clothing, and the Lord made the Egyptians kind to them, so that they gave them what they asked for.
