Frey was the god of sunshine and rain, and of all the good things that grow. He was the son of Njord, the god of the sea, and the brother of the goddess Freyja.

One day Frey did something he should not have done. He climbed up into Odin's high seat, Hlidskjalf. Only Odin was supposed to sit there. From that seat you can see out over the whole world.

Frey looked north, into the land of the giants. There he saw a great house, and a young woman walking up to its door. As she lifted her arm to open the door, a bright light shone from her hand, so bright that the sky and the sea and all the worlds lit up.

Her name was Gerd. She was the daughter of a giant, and she was the most beautiful woman Frey had ever seen.

Frey climbed down from the high seat. But from that moment on, he was very sad. He would not speak. He would not sleep. He would not eat or drink. Nobody dared to ask him what was wrong.

At last his father, Njord, sent for Frey's servant and friend, Skirnir. "Go and ask my son what is the matter," said Njord.

Skirnir did not want to go. He was afraid Frey would be angry with him. But he went, and he asked.

"I have seen the most beautiful girl in the world," said Frey. "And if I cannot marry her, I don't think I can live much longer. Go and ask her to marry me, Skirnir, and I will give you a great reward."

"I will go," said Skirnir. "But you must give me your sword."

Frey's sword was a magic sword. It could fight all by itself. But Frey was so much in love that he did not stop to think. He gave Skirnir his sword at once.

So Skirnir rode away to Giantland. And Gerd promised that in nine nights' time she would meet Frey at a place called Barey, and marry him there.

When Skirnir came back with the news, Frey said:

"One night is long. Two nights are long. How shall I wait for three? A whole month has often seemed shorter to me than half of this waiting!"

But he waited the nine nights, and then he and Gerd were married.

But Frey never got his sword back. Later, he had to fight a giant named Beli, and he had no sword, so he fought him with a deer's antler instead. He won that fight easily. Frey could have beaten Beli with his bare fist.

But the old stories say that a day will come, at the end of the world, when the fire giants will come riding out to fight the gods. And on that day, Frey will truly be sorry that he gave away his sword.
