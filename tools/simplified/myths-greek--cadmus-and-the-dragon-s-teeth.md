title: Cadmus and the Dragon's Teeth

Long ago, in a land by the sea, there lived a king with three sons, Cadmus, Phoenix and Cilix, and one little daughter, Europa.

One sunny day the four children were playing in a meadow by the sea. The boys ran off to chase a beautiful butterfly. Europa was tired, so she sat down among the flowers to rest.

When she opened her eyes, a bull was standing in front of her. She jumped up in fright. But then she saw that he was the most beautiful bull in the world. He was snowy white, with horns like ivory, and his breath smelled like clover. He looked at her so kindly that she stopped being afraid.

Europa hung flower crowns on his horns. She fed him grass from her hand. The bull was so happy that he danced and leaped around the meadow as lightly as a bird. Then he knelt down in front of her, as if to say, "Climb on my back, and I will give you a ride."

"Just one little ride," thought Europa. She climbed up.

The bull galloped across the meadow toward her brothers. Europa laughed and waved. "Goodbye!" her brothers called, laughing too.

But then the bull turned. He ran straight down to the beach and leaped into the sea. He swam away through the waves, faster and faster, with Europa holding tight to his horn. Her brothers ran to the shore, but it was too late. Soon the white bull was only a speck far out on the blue water. Then he was gone.

The three boys went home crying. When their father, King Agenor, heard the news, he was wild with grief and anger.

"Go and find your sister," he said. "And do not come back without her."

Their mother, Queen Telephassa, would not let her sons go alone. "If I lost you too, what would I do?" she said. Their best friend, Thasus, came with them as well.

So the five of them set off to find Europa. They walked over mountains and across rivers. They sailed over seas. Everywhere they went, they asked the same question.

"Have you seen a little girl riding on a snow-white bull?"

"A white bull with a princess on his back?" the farmers laughed. "No, we have never seen such a thing."

Years went by. The boys grew into tall young men. Their fine clothes wore out, and they dressed like poor travelers.

One day Phoenix sat down on a mossy bank. "I can't go on," he said. "We will never find her. I will build a little hut and live here."

So they helped him build a hut of branches, and the others went on. Other travelers came by, and liked the spot, and built homes next to Phoenix. Soon a whole city grew up there, and the people made Phoenix their king.

After a while, Cilix grew tired too. He stayed behind in a hut of his own, and a city grew up around him, and he became a king as well.

Then Thasus hurt his ankle and could not walk. "Go on without me," he said. He stayed, and a city grew around his hut, and he became a king too. But he never forgot Europa.

Now only Cadmus and his mother were left. The queen was old and tired. She leaned on Cadmus's arm, and they could only walk a few miles each day.

At last she said, "My dear Cadmus, I need to rest. My journey is over. You have been the best son a mother could have. When I am gone, go to Delphi, where the wise voice speaks, and ask what you should do."

She smiled at him, a bright and peaceful smile, and then she was gone. Cadmus planted flowers on her grave. Then he set off alone for Delphi.

At Delphi there was a hole in the side of the mountain. A cold wind blew out of it, and sometimes the wind spoke.

"Wise voice," said Cadmus, "where should I look for my sister?"

The wind sighed. "Seek her no more," it whispered. "Seek her no more. Follow the cow. Where the cow lies down, there is your home."

Cadmus walked away, puzzled. What cow?

Not far down the road, he saw a cow with brown and gray stripes lying by the path. As he came near, she stood up and began to walk. Cadmus followed her.

If he walked slowly, the cow walked slowly. If he ran, the cow ran too, and he could never catch her. She stopped for a bite of grass here and a drink from a stream there. But she never lay down.

Other people saw the cow and started to follow her too. They could not help it, because she was a magic cow. They talked with Cadmus as they walked, and they grew very fond of him.

"When the cow lies down," they said, "we will help you build a city there. And you will be our king."

At last they came to a lovely green plain with tall trees and a river. The cow stopped, looked around, and slowly lay down in the soft grass.

"This is my home," said Cadmus.

Everyone was hungry and thirsty. Cadmus's new friends went to fetch water from a spring under some trees. Suddenly Cadmus heard terrible screams. He ran to the trees, and there he saw a huge dragon with fiery eyes and rows and rows of sharp teeth. The dragon guarded the spring, and it had swallowed up all of Cadmus's friends.

Cadmus was so angry that he forgot to be afraid. He drew his sword and leaped right into the dragon's open mouth, so far down its throat that the teeth could not bite him. From inside, he fought the dragon until it fell down and died.

Cadmus climbed out. He was all alone again. "What shall I do now?" he cried.

A voice spoke to him. It seemed to come from the sky, or the ground, or from inside his own heart. "Cadmus, pull out the dragon's teeth and plant them in the earth."

So Cadmus pulled out the dragon's teeth. There were enough to fill a whole basket. He dug the ground with his sword and planted them like seeds.

Then a strange thing happened. Bright points of metal began to poke up out of the dirt, like little green shoots in spring. They were spearheads. Then came swords, and helmets, and faces. A whole army of soldiers grew up out of the ground. They shouted and blew their trumpets and waved their swords. They looked ready to fight the whole world.

"Throw a stone among them," said the voice.

Cadmus threw a stone. It hit one big soldier on his armor. He thought the soldier next to him had hit him, so he hit back. Soon all the soldiers were fighting each other. They fought and fought, until only five were left standing.

"Tell them to put away their swords," said the voice. "They will help you build your city."

Cadmus stepped forward like a king. "Put away your swords!" he commanded.

The five soldiers saluted and obeyed.

"Now," said Cadmus, "use those strong arms to cut stones, and help me build a city."

The soldiers grumbled. "We were made to knock cities down, not build them up." But Cadmus looked at them sternly, and they got to work. They worked hard, and soon there were streets and houses, with a home for each of them.

The last thing to build was the king's palace. But when Cadmus woke the next morning, there it was already, built of marble, with a golden dome shining in the sun. It had grown up out of the earth all by itself in the night.

"Long live King Cadmus!" shouted the five soldiers.

Cadmus walked into the great hall. At the far end, a beautiful young woman was coming toward him, wearing a crown of diamonds. His heart jumped. Was it Europa, at last?

"No, Cadmus," said the voice. "This is not your sister. This is Harmonia, a daughter of the sky. She is given to you in place of your sister, your brothers, your friend and your mother. You will find all of them in her."

So Cadmus married Harmonia, and they lived in the palace together. Before long they had a group of rosy little children, who ran to meet their father whenever he came home.

Cadmus worried that his children might have a little of the dragon's fierceness in them. So he found time every day to teach them their letters. In fact, some say Cadmus invented the ABCs, just for his children. So the next time you learn your letters, you can thank King Cadmus.
