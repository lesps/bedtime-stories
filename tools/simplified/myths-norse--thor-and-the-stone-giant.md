One day, while Thor was away fighting trolls in the east, Odin went riding into Giantland on his eight-legged horse, Sleipnir. There he met a giant named Hrungnir.

"Who is that in the golden helmet," said Hrungnir, "riding through the air and over the sea? That's a fine horse you have."

"I bet my head," said Odin, "that there's no horse as good as this in all of Giantland."

"It's a good horse," said Hrungnir, "but mine is better. My horse Golden Mane takes much longer strides!" And he was so annoyed by Odin's boasting that he jumped on Golden Mane and galloped after him, to teach him a lesson.

Odin galloped away, staying a whole hill ahead. And Hrungnir was in such a giant rage that he did not even notice where he was going, until he galloped right in through the gates of Asgard!

The gods were polite. They invited him into their hall and offered him a drink. They brought out Thor's own drinking bowls, which were very big, and Hrungnir emptied every one. And the more he drank, the more he boasted.

"I'm going to pick up this hall," he bragged, "and carry it off to Giantland! I'm going to sink Asgard into the sea and get rid of all you gods! Except for Freyja and Sif. I'll take them home with me. And I'll drink up all your ale!"

The gods soon got tired of his boasting. So they called for Thor.

In came Thor, with his hammer raised, as angry as a thunderstorm. "Who let a giant in here?" he shouted. "Who said he could drink in Valhalla?"

Hrungnir looked at Thor with very unfriendly eyes. "Odin invited me," he said. "I am his guest. You can't touch me."

"You'll be sorry you came," growled Thor.

"There's no glory in fighting a giant without his weapons," said Hrungnir. "I left my shield and my stone at home. Come and fight me properly, if you dare, at the border of Giantland, at the place called Stone Fence. Or else I'll tell everyone you're a coward."

Nobody had ever challenged Thor to a duel before, and he was not going to say no. So Hrungnir galloped home, and the news spread all over Giantland. The giants were worried. Hrungnir was the strongest of them all. If he lost, what would happen to them?

So the giants made a helper for Hrungnir, a giant out of clay. He was enormously tall, nine miles high! But they could not find a heart big enough for him, so they gave him the heart of a horse. And that heart did not stay brave for long.

Hrungnir himself was made of stone. He had a stone heart, with three sharp points. His head was stone. His shield was a huge stone slab, and for a weapon he carried a great whetstone, the kind of stone used for sharpening knives. He stood waiting for Thor at Stone Fence, holding his shield in front of him. Beside him stood the clay giant, Mokkurkalfi, shaking with fright. When he saw Thor coming, he was so frightened that he wet himself!

Thor came with his servant, Thialfi, the fastest runner in the world. Thialfi ran on ahead to Hrungnir.

"You're standing the wrong way, giant!" Thialfi called. "You're holding your shield in front of you. But Thor has seen you, and he's coming up at you from under the ground!"

So Hrungnir put his shield down on the ground and stood on top of it, to be safe from underneath. And just then he saw the lightning and heard the thunder. Here came Thor, roaring through the sky! Thor flung his hammer at Hrungnir, and Hrungnir threw his whetstone at Thor.

The hammer and the whetstone crashed together in the air. The whetstone burst into pieces. Some of the pieces fell to the ground, and that is where all the whetstone rocks in the world come from. But one piece hit Thor on the head and knocked him down flat. And Thor's hammer flew on and struck Hrungnir's stone head, and smashed it to pieces like a rock in a rockfall.

Down crashed Hrungnir, and he fell right on top of Thor, so that his great stone leg lay across Thor's neck. Meanwhile, Thialfi went after the clay giant, who toppled over with a feeble thud.

Thialfi tried to lift Hrungnir's leg off Thor, but he could not budge it. All the gods came running, and they all tried to lift it together. They could not move it at all.

Then along came Magni, Thor's son. He was only three days old! He picked up the giant's leg and tossed it off his father as if it were a twig. "I'm sorry I came so late, Father," he said. "I think I could have knocked that giant flat with my fist!"

Thor jumped up and hugged his son. "You will be a great hero one day," he said. "And I'll give you Hrungnir's horse, Golden Mane, as a present!" (Odin grumbled that Thor should have given the fine horse to his own father instead.)

But the piece of whetstone was still stuck in Thor's head. So a wise woman named Groa came to sing magic songs over him, and slowly, the stone began to come loose. Thor was so pleased that he wanted to make her happy too. So he told her some good news. "I carried your husband home from Giantland in a basket on my back," he said, "across the freezing rivers. One of his toes stuck out of the basket and froze, so I broke it off and threw it up into the sky, and now it is a star! He'll be home very soon."

Groa was so happy and excited to hear about her husband that she forgot the rest of her magic song. And so the piece of stone never came out. It is still stuck in Thor's head to this very day. That is why, in the old days, people were careful never to throw a sharpening stone across the floor. They said it would make the stone in Thor's head wiggle!
