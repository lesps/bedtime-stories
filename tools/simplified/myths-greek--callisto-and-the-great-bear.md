Callisto was a huntress who lived in the forests of Arcadia. Zeus, the king of the gods, liked her very much. That made his wife Hera jealous.

"I will take away your beauty," Hera said to Callisto. And she turned her into a bear.

Callisto fell down onto her hands and knees. Black fur spread over her arms. Her hands curled into paws with sharp claws. When she tried to speak, only a growl came out.

But inside, she was still Callisto. She was still kind, and she was still a person. She was afraid to sleep alone in the woods. Even though she was a bear now, she was afraid of the other bears. She was afraid of the wolves, too. She hid from the hunters she used to hunt with.

Years went by. Callisto had a son named Arcas, and he grew up to be a hunter too.

One day Arcas was hunting in the forest when he saw a big bear. The bear saw him too. It was his mother, and she knew him. She was so happy to see her son that she stood up and walked toward him, as if to hug him.

But Arcas did not know the bear was his mother. He was frightened. He lifted his spear to throw it.

Zeus saw what was about to happen. Quick as lightning, he scooped up both of them and lifted them into the sky. He turned them into stars. Callisto became the Great Bear, and Arcas became the Little Bear.

Now Hera was angrier than ever. She went to the old gods of the ocean. "Look up at the sky tonight," she said. "Zeus has put those two among the stars. Please, never let them come down to rest in your waters."

The ocean gods agreed. And that is why, the old Greeks said, the Great Bear and the Little Bear go round and round the North Star every night, all year long. All the other stars sink down below the edge of the sea to rest. But those two bears never do.

On a clear night, look north. You can find them. The seven bright stars of the Great Bear are sometimes called the Big Dipper. And the last star in the tail of the Little Bear is the North Star, which sailors have used for thousands of years to find their way home.
