---
title: Freyja's Necklace
adaptedFrom: norse-sources--freyja-s-necklace

---

Freyja was one of the greatest of all the goddesses, as great as Frigg, the wife of Odin.

Freyja's husband, Od, went away on long journeys, far across the world. Freyja missed him terribly. She wept for him, and her tears were not ordinary tears. They were drops of red gold. She went looking for him in faraway lands, and she gave herself a different name in every land she visited.

Freyja owned a wonderful treasure, the most beautiful necklace in the world. Its name was Brisingamen. It glittered and gleamed, and she loved it very much.

Now, the gods had a watchman named Heimdall. He lived at the top of the rainbow bridge, at the edge of the sky, guarding the way into Asgard so that no giants could sneak in. Heimdall was the perfect watchman. His teeth were made of gold. He needed less sleep than a bird. He could see a hundred miles away, by night just as well as by day. And his hearing was so sharp that he could hear the grass growing in the fields, and the wool growing on the sheep!

And then there was Loki, the trickster. Loki stole many things in his time. He stole a goat, and he stole Idun's golden apples. And once, he stole Freyja's necklace, Brisingamen.

Nobody knows exactly how he did it. But Heimdall saw, or heard, or both. And Heimdall set off after the thief.

Far out in the sea there is a lonely rock called Singastein. And that is where Heimdall caught up with Loki. Loki had changed himself into a seal, to escape through the waves. So Heimdall changed himself into a seal too. And the two seals fought among the rocks and the splashing waves, twisting and diving and wrestling, for a long time.

At last, Heimdall won. He took back the necklace from the sly trickster, and brought it home to Freyja.

There was an old poet named Ulf, who told this story in a poem. He sang it something like this:

"The famous guardian of the gods' road, wise and strong, went to Singastein against cunning Loki. And the mighty son of nine mothers won the lovely jewel first."

Heimdall was called the son of nine mothers, because the old stories say he had nine mothers, all sisters. And from that day, Heimdall and Loki were enemies.

Freyja's necklace appears in another story too. When the giant Thrym stole Thor's hammer, he said he would only give it back if Freyja would marry him. Freyja was so angry that she snorted, and the whole hall shook, and her precious necklace burst apart!

Of course, she refused to marry the giant. So it was Heimdall, once again, who had the idea that saved the day. "Let's dress Thor up as the bride," he said, "and let him wear Freyja's necklace." And so Thor went to the giant's wedding wearing a veil, and the famous necklace of Freyja around his neck.
