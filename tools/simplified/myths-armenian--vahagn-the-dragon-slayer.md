Long ago, the Armenians used to sing a song about how their hero Vahagn was born. This is how it goes:

> Heaven was giving birth,
> and earth was giving birth,
> and the purple sea was giving birth too.
> And in the sea, the little red reed
> was giving birth as well.
> Up through the reed's stem rose smoke,
> up through the reed's stem rose flame,
> and out of the flame ran
> a little golden-haired boy.
> He had hair of fire,
> he had a beard of flame,
> and his little eyes were suns.

Vahagn grew up to be the bravest of all heroes. He fought the vishaps, the great dragons, and beat them, and so people called him Vahagn the Dragon-Slayer. Some said that he was as strong as the Greek hero Herakles. Others said that he was a god, and in the old days they built temples to him, and honoured him as the god of courage.

People tell another story about Vahagn, too. They say that one bitter winter, Vahagn went to the land of Barsham, the king of Assyria, and took straw from his barns, and carried it home to Armenia. He hurried home across the sky with the straw in his arms, and as he ran, bits of straw fell out behind him, all along the way.

They are still there. On a clear, dark night you can see them, shining in a long white path across the sky. Armenians call it the Straw Thief's Way. In English we call it the Milky Way.
