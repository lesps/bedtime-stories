---
title: Telipinu, the God Who Went Away
basedOn: the Hittite myth of Telipinu, the god who vanished (clay tablets of about 1300 BC)
workId: hittite-telipinu
order: 1
---

Long ago, in the land of the Hittites, there lived a god called Telipinu. He was the god who made the crops grow and the animals thrive. When Telipinu was happy, the land was green and full of good things.

But one day, Telipinu got angry. He was so angry, and in such a hurry, that he put his right shoe on his left foot, and his left shoe on his right foot. And off he stomped. He took the grain and the good harvest with him, and he went away into the wild meadows, and lay down and fell asleep.

As soon as Telipinu had gone, everything went wrong. Mist covered the windows. Smoke filled the houses. The logs in the fireplaces smouldered and would not burn. The sheep would not look after their lambs, and the cows would not look after their calves. The barley and the wheat stopped growing. The trees and the mountain meadows dried up, and the springs ran dry. And people and gods alike began to go hungry.

The Sun God gave a great feast for all the gods. They ate, but they did not feel full. They drank, but they were still thirsty.

Then the Storm God, Telipinu's father, said, "My son Telipinu is not here. He was angry, and he has gone away, and he has taken all the good things with him."

So the gods went out to look for him. The Sun God sent out the swift eagle. "Search the high mountains," he said, "and the deep valleys, and the blue waves of the sea." The eagle flew everywhere, but he could not find Telipinu.

Then the Storm God went looking himself. He came to the gate of a town and knocked on it with his hammer, so hard that his hammer broke. But he did not find Telipinu either. So he sat down to rest.

Then the old Mother Goddess, Hannahanna, said, "Send the bee."

"The bee?" said the Storm God. "The bee is tiny, and its wings are tiny. The great gods have looked everywhere, and none of us could find him. How could a little bee find him?"

But Hannahanna called the bee. "Go and look for Telipinu," she told it. "When you find him, sting him on his hands and his feet, to make him get up. Then wipe his eyes and his feet with your wax, and bring him back to me."

So the little bee flew off. It searched the high mountains and the deep valleys and the blue waters, until its strength was nearly gone. And at last, in a meadow in a little wood, it found Telipinu fast asleep.

The bee stung Telipinu on his hands and his feet, and Telipinu jumped up. But he was angrier than ever. "I was sleeping in my anger!" he roared. "Why did you wake me up?" And in his rage he made things even worse than before.

Then the gods called for Kamrusepa, the goddess of magic and healing. She knew how to soothe away anger. She burned sweet-smelling wood, and she spoke gentle spells. "Let Telipinu's anger go," she said. "Let his rage go. Let his bad temper go."

And Telipinu's anger left him. The anger, the rage and the bad temper all went down into the dark earth. There they were locked away in great bronze pots, with lids of lead and bolts of iron, so that they could never come back up again.

At last Telipinu came home, and he cared for his land again. The mist left the windows. The smoke left the houses. The fires burned brightly in the fireplaces. The sheep looked after their lambs, and the cows looked after their calves, and mothers looked after their children. The barley and wheat grew tall again.

And in front of Telipinu's house there stood an evergreen tree. From it hung a bag made of sheepskin, and inside it were all the good things of the world: plenty of food and drink, flocks of sheep and herds of cattle, long life and many children.
