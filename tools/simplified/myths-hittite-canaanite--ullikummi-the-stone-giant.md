---
title: Ullikummi, the Stone Giant
basedOn: the Hurrian Song of Ullikummi, as the Hittites wrote it down (clay tablets of about 1300 BC)
workId: hurrian-ullikummi
order: 3
---

Long ago, the old god Kumarbi had been king of the gods. But Teshub, the Storm God, had taken his throne from him. And Kumarbi sat and brooded, and thought about how to get his throne back.

"I will make a rival for the Storm God," said Kumarbi, "someone he cannot beat."

So Kumarbi went to a great rock, as big as a mountain, and from that rock a son was born to him, a child made all of stone. Kumarbi named him Ullikummi.

"Grow, my son," said Kumarbi. "Grow tall, and strong, and go up to heaven, and knock the Storm God off his throne."

But Kumarbi knew that if the gods saw the stone child, they would destroy him while he was still small. So he hid him. He had Ullikummi carried far away, down under the sea, to Upelluri. Upelluri was a giant who stood in the sea, holding up the heavens and the earth on his shoulders. And they set the stone child on Upelluri's right shoulder.

There Ullikummi grew. Every day he grew taller, and every month he grew taller still. He grew up out of the deep sea. He grew and grew, until he stood in the sea like a great stone tower, as high as the sky. He reached right up to the gates of heaven, where the gods lived.

The Sun God saw him first, and he hurried to tell the Storm God. The Storm God climbed a mountain with his sister Shaushka, and they looked out at the stone giant standing in the sea. When the Storm God saw how huge Ullikummi was, he sat down on the ground and wept.

Shaushka said, "I will deal with him." She went down to the seashore, and she sang to the giant, and played sweet music, to charm him. But Ullikummi was made of stone. He could not hear her songs, and he could not see her beauty. So the music did nothing at all.

Then the Storm God rode out in his chariot, with thunder and storm and rain, and fought the stone giant. But it was no use. Ullikummi pushed the gods back, and back, right up to the gates of the Storm God's own city.

At last the Storm God went to Ea, the wisest of all the gods, who lived in the deep waters. "Help us," he said.

So Ea went down to Upelluri, the giant who holds up the world. "Upelluri," he said, "don't you know what is standing on your shoulder?"

"When they built heaven and earth on top of me," said Upelluri, "I didn't notice. When they cut heaven and earth apart, I didn't notice. But now my right shoulder hurts a little. I don't know who it is that is standing there."

Ea looked, and there was Ullikummi, standing on Upelluri's shoulder.

Then Ea called for the old copper cutting-blade that was kept in the storehouse of the gods. Long ago, at the very beginning, the gods had used it to cut the heavens apart from the earth. Ea took that ancient blade, and with it he cut Ullikummi's feet away from Upelluri's shoulder. And the stone giant's strength was gone.

"Now go and fight him again," said Ea to the gods.

So the Storm God climbed into his chariot once more, and rode out to fight the stone giant. Here the old tablets are broken, and the end of the story is lost. But in the stories the Hittites told, the Storm God stays king of the gods.
