Long ago, a Greek traveller called Herodotus visited Egypt, and the priests of the city of Heliopolis told him about a wonderful sacred bird called the phoenix.

Herodotus never saw the phoenix himself, except in a painting. That was because it came to Egypt only once every five hundred years, when its father died.

If the phoenix looked like the painting, it was about the size and shape of an eagle. Some of its feathers were golden, and some of them were red.

The priests told Herodotus that the phoenix lived far away in Arabia. And when its father died, it carried him all the way to Egypt, to bury him in the temple of the Sun at Heliopolis.

This is how it did it. First it made an egg out of myrrh, a sweet-smelling gum that comes from trees, as big as it could carry. Then it practised carrying the egg, to make sure it was strong enough. When it was ready, it hollowed out the egg and laid its father inside it. Then it filled up the hole with more myrrh, so that the egg weighed exactly the same as before. And it carried the whole egg to Egypt, and buried it in the temple of the Sun.

That is what the priests said. But Herodotus wrote that he could not quite believe the story.
