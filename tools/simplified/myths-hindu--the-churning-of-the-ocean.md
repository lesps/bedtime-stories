---
title: The Churning of the Ocean
adaptedFrom: ganguli-mahabharata--the-churning-of-the-ocean

namesNote: From the Mahabharata. The asuras were the enemies of the gods. Amrita is the drink that lets the gods live for ever. Shiva is called Nilakantha, "the Blue-Throated", because of the poison he swallowed.
---

Long ago, the gods met on the golden peak of Mount Meru. They wanted amrita, the drink that would let them live for ever. Then Vishnu said to Brahma, "Let the gods and the asuras churn the Ocean together. Then the amrita will come up, and many healing herbs and jewels too."

So the gods tried to pull up Mount Mandara, a mountain so high that it reached far up into the sky, to use as a churning stick. But they could not move it. So Vishnu asked the great serpent Ananta to do it, and Ananta pulled it up, with all its forests and all the creatures living in them, and the gods carried it to the shore of the Ocean. "O Ocean," they said, "we have come to churn your waters for amrita."

"Very well," said the Ocean, "as long as I get a share of it. I can bear being stirred up by the mountain."

Then the gods asked the king of the tortoises to hold the mountain on his back, and he agreed. So the mountain stood on the tortoise's back, and they wound the great snake Vasuki around it for a rope. The asuras held Vasuki by his head, and the gods held him by his tail, and they pulled back and forth, back and forth, and began to churn the sea.

As Vasuki was pulled, black smoke and flames came out of his mouth and turned into clouds full of lightning, and rain poured down and cooled the tired gods, and flowers fell on them from the trees on the whirling mountain. From deep in the sea came a great roar like thunder. The mountain's trees crashed together and caught fire, and the fire spread, until Indra put it out with heavy rain.

As they churned, the juices of the mountain's trees and herbs ran into the sea, and the milky water turned into butter. But still no amrita came. The tired gods went to Brahma. "We have no strength left," they said. Brahma asked Vishnu to help them, and Vishnu gave them new strength. "Put the mountain back in place," he said, "and churn again."

So they churned again. Out of the sea rose the gentle Moon, with his thousand rays. Then came Lakshmi, the goddess of good fortune, dressed in white; then the sacred drink Soma; then a white horse; and then the shining jewel Kaustubha, which Vishnu wears on his chest. Then up rose Dhanvantari, the doctor of the gods, holding a white pot full of amrita! "It's ours!" shouted the asuras.

Next came the huge elephant Airavata, with two pairs of white tusks, and Indra took him. But they kept on churning, and at last a terrible poison called Kalakuta came up. It spread over the earth, blazing like fire and smoke, and its smell made all three worlds faint. Then Brahma asked Shiva for help, and Shiva swallowed the poison to save the world. He held it in his throat, and that is why he is called Nilakantha, the Blue-Throated.

When the asuras saw all these wonders, they gave up hope, and got ready to fight the gods for Lakshmi and the amrita. Then Vishnu used his power of illusion, and took the form of a beautiful woman. The asuras were so charmed by her that they forgot everything else, and put the amrita into her hands. And Vishnu gave the amrita to the gods, and they drank it with joy.

But a demon called Rahu had disguised himself as a god, and he drank some too. The amrita had only got as far as his throat when the Sun and the Moon saw him and told the gods. At once Vishnu threw his discus and cut off his head. Rahu's head, as big as a mountain top, flew up into the sky, shouting terribly. Ever since then, Rahu's head has had a quarrel with the Sun and the Moon, and now and then it swallows them. That is what makes an eclipse.
