---
title: How People Were Made from Clay
basedOn: the Babylonian poem of Atrahasis (clay tablets of about 1700 BC)
workId: babylonian-atrahasis-creation
order: 2
---

Long ago, before there were any people, the gods had to do all the work themselves.

The three greatest gods drew lots to share out the world. Anu took the sky. Enlil took the earth. And Enki, the wise god, took the deep fresh waters under the ground.

But the younger gods had to do the hard work. They dug the beds of the great rivers, the Tigris and the Euphrates. They dug canals to bring water to the fields. They carried baskets of earth on their heads, and they dug and dug, for years and years.

At last the younger gods were too tired to go on. "This work is too hard!" they said. "Let us go to Enlil and tell him so." So they set fire to their spades and their baskets, and in the middle of the night they marched to Enlil's house and surrounded it, shouting.

Enlil's servant woke him up. "My lord, your house is surrounded!" Enlil was frightened, and he sent for Anu and Enki.

"Why have you done this?" the great gods asked the younger gods.

"Every one of us has had enough," said the younger gods. "The work is too heavy. It is killing us."

Then Enki, the wise god, had an idea. "The birth goddess is here," he said. "Let her make people, and the people can do the work instead of the gods."

So they called the birth goddess, Mami, who was also called Nintu. "Will you make people," they asked her, "to carry the load for the gods?"

"I cannot do it alone," said Mami. "But with Enki's help, I can."

So Enki made a plan. First, there was a holy bath, to make everything pure. Then one of the gods, a clever god called We, gave up his life. Mami mixed his flesh and blood into clay, so that god and clay were mixed together. And from the god's spirit came a beat, like the beat of a drum, so that people would always remember that there was a little of a god in them. Then all the younger gods spat on the clay.

Mami took the clay and pinched off fourteen pieces. She put seven pieces on the right, and seven pieces on the left, with a brick between them. And with the help of the birth goddesses, she made seven men and seven women.

When the gods saw what Mami had done, they were overjoyed, and they kissed her feet. "Before, we called you Mami," they said. "But from now on, your name shall be Lady of All the Gods."

And the people took picks and spades, and they dug new canals and kept the rivers clear, and they grew food for themselves and for the gods. And the younger gods could rest at last.
