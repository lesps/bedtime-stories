Apollo was the god of the sun, music and poetry. He was also a great archer. One day he shot a huge, terrible serpent called the Python with his golden arrows, and he was very proud of himself.

Then he saw Eros, the little god of love, playing with his own small bow and arrows.

"What are you doing with a bow, little boy?" laughed Apollo. "Leave those for real archers, like me."

Eros did not like being laughed at. "Your arrows may hit anything you like, Apollo," he said. "But mine can hit you."

Eros had two kinds of arrows. A golden arrow made you fall in love. A dull arrow made of lead made you run away from love. Eros flew up onto a mountain. He shot a golden arrow at Apollo. Then he shot a lead arrow at a nymph named Daphne.

Daphne was the daughter of a river god. She loved the woods. She liked to run free and hunt with her bow, with her hair blowing in the wind. She did not want to marry anyone.

"Father," she had said, "please let me stay free forever, like the goddess Artemis."

The moment the golden arrow struck him, Apollo saw Daphne and loved her. The moment the lead arrow struck her, Daphne saw Apollo and wanted to run away.

"Wait!" called Apollo. "Don't run! I am not your enemy. I am Apollo, the god of the sun!"

But Daphne did not want to wait. She ran through the trees as fast as the wind. Apollo ran after her. He was fast, but because of the arrow, he would not give up. He came closer and closer, until she could feel his breath on her hair.

Daphne was so tired she could hardly run another step. She saw her father's river ahead. "Father, help me!" she cried. "Change me, so I can stay free!"

As soon as she spoke, her feet grew heavy and stuck to the ground. They turned into roots. Her skin turned into soft bark. Her arms turned into branches, and her hair turned into green leaves. Daphne had become a laurel tree.

Apollo stopped. He put his hand on the bark, and he could feel her heart still beating inside the tree.

He was sad, but he understood. "You will not be my wife," he said. "But you will be my tree. I will wear your leaves on my head. When people win races and contests, they will wear crowns of your leaves. And your leaves will stay green all year round."

The laurel tree bowed its branches, as if to nod. And to this day, the laurel stays green all year, and a crown of laurel leaves is a sign of a winner.
