adaptedFrom: bharati-krishna--krishna-the-butter-thief

namesNote: Bharati, a teacher of devotion to Krishna, wrote his life of Krishna in New York; the same stories are told in the Bhagavata Purana. The gopis are the women who look after the cows and make the butter; Narada is a wandering sage.
---

When Krishna was born, he was carried away in secret to a village of cowherds, where the cowherd Nanda and his wife Yasoda brought him up as their own son. His brother, Balarama, was there too.

When Krishna was a year old, Nanda held a great feast to give the two boys their names, and many guests came. A wise man who read the stars looked at the baby, and he bowed down at the baby's feet. "This child will help everyone who is in trouble," he said, "and he will bring blessings wherever he goes. You don't know who he is. But I am glad I have seen him."

The little baby only smiled, and held out his arms for his mother. Yasoda proudly laid him down beside a cart full of brass pots and food, and turned to greet her guests. And then, with one kick of his tiny foot, the baby turned the whole cart over, and the pots smashed on the ground! Everyone was amazed at how strong he was.

Another day, Yasoda was sitting with Krishna on her lap, when all at once he grew so heavy that she felt as if the whole world were resting on her knees. Her knees gave way, and he slipped to the ground. Then a whirlwind came, like a storm of dust, and carried the baby high up into the air. But soon he floated gently down again, and landed on his feet beside his mother.

Yasoda snatched him up and kissed him again and again. Then the baby opened his mouth, and inside it she saw the whole universe!

In that moment, Yasoda understood that her little son was God, who made everything. She understood that the whirlwind had been a demon, sent by the wicked king Kansa to carry her child away, and that her son was far stronger than any demon. And she bowed down to him.

But the baby only smiled. And the next moment, Yasoda saw just her own little boy again. For how could a mother remember for long that her own baby was the Lord of the universe?

As Krishna grew, he was very naughty and full of mischief. The gopis kept shining rows of golden butter, and Krishna loved to steal it, and feed it to the cheeky monkeys who crowded around to help him with his tricks.

One day, Krishna was hungry and cross, because his mother didn't come to feed him when he cried. So he ran in among the pots and churns where she had been making butter, and knocked them all over, so that they broke, and he shared the sweet butter with the pets who followed him everywhere.

When Yasoda heard what he had done, she decided to punish him by tying him to a heavy wooden stand with a rope. But the rope was two inches too short. So she found another rope and tied it on, and still it was two inches too short. She added rope after rope, but it was always two inches too short. Yasoda stared at her son, frightened and amazed. What kind of wonder was this?

When Krishna saw how worried his mother looked, he let her tie him up.

So Krishna stood there for a while in disgrace, tied to the heavy stand. Then he noticed two huge arjuna trees nearby. He walked over to them, pulling the stand behind him, and stood between the two trees. He wound the rope around them, and pulled them up by their roots.

Down came the trees with a mighty crash. And out of them rose two shining spirits, who bowed to Krishna, and prayed to him, and vanished in a cloud of light.

For long ago, the wise sage Narada had walked past two young men who were playing in a stream. The young men did not greet him politely. Instead they laughed at him, and called out rude things. So Narada prayed that they would be born again as trees, and remember how rude they had been. But he also prayed that when God came to the earth, he would set them free. And now Krishna had done it, and their souls rose up to heaven.

Krishna only stood there and smiled. And everyone wondered how the trees had fallen, and how the child had not been hurt when they came crashing down beside him.
