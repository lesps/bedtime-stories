---
title: Isis, Horus and the Seven Scorpions
adaptedFrom: budge-legends--isis-and-horus-in-the-marshes
order: 4
namesNote: Both parts come from the Metternich Stela, a stone carved with spells against scorpion stings. Budge spells Serqet as Serq.
---

Long ago, after the wicked god Set had killed the good king Osiris, Set shut up Osiris's wife, the goddess Isis, in his house as a prisoner. And Isis had a little baby son, called Horus.

One day, Thoth, the wise god, came to Isis. "Isis," he said, "listen to my advice. Take your son and hide with him. One day he will grow up strong, and sit on his father's throne."

So one evening Isis escaped from Set's house. And seven scorpions came with her to protect her. Two of them, Tefen and Befen, walked behind her. Two walked beside her. And three of them went in front, to find the way.

"Don't speak to anyone," Isis told them, "and keep your faces turned to the ground, so that you can show me the way." And the scorpions led her to the edge of the papyrus marshes, to a town where people lived.

There Isis came to the house of a rich lady called Usert. But when Usert saw Isis coming along the road with seven scorpions, she was frightened, and she shut her door in Isis's face.

So Isis went on, and a poor woman opened her door and let Isis in.

But the scorpions were angry with the rich lady. They all gave their poison to Tefen, and Tefen crept in under Usert's door and stung her little son. Then a fire broke out in Usert's house, and there was no water to put it out. But the sky sent rain, even though it was not the rainy season.

Usert ran through the town crying for help, because she did not know whether her son would live. But nobody came.

Isis felt sorry for the little boy, for it was not his fault. So she called out, "Come to me! Come to me! I know words of power that can drive away death!"

And Isis laid her hands on the child, and spoke to the poison of each of the seven scorpions. "Poison of Tefen, come out and fall on the ground! Poison of Befen, come out! I am Isis, the lady of magic. Listen to me, every creature that stings, and fall down!" And the child lived, and the poison died.

The fire went out, and the sky was pleased with Isis. And Usert brought all her riches and gave them to the poor woman who had opened her door to Isis. For Usert was sorry that she had shut her own door.

After that, Isis hid with her baby in the papyrus marshes. Every day she went out to find food for him, and she left him hidden in a nest of papyrus plants.

But one day, when she came back, she found Horus lying still. He was not breathing, and his body was limp. A scorpion had stung him.

Isis cried out in sorrow. "My son, my son! I have no one to help me. My father and my mother are gone, and my husband lies in his coffin." She called out to the people who lived in the marshes, and they came out of their houses and wept with her. But nobody knew how to make the child live again.

Then a wise woman came. "Don't be afraid, mother," she said. "Set cannot come into this place. A scorpion has stung the child, but he will live for his mother."

Isis bent close to Horus's mouth, to feel whether he was breathing. She found the poison in his wound. And she held him in her arms and cried, "Horus has been stung, O Ra! Your son has been stung, Osiris!"

Then her sister Nephthys came, weeping, and the scorpion goddess Serqet came too. "Pray to heaven," they said. "Ask the sailors of Ra to stop their boat."

So Isis called up to heaven, to the boat of the sun, which sails across the sky every day. And the sun stopped still in the sky, and did not move. And down from the sun's boat came Thoth, the wise god, who knows all the words of power.

"What is it, Isis?" said Thoth. "Don't be afraid. I have come from heaven to heal your child."

And Thoth said his words of power. "Wake up, Horus! You are protected. Make your mother's heart happy. Poison, come out and fall to the ground! The boat of the sun stands still, and will not move on, until Horus is healed for his mother Isis."

And the poison came out, and Horus woke up and lived.

Then Thoth said, "Now the boat of the sun can sail on again." And he went back up to the sun's boat, and the sun sailed on across the sky. And Isis took her son Horus in her arms, safe and well. And Horus grew up to be strong, and one day he sat on his father's throne.
