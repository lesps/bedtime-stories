title: Adapa and the South Wind

Long ago, when gods and people still visited each other, there lived a fisherman called Adapa. He was a son of Ea, the god of the deep waters, and he fished in the sea for Ea's house.

One day, while Adapa was out fishing, the water lay calm all around him. But then the South Wind began to blow. The South Wind was like a great bird, and it pushed Adapa under the water, down to the home of the fish.

Adapa was angry. "South Wind!" he cried. "You have been cruel to me. I will break your wings!" And even as he spoke, the wings of the South Wind were broken. For seven days, the South Wind did not blow across the land.

Up in heaven, Anu, the god of the sky, noticed. "Why has the South Wind not blown for seven days?" he asked his messenger, Ilabrat.

"My lord," said Ilabrat, "Adapa, the son of Ea, has broken the wings of the South Wind."

Anu was furious. "Bring Adapa to me!" he ordered.

But Ea took care of Adapa. "You must go up to Anu," he told him, "but do as I say. Put on clothes of mourning, as if someone had died. At the gate of heaven stand two gods, Tammuz and Gishzida. They will ask you, 'Why are you dressed for mourning?' And you must say, 'Two gods have gone away from the earth, and that is why I am dressed like this.' They will ask, 'Who are the two gods?' Then Tammuz and Gishzida will look at each other, for they will know you mean them. And they will say a kind word for you to Anu.

"When you come before Anu, they will offer you the food of death. Don't eat it. They will offer you the water of death. Don't drink it. They will offer you a robe. Put it on. They will offer you oil. Rub it on yourself. Don't forget what I have told you."

Then Anu's messenger came for Adapa, and Ea let him go. And everything happened just as Ea had said.

When Adapa came to the gate of heaven, Tammuz and Gishzida saw him. "Why are you dressed like this?" they cried. "Who are you mourning for?"

"Two gods have gone away from the earth," said Adapa, "and that is why I am dressed for mourning."

"Who are the two gods who have gone away from the earth?"

Tammuz and Gishzida looked at each other, and they began to weep. "Adapa," they said, "go in to King Anu."

When Anu saw Adapa, he called out, "Come here, Adapa! Why did you break the wings of the South Wind?"

"My lord," said Adapa, "I was fishing in the sea for the house of my lord Ea. The water was calm all around me, when the South Wind blew, and pushed me down to the home of the fish. And in my anger, I broke its wings."

Then Tammuz and Gishzida spoke up for Adapa, and Anu was no longer angry. But he was surprised. "Why did Ea let a man of the earth see the secrets of heaven and earth?" he said. "He has made him great, and given him fame. Now what shall we give him? Bring him the food of life, so that he may eat it."

So they brought Adapa the food of life, but he did not eat it. They brought him the water of life, but he did not drink it. They brought him a robe, and he put it on. They brought him oil, and he rubbed it on himself.

Anu looked at him sadly. "Come, Adapa," he said. "Why didn't you eat and drink? Now you cannot live for ever."

"Ea, my lord, told me not to eat and not to drink," said Adapa. For he did not know that he had been tricked.

So Adapa went back down to the earth. And what happened to him after that, nobody knows, because here the old tablet comes to an end.
