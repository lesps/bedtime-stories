---
title: Jūratė and Kastytis
basedOn: the Lithuanian legend of the amber castle, first written down by Liudvikas Adomas Jucevičius in 1842 and made famous by Maironis's ballad (1920); a story told by writers more than by folk tradition
workId: lithuanian-jurate
order: 2
---

Deep under the Baltic Sea, long ago, there stood a palace made all of amber. Its walls glowed golden in the green water, and in it lived Jūratė, the goddess of the sea.

Not far away, on the shore of Lithuania, there lived a young fisherman called Kastytis. Some say he came from the village where the little river Šventoji runs into the sea. Every day he rowed out over the waves and cast his nets.

One day Jūratė rose up through the water and saw Kastytis in his boat. And the goddess of the sea fell in love with the young fisherman. She took him down with her to her amber palace under the sea, and there they lived together, and they were very happy.

But high above the clouds lived Perkūnas, the god of thunder, the mightiest of all the gods. And when he found out that a goddess had fallen in love with a man, a mortal fisherman, he was furious.

Perkūnas raised his hand and hurled down a great bolt of lightning into the sea. It struck the amber palace, and the palace shattered into thousands and thousands of pieces.

What happened to the two lovers? Some say that Kastytis was lost in the storm, and that Jūratė mourns for him still. Others say that Perkūnas chained Jūratė among the ruins of her palace, at the bottom of the sea, for ever.

But the pieces of the amber palace are still there, scattered all over the sea floor. And after every great storm on the Baltic, the waves wash little pieces of amber up onto the beaches of Lithuania, where people walk along the sand to find them. Some people say that these are not pieces of the palace at all, but Jūratė's tears, still falling for Kastytis.

And on stormy nights, when the wind howls over the sea, they say you can hear her weeping.
