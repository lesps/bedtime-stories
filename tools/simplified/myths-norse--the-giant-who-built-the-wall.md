title: The Giant Who Built the Wall

When the gods were building their home in Asgard, a builder came to see them.

"I will build you a wall," he said, "so high and so strong that no giant will ever be able to get over it. And I will finish it in three half-years."

"What do you want for your pay?" asked the gods.

"I want the goddess Freyja to be my wife," said the builder. "And I want the sun and the moon as well."

The gods talked it over. They did not want to give away Freyja, or the sun and the moon either. But they wanted that wall. So they made a tricky deal.

"You may have what you ask," they said, "but only if you finish the whole wall in one winter, all by yourself, with no one to help you. If any of it is unfinished on the first day of summer, you get nothing."

"Very well," said the builder. "But may I use my horse, Svadilfari?"

Loki thought one horse could not make any difference. "Let him have his horse," Loki said. So the gods agreed, and they all swore solemn oaths to keep the bargain.

On the first day of winter, the builder began. All day he built. All night his horse dragged enormous stones from far away. The gods could hardly believe how big the stones were. And the horse did twice as much work as his master!

The winter went on, and the wall rose higher and higher. With three days left until summer, there was only the gate left to build.

Now the gods were worried. They sat down together. "Who told us to make this deal?" they asked each other. "Who said we should give Freyja away to Giantland, and take the sun and moon out of the sky, so the world goes dark?"

Everyone looked at Loki. It was always Loki.

"Fix this," the gods told him, "or you will be very sorry."

Loki was scared. "I'll fix it," he promised. "Whatever it costs me."

That very night, as the builder and his horse went out to fetch more stones, a pretty mare came trotting out of the woods and gave a loud, friendly whinny. It was Loki, who had turned himself into a horse!

Svadilfari took one look at the mare, broke free of his harness and galloped after her into the woods. The builder ran after his horse, shouting. All night long they ran around the forest, and no stones were brought at all.

In the morning, the builder saw that he could never finish in time. He was so angry that he swelled up to his real size. He was not a man at all. He was a mountain giant!

Now that the gods knew he was a giant, they called for Thor, who had just come home from fighting trolls in the east. Thor came striding up, swinging his hammer, Mjollnir. And that was the end of the giant builder. He got no sun, no moon and no Freyja.

So the gods got their wonderful wall. And a while later, Loki came home leading a little grey foal. The foal had eight legs! His name was Sleipnir, and he grew up to be the fastest horse in all the worlds. He became Odin's horse, and he could gallop over land and sea and through the air.
