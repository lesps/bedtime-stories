---
title: Abraham and the Idols
adaptedFrom: ginzberg-legends--abraham-and-the-idols
order: 1
namesNote: In the Bible, Abraham is the first father of the Jewish people. This legend tells how he turned away from his father's idols.
---

Long ago, Abraham's father Terah made idols out of wood and stone, and worshipped them. Abraham asked his father, "Who is the God who made heaven and earth, and all the people in it?"

Terah took Abraham into a great hall. In it stood twelve big idols, and lots and lots of little ones. "Here they are," said Terah. "These are the gods who made everything you see on the earth. They made me, and you, and everybody." And he bowed down to his gods, and then he left the hall with his son.

Abraham went to his mother. "Father has shown me the gods who made heaven and earth and everybody," he said. "So please, quickly fetch a young goat from the flock, and cook a tasty meal with it. I will take it to my father's gods. Perhaps they will be pleased with me."

His mother did as he asked. But when Abraham set the meal in front of the gods, he saw that they had no voices, and could not hear, and could not move. Not one of them reached out a hand to eat.

Abraham laughed at them. "Perhaps you don't like my cooking," he said. "Or perhaps there isn't enough for you! Tomorrow I will bring you a better meal, and more of it, and we shall see what happens."

So the next day he brought them an even better meal. But the gods sat there just as before, silent and still. And Abraham cried out, "How sad it is that my father and his people serve these idols of wood and stone! They cannot eat, or smell, or hear, or speak. They have mouths, but cannot talk. They have eyes, but cannot see. They have ears, but cannot hear. They have hands, but cannot feel. They have legs, but cannot walk!"

Then Abraham took a hatchet and smashed all his father's gods. When he had finished, he put the hatchet into the hands of the biggest god of all, and went out.

Terah heard the crash of the hatchet on the stone, and he ran to the hall. When he saw what had happened, he hurried after Abraham. "What is this mischief you have done to my gods?" he cried.

"I put a tasty meal in front of them," said Abraham. "And when I came near, they all reached out to grab the food, before the big one had a chance to eat. So the big one got angry with them, and took the hatchet, and smashed them all. Look, he's still got the hatchet in his hands!"

"You're lying to me!" shouted Terah. "These gods don't have any life, or any power, to do what you say. They're only wood and stone! I made them myself! It was you who put the hatchet in the big god's hands, and now you say he smashed them all."

"Then why do you serve these idols," said Abraham, "if they have no power to do anything? Can they save you? Can they hear you when you pray to them?" And he begged his father to change his ways and stop worshipping idols.

Then Abraham jumped up, took the hatchet from the big idol, smashed that one too, and ran away.
