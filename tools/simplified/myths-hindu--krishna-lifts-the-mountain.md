When the rainy season was over and the autumn came, Krishna went home to his village one day, and found all the cowherds busy getting ready for a great festival.

"What festival is this?" Krishna asked. "Why are you all so happy?"

"It is the festival of Indra, the king of the gods," said Nanda, the leader of the cowherds. "Indra is the lord of the clouds. He sends the rain that makes the grass grow, so that our cows have calves and give us milk. That is why all the kings make offerings to Indra at the end of the rainy season, and so do we."

"But we aren't farmers, Father," said Krishna, "and we aren't merchants. We live in the forests, and the cows are our gods. People should honour the thing that looks after them. We should honour the mountains and the cows, not Indra. What have we to do with Indra? So let us make offerings to the mountain Govardhana. Let us collect milk from everyone, and give a feast to the priests and to everyone who wants to come. And then let us put garlands of autumn flowers on the cows, and walk all the way around them."

When the cowherds heard this, their faces shone. "You are right," they said. "We will do just what you say."

So the people of the village made offerings to the mountain of curds and milk and other food, and they fed hundreds and thousands of priests and guests, and they walked around their cows and bulls. And Krishna stood on top of the mountain and said, "I am the mountain," and ate the food that the cowherds had brought. But at the same time, in his own shape, he climbed the mountain with the other cowherds and worshipped his other self. Then the mountain-Krishna promised them many blessings and vanished, and everyone went home.

But Indra, the king of the gods, was furious that he had not been given his offerings. He called the great storm clouds. "The foolish cowherds have stopped my offerings," he said. "Go and drown their cattle with wind and rain! I will ride my elephant, and make the storm stronger."

So the clouds came down in a terrible storm. The rain poured down, day and night, until the earth and the sky seemed to melt into one, and there was water everywhere. The cows huddled together, and some covered their calves with their bodies. The trembling calves looked at their mothers, as if they were begging Krishna to help them.

When Krishna saw the whole village so frightened, he thought, "This is Indra's doing, because he did not get his offerings. I must protect these cowherds. I will lift up this mountain and hold it over them like an umbrella."

So Krishna picked up the mountain Govardhana with one hand, as if it were a toy. "Look, the mountain is up in the air!" he called. "Come underneath it quickly, and you will be safe from the storm. Don't be afraid that it will fall."

All the people came under the mountain, with their cattle and their wagons and all their things, and Krishna held the mountain steadily over their heads. The cowherds looked at him in wonder and delight, and sang his praises.

For seven days and seven nights, Indra's clouds poured rain on the village, but the people stayed safe under the mountain. At last Indra gave up, and told the clouds to stop. The sky grew clear again, and everyone came out and went back to their homes. And Krishna put the great mountain Govardhana back in its place.

Afterwards, Indra wanted to see Krishna. He rode down on his huge elephant, Airavata, and found Krishna on the mountain, looking after the cows, with the cowherd boys all around him. And above Krishna's head, Indra saw Garuda, the king of the birds, invisible to people, spreading his wings to shade him.

Indra climbed down from his elephant. "Krishna," he said, "I was angry when my offerings were stopped, and I sent the clouds to flood your village. But by holding up the mountain, you saved the cows, and I am very pleased with you. The cows themselves have asked me to honour you. So I make you Upendra, the younger Indra. And because you are the lord of the cows, you will be called Govinda."

Then Indra took a jar of holy water from his elephant, and poured it over Krishna, as a king is crowned. And as he did, the cows let their milk flow out over the whole earth.
