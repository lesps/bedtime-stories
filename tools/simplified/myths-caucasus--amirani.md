---
title: Amirani, the Chained Giant
basedOn: the Georgian legend of Amirani, told in the mountains of the Caucasus in many versions and written down by folklorists from the nineteenth century on
workId: georgian-amirani
order: 2
---

Long ago, in the high mountains of Georgia, there lived Dali, the goddess of the hunt. She watched over the wild goats and deer of the mountains, and she had long golden hair that shone like the sun.

Dali had a son called Amirani. Even as a baby he was stronger than any man, and he grew up to be a giant of a hero, with the strength of a hundred men.

Amirani used his strength to fight the devis, the terrible many-headed giants who lived in the mountains and did harm to people. He fought them, one after another, and beat them all.

Once, a great dragon swallowed Amirani whole. But Amirani was not afraid. He took out his knife and cut his way out through the dragon's side, and climbed out again into the daylight.

But Amirani became proud. He was so strong that he thought he could do anything. At last he became so proud that he even challenged God. He boasted that he was as strong as God himself, and that no one in heaven or on earth could beat him.

So God punished Amirani. He chained him with an iron chain to an iron post, deep inside a cave in the high mountains of the Caucasus. And there Amirani has stayed ever since.

But Amirani was not alone. With him in the cave was his faithful dog, a dog with wings. All year long, the dog licked and licked at Amirani's chain. And every year the chain grew thinner and thinner, until it was almost worn through.

But every year, on one special day, just before Easter, the blacksmiths of Georgia went to their forges, and struck their anvils with their hammers. Clang! Clang! Clang! And at the sound of the hammers, Amirani's chain grew strong again, as thick as it was before.

And so it goes on, year after year. Some people say that if you climb high enough in the mountains, you can still hear Amirani pulling at his chain, deep inside the mountain. And some people say that one day, the chain will break, and Amirani will come out again.
