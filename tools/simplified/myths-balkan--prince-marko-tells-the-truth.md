Long ago, the great Tsar Dushan of Serbia died. And four armies gathered on the wide field of Kosovo, near the white church of Samodrezha.

The first army belonged to King Vukashin, who was Prince Marko's father. The second belonged to Marko's uncle Uglyesha, and the third to his uncle Goyko. And the fourth belonged to young Urosh, the son of Tsar Dushan.

The three grown men were quarrelling over who should be the new Tsar, and they were so angry that they were ready to fight. "The empire was left to me!" said King Vukashin. "No, it's mine!" cried Goyko. "You're both wrong," said Uglyesha. "It's mine!" But young Urosh said nothing at all, because he was too shy to speak in front of his proud elders.

None of them knew who the old Tsar had really chosen. So each of them sent a messenger to Archdeacon Nedelyko in the city of Prizren. The Archdeacon had been with the Tsar when he died, and he looked after the Tsar's papers.

The messengers found the Archdeacon in church, taking the morning service. They were so impatient that they rode their horses right into the church, and struck him with their whips. "Come to Kosovo at once," they shouted, "and tell us who is to be Tsar!"

The Archdeacon wept. "Leave the house of God!" he said. "Let me finish the service, and then I will tell you."

So the messengers waited outside, and when the service was over, the Archdeacon came out to them. "My children," he said, "when the Tsar was dying, he never said one word to me about the empire. We only talked about his sins. Go to the castle of Prince Marko, at Prilip. Marko learned to read and write from me, and he was the Tsar's secretary, and he looked after the Tsar's papers. He will know who the Tsar chose. And Marko will tell you the truth, because he is afraid of nobody but God."

So the messengers rode to Prilip and knocked on the castle gates. Marko welcomed them kindly, and they told him about the quarrel on the field of Kosovo. "You must come," they said, "and tell everyone who is the true Tsar."

Marko went to his mother, Yevrosima, for her advice. "Marko, my only son," she said, "don't tell a lie to please your father, or your uncles. Tell the truth before God. It is better to lose your life than to lose your soul."

So Marko took the old papers, and climbed onto his horse Sharatz, and rode to the field of Kosovo.

When King Vukashin saw him coming, he cried, "How lucky I am! Here is my son Marko. He will say the empire is mine. Of course it passes from father to son!" But Marko didn't say a word, and he didn't even turn his head towards his father's tent.

When Uncle Uglyesha saw him, he said, "How lucky I am! Here is my nephew Marko. Say the empire is mine, Marko, and we will rule together like brothers!" But Marko didn't say a word, and didn't even turn his head.

When Uncle Goyko saw him, he cried, "Here is my dear nephew Marko! When you were little, I used to take you riding with me. Say the empire is mine, Marko, and you will really be the ruler, and I will be your helper!" But Marko didn't say a word, and rode straight past, to the tent of young Urosh. There he climbed down from Sharatz.

Urosh jumped up joyfully. "Here is my godfather Marko!" he cried. "Now he will tell us who is the true Tsar!" And they hugged each other, and sat down together.

The next morning, after church, the King and the princes and lords all sat down at tables in the churchyard. Then at last Marko opened the old papers, and spoke out loud.

"Father, aren't you happy with your own kingdom? Uncle Uglyesha, isn't your land big enough for you? Uncle Goyko, isn't your dukedom big enough? Why do you all want to take an empire that belongs to someone else? Look! It says here in the papers that the empire was left to Urosh. It passes from father to son. On the day he died, the Tsar named his son Urosh to rule after him."

When King Vukashin heard this, he jumped up in a fury, pulled out his golden dagger, and rushed at his own son. Marko ran, because he would never fight his own father. He ran around the church of Samodrezha, with his father close behind him. Three times they ran round the church, and his father had almost caught him, when a mysterious voice came from inside the church.

"Run into the church, Prince Marko! You told the truth, which God loves."

The church doors opened all by themselves. Marko ran inside, and the doors closed behind him. King Vukashin struck at the doors with his dagger, until he saw drops of blood trickling down the wood. Then he was filled with sorrow. "Oh, what have I done?" he cried. "I have killed my son Marko!"

But the voice from the church answered, "You have not even scratched your son Marko. You have struck an angel of God."

Then King Vukashin grew angry with Marko all over again, and put a curse on him. But young Urosh, the new Tsar, gave Marko his blessing. "Marko, my dear godfather," he said, "may God always help you. May your word always be trusted. May no hero ever beat you. And may your name be remembered with honour as long as the sun and the moon shine."
