---
title: The Seven Sleepers of Ephesus
adaptedFrom: baring-gould-myths--the-seven-sleepers-of-ephesus

namesNote: Baring-Gould translates the story from The Golden Legend, a book of the lives of the saints written in Latin by Jacobus de Voragine about 1260. In the Gospel of John, Jesus brings his friend Lazarus back to life.
---

Long ago, in the city of Ephesus, there lived seven young Christians. Their names were Maximian, Malchus, Marcian, Dionysius, John, Serapion and Constantine.

In those days the emperor was Decius, and he was cruel to the Christians. When he came to Ephesus, he ordered temples to be built, and he commanded everyone to come and make offerings to the idols. Any Christian who would not worship the idols would be put to death. The people of the city were so afraid that friends gave each other away, and fathers and sons turned against each other.

But the seven young men would not make offerings to the idols. They stayed in their houses, praying and fasting. Someone told the emperor about them, and they were brought before him, and they told him that they were Christians. The emperor gave them a little time to think about what they would do.

So the seven young men gave away everything they had to the poor. Then they went off together to Mount Celion, to hide.

One of them, Malchus, dressed up as a doctor and went into the town to buy food. While he was there, the emperor came back to Ephesus, and ordered his men to search for the seven. Malchus slipped out of the town and hurried back to his friends, full of fear, and told them how angry the emperor was. They were very frightened. But Malchus gave them the loaves he had bought. "Eat," he said, "so that you will be strong and brave when the hard time comes." So they ate. And then, as they sat crying and talking together, by the will of God they fell asleep.

The emperor's men searched everywhere, but they could not find the seven, and Decius was very angry. He sent for their parents and threatened them. But all the parents could tell him was that their sons had given everything away to the poor, and that they did not know where they had gone.

Then Decius thought that they might be hiding in a cave. So he had the mouth of the cave blocked up with stones, so that they could never come out.

Three hundred and sixty years went by. Theodosius was emperor now. And in his time, some people had begun to say that the dead would never rise again.

Then one day, a man of Ephesus was building a stable on the side of Mount Celion. He found a pile of stones that was just right for his building, and he took them away. And so the mouth of the cave was opened, and the seven sleepers woke up. To them it seemed that they had slept for only one night. They asked Malchus what the emperor had decided to do with them.

"He is going to hunt us down, and make us worship the idols," said Malchus.

"God knows we will never do that," said Maximian. And he cheered up his friends, and asked Malchus to go back to the town, to buy more bread and find out what was happening.

So Malchus took five coins and left the cave. When he saw the stones outside, he was astonished, but he went on toward the city. And when he came near the gate, what a surprise! There was a cross over it. He went to another gate, and there was a cross over that one too. And so it was over every gate of the city. He thought he must be dreaming.

He went into Ephesus, rubbing his eyes, and he heard people speaking the name of Jesus. "Yesterday no one dared to say the name of Jesus," he said to himself, "and now everyone is saying it! I can hardly believe that I am in Ephesus." He asked a man what the city was called, and the man said, "Ephesus." Malchus was amazed.

He went into a baker's shop and put down his money. The baker looked at the coins, and asked if he had found a treasure, and began whispering to the other people in the shop. Malchus thought that they had found out who he was, and that they were going to take him to the emperor. He begged them to let him go, and said they could keep the loaves and the money too. But the men in the shop grabbed him. "Whoever you are, you have found a treasure," they said. "Show us where it is, and share it with us, and we will hide you."

Malchus was too frightened to answer. So they pulled him through the streets to the market place. Soon everyone had heard that the young man had found a great treasure, and a huge crowd gathered around him. Malchus said again and again that he had done nothing wrong. No one knew him. And when he looked round at all the faces, he could not see a single one that he knew.

Then the bishop, Saint Martin, and the governor, Antipater, heard about all the excitement, and they sent for the young man and the bakers. They asked Malchus where he had found the treasure. He said he had not found any treasure; the coins were from his own purse. They asked him where he came from. "I come from Ephesus," he said, "if this is Ephesus."

"Send for your family, then," said the governor. "Your parents, if they live here."

"They do live here," said Malchus, and he told them his parents' names. But no one in the town had ever heard of them.

"How can you say this money belonged to your parents?" said the governor. "It is hundreds of years old, from the beginning of the reign of the emperor Decius, and it is not at all like the money we use now. Do you think you can fool the wise old men of Ephesus? The law will punish you until you show us where you found it."

"I beg you, in the name of God," cried Malchus, "answer a few questions for me, and then I will answer yours. Where has the emperor Decius gone?"

"My son," said the bishop, "there is no emperor of that name now. The one who was called Decius died long ago."

"Everything I hear puzzles me more and more," said Malchus. "Come with me, and I will show you my friends. Only yesterday they ran away with me to a cave on Mount Celion, to escape from the cruel Decius. I will take you to them."

The bishop turned to the governor. "The hand of God is in this," he said. So they followed Malchus, and a great crowd followed them. Malchus went first into the cave to his friends, and the bishop went in after him. And there they saw the young men sitting in the cave, with their faces as fresh and blooming as roses. Then everyone fell down on their knees and praised God.

The bishop and the governor sent a message to the emperor Theodosius, and he hurried to Ephesus. All the people of the city came out to meet him, and took him to the cave. As soon as the seven saw the emperor, their faces shone like the sun. The emperor gave thanks to God, and hugged them, and said, "Seeing you is like seeing Jesus bring Lazarus back to life."

"Believe us!" said Maximian. "God has woken us before the great day when the dead will rise, so that you may truly believe that the dead will rise again. A baby lives inside its mother without being hurt. And in the same way, we have lived without being hurt, fast asleep."

And when they had said this, they bowed their heads, and their souls went back to God who made them. The emperor bent over them and hugged them, weeping. He ordered golden caskets to be made for them. But that night the seven came to him in a dream. They told him that until now they had slept in the earth, and that in the earth they wanted to go on sleeping, until God should raise them up again.
