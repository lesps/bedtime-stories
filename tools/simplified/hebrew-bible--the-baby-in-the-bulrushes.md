Many years later, a new king came to rule over Egypt, a Pharaoh who knew nothing about Joseph.

He said to his people, "Look, the children of Israel are too many and too strong for us. Let us deal cleverly with them, or they will grow even more. If there is ever a war, they might join our enemies and fight against us."

So the Egyptians made the children of Israel into slaves. They put cruel masters over them, and made them build store-cities for Pharaoh. They made their lives bitter with hard work, making bricks and mortar, and working in the fields. But the more the Egyptians made them suffer, the more the children of Israel grew in number.

Then the king of Egypt spoke to the Hebrew midwives, the women who helped the mothers when their babies were born. Their names were Shiphrah and Puah. "When you help a Hebrew woman have her baby," said the king, "if it is a boy, kill him. But if it is a girl, let her live."

But the midwives feared God, and they did not do what the king of Egypt told them. They let the baby boys live.

The king sent for the midwives. "Why have you done this?" he said. "Why have you let the boys live?"

"The Hebrew women are not like the Egyptian women," the midwives told Pharaoh. "They are so strong that their babies are born before the midwife even gets there!" And God was good to the midwives, and the people kept growing in number, and became very strong.

Then Pharaoh gave an order to all his people. "Every baby boy that is born to the Hebrews, throw him into the river. But let every girl live."

Now a man and a woman of the family of Levi had a baby son. His mother saw what a fine child he was, and she hid him for three months. When she could not hide him any longer, she took a basket made of bulrushes, and covered it with tar and pitch to keep the water out. She put the baby in the basket, and laid it among the reeds at the edge of the river. And the baby's sister stood a little way off, to see what would happen to him.

Then Pharaoh's daughter came down to the river to bathe, while her maids walked along the riverbank. She saw the basket among the reeds, and sent her maid to fetch it. She opened it, and there was the baby, crying! And she felt sorry for him. "This is one of the Hebrews' children," she said.

Then the baby's sister came up to Pharaoh's daughter. "Shall I go and find one of the Hebrew women to nurse the baby for you?" she asked.

"Yes, go," said Pharaoh's daughter. So the girl went and fetched the baby's own mother.

"Take this baby and nurse him for me," Pharaoh's daughter told her, "and I will pay you." So the woman took her own baby home, and looked after him.

When the child grew older, his mother brought him to Pharaoh's daughter, and he became her son. She named him Moses, "because," she said, "I drew him out of the water."
