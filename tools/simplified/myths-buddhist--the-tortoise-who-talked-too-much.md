title: The Tortoise Who Talked Too Much

Long ago, when Brahmadatta was king of Benares, the Buddha-to-be was born into a family at the king's court. When he grew up, he became the king's wise adviser. But the king talked far too much. Once he started talking, nobody else could get a word in. The wise adviser wanted to put a stop to it, so he waited for the right moment.

Now far away, in a pond in the Himalaya mountains, there lived a tortoise. Two young wild geese came there looking for food, and they made friends with the tortoise, and soon the three of them were the best of friends.

One day the geese said, "Friend Tortoise, we have a lovely home high up in the Himalayas, in a golden cave on a mountain. Will you come with us?"

"How can I get there?" said the tortoise.

"We will carry you," said the geese, "if only you can keep your mouth shut, and not say a single word to anybody."

"Oh yes, I can do that," said the tortoise. "Take me along!"

So the geese gave the tortoise a stick to hold between his teeth. Then each goose took hold of one end of the stick, and up they flew into the air.

Some village children looked up and saw them. "Look!" they shouted. "Two geese are carrying a tortoise on a stick!"

By now the geese were flying over the king's palace in Benares. The tortoise wanted to shout back, "If my friends want to carry me, what's that to you, you rascals?" So he opened his mouth to speak, and let go of the stick. Down he fell, into the open courtyard of the palace, and that was the end of him.

What a commotion there was! "A tortoise has fallen into the courtyard!" everyone cried. The king came out with his wise adviser and all his courtiers to see.

"Wise sir," said the king, "what made this creature fall?"

"Now is my chance!" thought the adviser. And he worked it out. The tortoise and the geese must have been friends. The geese must have been carrying him to the mountains on a stick. Then he must have heard someone say something, and wanted to answer back. And because he could not keep his mouth shut, he let go, and fell from the sky.

So he said to the king, "O king, this is what happens to those who talk too much, and never know when to stop." And he said:

> The tortoise had to speak out loud,
> though he held a stick in his teeth.
> He spoke, and so he fell.

> So mark it well, great king:
> speak wisely, and speak at the right time.
> The tortoise fell to his death.
> He talked too much: that was the reason.

"Is he talking about me?" the king wondered. And he asked his adviser if he was.

"Whether it is you, great king, or anyone else," said the adviser, "whoever talks too much comes to grief like this." And from that day on, the king talked much less, and became a man of few words.

Long afterwards, the Buddha told this story about a monk who talked too much. "In those days," he said, "I myself was the king's wise adviser."
