---
title: The First Kings of Persia
adaptedFrom: zimmern-kings--the-first-shahs
order: 3
namesNote: Zimmern spells the kings' names Kaiumers, Husheng, Tahumers and Jemshid. Ahriman is the spirit of evil, and the divs are demons. Nowruz, the Persian New Year, is still kept on the first day of spring.
---

Long, long ago, the first king of Persia was Kayumars, and he was master of the world. He lived in the mountains, and he and his people dressed in tiger skins, and he taught them how to make clothes, which no one had known how to do before. People and animals came from every part of the earth to bow to him and learn his laws, and his glory was like the sun.

But Ahriman, the spirit of evil, grew jealous. He sent his son, a mighty div, a demon, with an army against Kayumars and his beloved son Siyamak. The angel Sorush, who flies around the earth seven times every night to watch over people, came and warned Kayumars. So when Siyamak rode out against the army, he knew he was fighting a demon, and he fought with all his strength. But the demon was stronger, and Siyamak was killed.

When Kayumars heard, he bowed his head to the ground. For a whole year he wept, and his army wept with him, and even the wild beasts and the birds of the air joined in. Then the angel Sorush told him to lift his head and fight back. So Kayumars sent Siyamak's son, Hushang, at the head of the army against the demons. And in that army there were peris, the fairy people, and tigers and lions and wolves. When the black demon heard them roaring, he trembled with fear, and Hushang beat him. Then Kayumars, who was very old, lay down and died, and Hushang became king.

Hushang was wise and fair, and he ruled for forty years, and the world was better for it. He was the first to give people fire, and he showed them how to strike it from a stone. He taught them to lead the rivers to water the land, and to plough and to harvest. And he sorted the animals into pairs and gave them names.

After him came his son Tahmuras, and he too opened people's eyes: he taught them to spin and to weave. The demons were jealous of him too, and tried to destroy him, but Tahmuras beat them and threw them to the ground. Then some of them begged for mercy, and promised to teach him something if he spared them. Tahmuras listened, and they taught him how to write. So from the wicked demons came a gift for everyone.

When Tahmuras had reigned for thirty years, his glorious son Jamshid came after him. Jamshid reigned for seven hundred years, and demons, birds and peris all obeyed him. The world was happy in his time. No one died, and no one knew pain or sorrow. He divided people into priests, warriors, craftsmen and farmers, and he divided the year into parts. With the help of the demons he built mighty buildings, among them the great palace of Persepolis, which is still called the Throne of Jamshid. People came from every corner of the earth to bring him gifts.

And Jamshid held a great feast, and called it Nowruz, the New Day, and the people of Persia still keep it today. The world was at peace, and people saw nothing in Jamshid but good.

But then Jamshid's heart grew proud, and he forgot where all his blessings came from. He saw no one in the world but himself. He called himself God, and sent out his statue for people to worship. The wise men hung their heads in sorrow, and no one knew how to answer him. Then God took His hand away from Jamshid. The kings and nobles turned against him, and took their warriors away from his court, and Ahriman had power over the land.
