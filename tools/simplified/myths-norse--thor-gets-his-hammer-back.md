One morning Thor woke up, reached for his hammer, and it was not there. He looked everywhere. He shook his beard and pounded his forehead. His hammer, Mjollnir, was gone!

"Listen, Loki," he said. "Nobody on earth or in heaven knows this yet. Someone has stolen my hammer!"

Without his hammer, Thor could not protect the gods from the giants. This was terrible.

So Thor and Loki went to see the beautiful goddess Freyja. "Freyja," said Thor, "will you lend me your cloak of feathers, so that I can fly and look for my hammer?"

"I would lend it to you even if it were made of gold," said Freyja, "or of silver."

So Loki put on the feather cloak and flew away, with the feathers whirring, out of the land of the gods and into the land of the giants.

There, sitting on a hill, was Thrym, the king of the giants. He was braiding golden collars for his dogs and combing his horses' manes.

"How are things with the gods?" asked Thrym, with a nasty grin. "Why have you come here all alone?"

"Things are bad with the gods," said Loki. "Have you hidden Thor's hammer?"

"I have," said Thrym. "I have hidden it eight miles under the ground. And nobody will ever get it back, unless they bring me the goddess Freyja to be my wife."

Loki flew straight back to Asgard. Thor was waiting for him. "Well?" he said. "Tell me the news while you're still up in the air!"

"Thrym has your hammer," said Loki. "And he won't give it back unless he can marry Freyja."

So they went to Freyja again. "Put on your wedding veil, Freyja," said Thor. "You and I are driving to Giantland."

Freyja was so furious that she snorted, and the whole hall of the gods shook beneath her. Her precious necklace, Brisingamen, burst apart. "Marry a giant?" she cried. "Never! Everyone would think I'd gone completely mad!"

So all the gods and goddesses met together to decide what to do. Then Heimdall, the wisest of the gods, who could see into the future, had an idea.

"Let's dress Thor up as the bride," he said. "Let's put a wedding veil on him, and Freyja's necklace, and a long dress, and jewels, and a pretty bonnet on his head. And let's hang a bunch of keys from his belt, like a housewife."

"Me?" roared Thor. "Dressed as a bride? The gods would laugh at me forever!"

"Be quiet, Thor," said Loki. "If you don't get your hammer back, the giants will soon be living in Asgard."

So they dressed Thor up as a bride, in a long dress and a veil and Freyja's necklace. And Loki dressed up as the bride's maid, to go with him. Then off they went in Thor's chariot, pulled by goats. The mountains cracked and the earth burned as they drove along.

"Get up, giants!" cried Thrym when he saw them coming. "Here comes my bride! Put straw on the benches! I have golden-horned cows and black oxen and heaps of treasure. All I was missing was Freyja!"

That evening there was a great wedding feast. And the bride was very hungry. She ate a whole ox, eight salmon, and all the cakes and sweets meant for the ladies. And she drank three barrels of mead!

Thrym stared. "I've never seen a bride eat like that!" he said.

The clever bridesmaid had an answer ready. "Freyja was so excited about marrying you," said Loki, "that she hasn't eaten anything for eight whole days."

Thrym leaned over to kiss his bride and lifted up the veil. But he jumped back, all the way to the end of the hall. "Why are Freyja's eyes so fierce?" he cried. "They look like they're burning with fire!"

"Freyja was so excited about marrying you," said Loki, "that she hasn't slept for eight whole nights."

Then Thrym's old sister came in and asked the bride for a present of gold rings. But the bride did not answer.

"Bring in the hammer," said Thrym, "to bless my bride! Lay it on her lap, and then we shall be married!"

So the giants brought in Thor's hammer and laid it in the bride's lap. And when Thor felt his hammer, his heart laughed inside him. He threw off his veil, grabbed Mjollnir and stood up, as tall and terrible as a thunderstorm. And Thrym and his giants never troubled the gods again.

And that is how Thor got his hammer back.
