Moses was looking after the sheep of his father-in-law, Jethro, the priest of Midian. He led the flock to the far side of the wilderness, and came to Horeb, the mountain of God.

There the angel of the Lord appeared to him in a flame of fire, out of the middle of a bush. Moses looked, and the bush was burning, but it did not burn up.

"I will go over and look at this strange sight," said Moses. "Why doesn't the bush burn up?"

When the Lord saw that Moses had turned aside to look, God called to him out of the bush. "Moses! Moses!"

"Here I am," said Moses.

"Do not come any closer," said God. "Take off your shoes, for the place where you are standing is holy ground. I am the God of your father, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob." And Moses hid his face, because he was afraid to look at God.

Then the Lord said, "I have seen how My people in Egypt are suffering. I have heard them crying out because of their cruel masters. I know their pain. And I have come down to rescue them from the Egyptians, and to bring them out of that land to a good land, a wide land, a land flowing with milk and honey. So now, go. I am sending you to Pharaoh, to bring My people, the children of Israel, out of Egypt."

But Moses said to God, "Who am I, to go to Pharaoh, and to bring the children of Israel out of Egypt?"

"I will be with you," said God. "And this will be the sign that I have sent you: when you have brought the people out of Egypt, you will worship God on this mountain."

"But when I go to the children of Israel," said Moses, "and say to them, 'The God of your fathers has sent me to you,' they will ask me, 'What is His name?' What shall I tell them?"

And God said to Moses, "I AM THAT I AM." And He said, "This is what you shall say to the children of Israel: 'I AM has sent me to you.' Tell them that the Lord, the God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac and the God of Jacob, has sent you. This is My name for ever.

"Go and gather the elders of Israel, and tell them that I have seen what is being done to them in Egypt, and that I will bring them out. They will listen to you. Then you and the elders shall go to the king of Egypt and say, 'The Lord, the God of the Hebrews, has met with us. Let us go three days' journey into the wilderness, to make offerings to the Lord our God.' I know that the king of Egypt will not let you go, unless a mighty hand makes him. So I will stretch out My hand, and strike Egypt with all My wonders. And after that, he will let you go."

"But they will not believe me," said Moses. "They will not listen to me. They will say, 'The Lord did not appear to you.'"

"What is that in your hand?" said the Lord.

"A staff," said Moses.

"Throw it on the ground," said the Lord. Moses threw it on the ground, and it became a snake! And Moses ran away from it.

"Reach out your hand and take it by the tail," said the Lord. So Moses reached out and caught hold of it, and it turned back into a staff in his hand. "That is so they will believe that the Lord, the God of their fathers, has appeared to you."

Then the Lord said, "Now put your hand inside your cloak." Moses put his hand inside his cloak, and when he took it out, it was white as snow, covered in a skin disease. "Put your hand back inside your cloak," said the Lord. And when Moses took it out again, it was healthy, like the rest of his skin.

"If they will not believe the first sign," said the Lord, "they will believe the second. And if they do not believe even these two signs, take some water from the river and pour it on the dry ground, and the water will turn to blood on the ground."

But Moses said to the Lord, "Oh Lord, I am not good with words. I never have been, and I am not now, even since You have spoken to me. I am slow of speech, and slow of tongue."

"Who made people's mouths?" said the Lord. "Who makes a person unable to speak or to hear, or able to see, or blind? Is it not I, the Lord? So go now. I will be with your mouth, and I will teach you what to say."

"Oh Lord," said Moses, "please send someone else."

Then the Lord was angry with Moses. "What about your brother Aaron the Levite?" He said. "I know he speaks well. He is already on his way to meet you, and when he sees you he will be glad. You shall tell him what to say, and I will be with your mouth and with his mouth, and teach you both what to do. He will speak to the people for you. And take this staff in your hand, for with it you will do the signs."
