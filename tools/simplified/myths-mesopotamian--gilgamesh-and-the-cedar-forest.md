title: Gilgamesh and the Cedar Forest

namesNote: The second of six parts of the Epic of Gilgamesh. Budge spells the names Khumbaba (Humbaba), Erech (Uruk) and Ninsunna, and calls the god Enlil Bêl.

One day Enkidu was unhappy, and he cursed the woman from the temple who had brought him out of the wild.

Then Shamash, the sun god, called to him from heaven. "Enkidu, why do you curse the woman from the temple? She gave you bread to eat that was good enough for a god, and wine to drink that was good enough for a king. She dressed you in fine clothes. And she gave you Gilgamesh for your friend! Gilgamesh lets you sleep on a fine bed, and sit in the seat of honour beside him, and princes kiss your feet. And for your sake he would put on a rough lion skin and follow you out over the plains."

When Enkidu heard this, his troubled heart was at peace.

Then Enkidu had a terrible dream. In his dream, the heavens thundered and the earth shook. A creature with an awful face, and claws like an eagle's, grabbed hold of him. It carried him down into the dark House of Dust, where the dead live. Whoever goes in there never comes out again. There is no light there. The people eat dust and mud, and they are dressed in feathers like birds, and they live in darkness. And in his dream Enkidu saw kings there, who had once ruled on the earth, and priests and magicians, and the Queen of the House of Dust herself.

When Gilgamesh heard about the dream, he set out a table with honey and butter on it, as a gift for Shamash.

Then Gilgamesh took Enkidu to see his mother, Ninsun, the wise woman, to ask her what the dream meant. Enkidu told her his dream. She burned sweet incense, and she prayed to Shamash. "Why did you give my son a heart that can never keep still?" she asked. For she knew that Gilgamesh had made up his mind to go on a dangerous adventure. He wanted to fight the mighty Humbaba. And she hoped that the god would stop him from going.

But Gilgamesh would not be stopped. He and Enkidu set out at once for the mountains where the cedar trees grow.

At last the two heroes came to the cedar forest. They stood and stared, for the trees were so tall and grew so close together. The forest belonged to the great god Enlil, and he had made Humbaba its guard. Humbaba's voice was like the roar of a storm, and his breath was like a gale of wind.

When Enkidu saw how dark and thick and frightening the forest was, he begged Gilgamesh to turn back. But Gilgamesh would not listen.

As they went through the forest, Enkidu had more dreams, two or three of them. He told them to Gilgamesh, and Gilgamesh said, "These dreams mean that we will win!"

Nobody knows exactly how the two heroes fought Humbaba, because that part of the old clay tablets is broken. But we do know that they beat him. And Gilgamesh and Enkidu came home to Uruk in triumph.
