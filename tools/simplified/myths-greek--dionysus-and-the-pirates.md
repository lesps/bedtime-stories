---
title: Dionysus and the Pirates
adaptedFrom: hesiod-hymns--hymn-to-dionysus
order: 42
---

One day, a young man was standing on a rocky point by the sea. He had thick, dark, curly hair, and he wore a fine purple cloak over his shoulders. He looked like a prince.

Just then a pirate ship came sailing by. The pirates saw the young man, and they nudged each other and grinned.

"Look at him," they whispered. "He must be the son of a king. His family will pay us lots of gold to get him back!"

So they rowed to shore, jumped out, grabbed the young man and carried him onto their ship. They tried to tie him up with ropes. But the ropes would not stay tied. They slipped off his hands and feet all by themselves and fell onto the deck. The young man just sat there, smiling, with a twinkle in his dark eyes.

The pirate steering the ship saw this, and he was frightened.

"You fools!" he shouted. "This is no ordinary young man. He's a god! Maybe Zeus, or Apollo, or Poseidon. Let him go, right now, before he gets angry and sends a terrible storm!"

But the captain only laughed at him. "Mind your own business," he said, "and help with the sails. We'll keep this fellow. Sooner or later he'll tell us who his rich friends are."

So they raised the sail, and the wind filled it, and the ship sped out to sea.

Then strange things began to happen.

First, sweet purple wine began to flow all over the ship, splashing across the deck, and the whole ship smelled delicious. The pirates stared.

Then a grapevine began to grow along the top of the sail, with bunches of grapes hanging down. Green ivy curled up and up around the mast, covered with flowers and berries. And garlands of flowers sprang up on all the oars.

Now the pirates were scared. "Steer for land!" they shouted. "Quick!"

But it was too late. The young man turned into a huge lion, right there at the front of the ship, and he roared a mighty roar. In the middle of the ship, a great shaggy bear appeared out of nowhere and stood up on its hind legs.

The pirates ran to the back of the ship and huddled around the steersman, the only one who had been kind. Then the lion sprang toward the captain. The pirates were so frightened that they all leaped over the side, into the sea.

And as they splashed into the water, they changed. Their legs joined together into tails. Their arms became flippers. Their bodies became smooth and grey. Every one of them turned into a dolphin! And that is why, they say, dolphins love to swim alongside ships and play in the waves around them.

Only the kind steersman was left on board. He was trembling all over. But the lion turned back into the young man, who smiled at him.

"Don't be afraid," he said. "You have a good heart. I am Dionysus, the son of Zeus, the god of grapes and wine."

And Dionysus made the kind steersman happy for the rest of his life.
