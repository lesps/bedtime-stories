---
title: Inanna and the Gifts of Wisdom
basedOn: the Sumerian poem Inanna and Enki (clay tablets of about 1800 BC)
workId: sumerian-inanna-me
order: 5
---

Long ago, Inanna, the young goddess of the city of Uruk, decided to visit Enki, the god of wisdom. Enki lived in the city of Eridu, in his temple on the deep fresh waters, and he kept all the gifts of wisdom there. The gifts were called the me (you say it "may"). They were the secrets of everything that makes a city a city: how to be a king, how to make music, how to tell the truth, how to build, how to write, how to work wood and copper and leather, how to light a fire and how to put one out, and many, many more.

When Enki heard that Inanna was coming, he called his servant Isimud. "Welcome the young lady at the gate," he said. "Give her butter cake and cool water, and pour her beer, and set a table for her."

So Isimud did, and Enki and Inanna sat down together and feasted and drank. And as they drank, Enki grew merrier and merrier, and more and more generous.

"By my power," said Enki, "I will give holy Inanna, my daughter, the gift of being a king!"

And Inanna took it.

"I will give her the crown, and the throne, and the sceptre!"

And Inanna took them.

"I will give her the gift of truth! And the gift of song! And the gifts of the carpenter and the coppersmith and the writer!"

And Inanna took them.

And Enki went on and on, giving her one gift after another, dozens and dozens of them: wisdom, and understanding, and judging fairly, and lighting fires, and putting them out. And Inanna took every one. Then she loaded all the gifts into her ship, the Boat of Heaven, and she set sail for home.

When the beer wore off, Enki looked around his temple. "Isimud," he said, "where are the gifts of wisdom?"

"My lord," said Isimud, "you gave them all to Inanna."

Enki was horrified. "Quick!" he said. "Take my guardians, and go after the Boat of Heaven, and bring it back!"

Isimud caught up with the Boat of Heaven at its first stopping place. "Inanna," he said, "my master Enki says you must give the boat back, and go home without it."

"Enki gave me these gifts himself!" said Inanna. "Has he broken his word?" Then she called to her faithful servant, Ninshubur. "Ninshubur, save the Boat of Heaven and the gifts!"

And Ninshubur did. She sent Enki's guardians away, and the Boat of Heaven sailed on.

But Enki did not give up. Again and again, at one stopping place after another, he sent more creatures after the boat: sea monsters, and giants, and great fish. And every time, Ninshubur saved the Boat of Heaven, and it sailed on towards Uruk.

At last the Boat of Heaven came safely into Uruk. All the people came out to welcome Inanna, and there was a great festival. Inanna unloaded the gifts of wisdom at the quay, one by one, and as she did, people saw that there were even more gifts than Enki had given her.

And the gifts of wisdom stayed in Uruk.
