---
title: Yggdrasil and Odin's Eye
adaptedFrom: blackwell-prose-edda--the-ash-yggdrasil

namesNote: Blackwell calls Odin “All-father.”
---

The gods built a bridge from the earth up to their home in the sky. It was the rainbow, and they called it Bifrost. It shimmers in three colors, and the red you see in it is burning fire, so that the giants cannot climb it. It is the strongest bridge ever made. But one day, at the end of the world, even Bifrost will break.

In the middle of everything grows the greatest and most wonderful of all trees. It is an enormous ash tree called Yggdrasil. Its branches spread out over the whole world and reach up above the sky. It holds all the nine worlds together, the world of the gods, the world of people, the world of giants, the worlds of elves and dwarfs, and the cold, dark world of the dead.

Yggdrasil has three great roots, stretching far apart from each other.

The first root reaches down to the cold, misty world of Niflheim. Under it is a bubbling spring, and around the root lives a dragon called Nidhogg, who gnaws and gnaws on it from below.

The second root reaches toward the land of the frost giants. Under it is the Well of Mimir, and in its water lie hidden all wisdom and understanding. Mimir, who guards the well, is the wisest of all, because every morning he drinks from it with a horn.

One day, Odin came to the well and asked Mimir for a drink of its water. Mimir agreed. But there was a price. Odin had to leave one of his eyes in the well. And Odin paid it. He gave up one of his eyes, so that he could drink and become wise. That is why Odin has only one eye. The other one lies at the bottom of Mimir's Well.

The third root reaches up into the sky, to the world of the gods. Under it is the holy Well of Urd. Every day the gods ride over the rainbow bridge to sit beside Urd's Well and make their decisions. Only Thor cannot ride over the bridge. He has to wade through the rivers instead, because the gods are afraid that his thunder and lightning would set Bifrost on fire.

Beside Urd's Well stands a beautiful hall, and in it live three maidens called the Norns. Their names are Urd, Verdandi and Skuld, which mean What Was, What Is and What Shall Be. They decide how long every person will live. Every day the Norns take water from the well, and white clay from around it, and pour it over the branches of the great ash tree, so that its branches never wither or rot. The water is so holy that it turns everything that touches it white. Two swans swim in the well, and all swans come from them.

All sorts of creatures live in the tree. At the very top sits an eagle, very wise, and between its eyes perches a hawk. A squirrel named Ratatosk runs up and down the trunk all day long, carrying messages back and forth between the eagle at the top and the dragon gnawing at the bottom. And they are not polite messages! Four stags leap among the branches, nibbling the buds. And so many snakes live down by the roots that no one could ever count them.

All around the tree are the homes of the gods. There is Alfheim, where the light elves live, who are brighter than the sun. There is Breidablik, where Baldur the beautiful lives, a place where nothing bad can ever happen. There is Glitnir, whose walls and pillars are made of gold and whose roof is silver. There is Himinbjorg, at the end of the rainbow bridge, where Heimdall the watchman lives. And there is Valaskjalf, Odin's hall, with a roof of silver, where his high seat stands.

And at the southern end of the sky stands the most beautiful hall of all, brighter than the sun, called Gimli. It will still be standing when heaven and earth have passed away, and good people will live there forever.
