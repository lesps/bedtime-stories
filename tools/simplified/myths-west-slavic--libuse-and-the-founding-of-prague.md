Long, long ago, on a high hill called Vyšehrad, above the river Vltava, there lived Krok, the first ruler of Bohemia. Krok had no sons, but he had three daughters: Kazi, Teta and Libuše. When Krok died, it was Libuše, the youngest, who became ruler of the land.

Libuše was a wonderful woman. She was good and fair, and kind and friendly to everyone, and no one judged the people's quarrels more wisely than she did. And she had a special gift. She could see into the future.

But some of the people did not like being ruled by a woman. One day, two brothers came to Libuše. Their father had died, and they were quarrelling over who should have his land. Libuše listened to them both, and gave her judgment. But the brother who lost was angry, and he shouted, "It is a shame for our country to be ruled by a woman!"

Then Libuše said, "If you are so fierce, I will rule you no longer. Go home now, and come back tomorrow. Choose a husband for me, and I will marry him, and he shall be your ruler."

But the people said, "No, you choose. We will take as our ruler whatever man you choose."

So Libuše looked into the future. She pointed to the hills far away, and said, "Behind those hills is a little river called Belina. On its bank is a farm called Stadice. Near the farm is a field, and in that field, your future ruler is ploughing, with two oxen covered in spots. His name is Přemysl, and his children's children will rule over you for ever. Take my horse, and follow it. It will lead you to him."

So the people's messengers set off, and Libuše's horse led them over the hills. And there in a field they found a man ploughing with two spotted oxen, just as Libuše had said. "Hail, Přemysl, our ruler!" they cried. Přemysl climbed onto the horse and rode back with them to Vyšehrad, still dressed in his rough farmer's clothes. And there he and Libuše were promised to each other in marriage. People say that his old farmer's shoes were kept safe in the castle at Vyšehrad for hundreds of years afterwards.

Then one day, Libuše stood on a high rock at Vyšehrad, with her husband Přemysl and the elders of the people around her. Again she looked into the future, and she said:

"I see a city whose glory will reach the stars. In the middle of the forest there is a place where the river Vltava curves around, with a deep valley to the north and a rocky hill to the south. Go there, and you will find a man in the forest, making a doorstep for a house. And just as even mighty lords must bow their heads to pass through a low door, so you shall call the city you build there Praha, the doorstep."

So the people went into the forest, and found the man making a doorstep, just as Libuše had said. And there they built a city, and called it Praha. And that city is Prague, which became the greatest city in all of Bohemia.
