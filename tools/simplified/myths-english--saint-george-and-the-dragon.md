Saint George is the patron saint of England, and Christians honor him to this day. Long ago, an Italian bishop wrote down his story in a book of saints' lives called The Golden Legend. This is the part of it that tells about the dragon.

George was an officer in the Roman army, and his family came from a land called Cappadocia. One day his travels brought him to a city called Silena, in the land of Libya.

Near the city there was a lake as big as a sea, and in it lived a terrible dragon. Whenever the people armed themselves and went out to fight it, it drove them back. And when it came up to the city walls, its breath was so poisonous that people fell sick and died.

So, to keep the dragon calm, the people gave it two sheep every day. But soon they had almost no sheep left. And then they made a dreadful law: every day, they would draw lots, and whoever's name was drawn would be given to the dragon, along with a sheep. Nobody was spared.

One day, the lot fell on the king's only daughter.

The king was heartbroken. "Take my gold and silver," he begged the people, "and half my kingdom, but let my daughter go!"

But the people were angry. "You made this law, O king," they said, "and our own children have been taken. Now you want to save yours? If you don't keep your own law, we will burn down your palace!"

So the king wept for his daughter. "Oh, my sweetest daughter," he cried, "I always hoped to see your wedding, with princes for guests, and the palace decorated with pearls, and music playing. And now you must go to the dragon." He begged the people to give him eight days to say goodbye, and they agreed. But when the eight days were over, they came back. "We are all dying from the dragon's breath," they said. "You must let her go."

So the king dressed his daughter in her royal robes, and hugged her, and kissed her, and gave her his blessing, weeping. And she walked out alone to the lake.

Just then George came riding by. He saw the princess crying, and he stopped. "What's wrong?" he asked.

"Good young man," she said, "get on your horse and ride away quickly, or you will die with me."

"Don't be afraid," said George. "Tell me why you are waiting here, with all the people watching from the walls."

"I can see you have a brave heart," she said. "But do you want to die with me? Go, quickly!"

"I won't go anywhere," said George, "until you tell me what is wrong."

So she told him everything. "Don't be afraid," said George. "In the name of Christ, I will help you."

"Good knight," she said, "save yourself. It is enough if I die alone. You cannot save me, and you will only die too."

Just as she said this, the dragon lifted its head out of the lake.

"Run, good lord!" cried the princess, trembling. "Run!"

But George leaped onto his horse. He made the sign of the cross, and he charged straight at the dragon as it came at him. He swung his lance with all his strength, and he struck the dragon a mighty blow, and threw it to the ground.

"Now," he said to the princess, "take off your belt and put it around the dragon's neck. Don't be afraid."

She did. And the great dragon got up and followed her as gently as a little dog on a lead.

She led it into the city. But when the people saw it coming, they ran away in terror over the hills, crying, "Now we shall all die!"

"Don't be afraid!" George called to them. "God sent me to save you from this dragon. Only believe in Christ and be baptized, and I will kill the dragon."

So the king and all his people were baptized, and George drew his sword and killed the dragon. It was so big that it took four pairs of oxen to drag it out of the city into a great field.

The king built a wonderful church in honor of Mary and of George, and from beside its altar there flowed a spring whose water healed the sick. The king offered George a huge fortune as a reward. But George would not take it. "Give it to the poor," he said.

Then, before he left, George told the king four things: to take care of the churches, to honor the priests, to go to church faithfully, and always to remember the poor. And he kissed the king goodbye and rode away.
