---
title: Emese's Dream and the Turul Bird
basedOn: the story of Emese's dream in the Gesta Hungarorum, written by the anonymous notary of King Béla of Hungary (about 1200), and the turul bird of the later chronicles
workId: hungarian-emese-turul
order: 2
---

Long ago, far away in the east, the Magyar people lived in a land called Scythia. Their leader, Ügyek, married a young woman called Emese, the daughter of a prince.

One night, Emese had a wonderful dream. In her dream, a great bird came to her, a bird like a falcon. It was the turul, a bird sent from heaven.

And in her dream, it seemed to Emese that a spring of water began to flow out of her. At first it was only a little stream. But it grew and grew, into a great river, and the river flowed away out of her own country, into a faraway land.

When Emese woke up, she knew that her dream meant something important. And soon afterwards, she had a baby son. Because he had been promised to her in a dream, she called him Álmos, from the Magyar word álom, which means "dream."

The wise people said that Emese's dream was a sign. The great river meant that from her son would come a great family of kings. But they would not rule in the land where Emese lived. Like the river, they would flow far away, and rule in a new land.

Álmos grew up to be wise and brave, and the Magyar people chose him as their leader. In his time, there were so many Magyars that their old land could no longer hold them. So Álmos led his people out of Scythia, westward, on a long, long journey across rivers and plains and mountains.

At last they came to the great green plain beside the river Danube, ringed by the Carpathian mountains. There Álmos's son, Árpád, led the Magyars into their new homeland, which is Hungary today. And just as Emese had dreamed, Árpád's children and grandchildren ruled there as kings for hundreds of years.

The Magyars never forgot the turul bird. They painted it on their shields and their banners, as the sign of their people. And you can still see the turul today, with its great wings spread wide, on the old buildings of Hungary.
