Long ago, the king of Britain was called Lludd. He was a good king, brave and generous, and he rebuilt the walls of London and filled it with fine houses. He loved London more than any other city, and lived there most of the year, so it was called Caer Lludd, Lludd's Fort. And that is how London got its name, or so the old story says.

Lludd had a brother called Llefelys, whom he loved more than all his other brothers, because he was so wise. When the king of France died, leaving only a daughter, Llefelys sailed to France, and married the princess, and became the king of France.

Then three terrible plagues fell on the Island of Britain.

The first plague was a strange people called the Coranians. They had such sharp ears that, wherever anyone spoke in the whole of Britain, however quietly, if the wind carried the words, the Coranians heard them. So nobody could make any plan against them.

The second plague was a scream. Every year, on the night before the first of May, a dreadful scream rang out over every house in Britain. It was so terrible that the men lost their strength, the young people lost their wits, and the animals and trees and fields and rivers stopped bearing fruit.

The third plague was a thief. However much food and drink was prepared in the king's palace, even enough for a whole year, it was all gone the next morning, except what had been eaten on the first night. And nobody knew who took it.

King Lludd was very worried. "Go to your brother Llefelys," his nobles told him. "He is the wisest man we know." So Lludd got ready some ships, very quietly, so that the Coranians would not find out, and sailed to France.

Llefelys came out to meet him with a great fleet. When Lludd saw it, he left all his ships behind except one, and sailed to meet his brother, and Llefelys did the same. And the two brothers threw their arms around each other.

"We must talk," said Lludd, "but the Coranians will hear us, if the wind carries our words."

So Llefelys had a long brass horn made, and they spoke to each other through it, so that the wind could not catch their words. But whatever they said into the horn came out as rude, angry words. "There is a demon in this horn," said Llefelys. So they poured wine into it, and washed the demon out. And then they could talk.

"For the first plague," said Llefelys, "I will give you some little insects. Keep a few of them to breed, in case the Coranians ever come back. Crush the rest in water. Then call together all your own people and all the Coranians, as if you wanted to make peace between them. And when they are all together, sprinkle the water over everyone. It will do no harm to your own people, but it will be the end of the Coranians.

"The second plague is a dragon. It is the dragon of your own island, and another dragon, from a foreign land, is fighting with it, and that is why your dragon screams. When you get home, measure the island from end to end and from side to side, and find the exact middle. Dig a pit there. Put a cauldron full of the best mead in the pit, and cover it with a cloth of satin. Then watch. You will see the dragons fighting in the shapes of terrible beasts, and then as dragons in the air. And when they are tired out, they will fall down on the cloth in the shape of two pigs, and sink down into the cauldron, and drink all the mead, and fall asleep. Then wrap them up in the cloth, and bury them in a stone chest in the strongest place in your kingdom. As long as they stay there, no plague will come to Britain from anywhere else.

"The third plague is a mighty magician. He sends everyone to sleep with his magic, and then he takes all your food. You must watch your food yourself. And so that he doesn't send you to sleep, keep a tub of cold water beside you, and whenever you feel sleepy, jump in."

So Lludd went home. First he called together his own people and the Coranians, and sprinkled the magic water over them all. And that was the end of the Coranians, but none of the Britons was harmed.

Then Lludd had the island measured, and he found that the middle of it was at Oxford. There he dug a pit, and put in a cauldron of the best mead, with a satin cloth over it, and he sat and watched all night. And he saw the dragons fighting. And when they were tired, they fell onto the cloth and sank down into the cauldron, and drank up all the mead, and fell asleep. Then Lludd wrapped them up in the cloth, and carried them away to the mountains of Snowdon, and buried them in a stone chest in the safest place there. That place is called Dinas Emrys. And the terrible scream was never heard again.

Last of all, Lludd held a great feast. And he sat up all night beside the food, in his armour, with a tub of cold water beside him. In the middle of the night, he heard beautiful, strange music and singing, and he grew very sleepy. But every time his eyes began to close, he jumped into the cold water, and woke up again.

At last a huge man came in, wearing heavy armour, and carrying a big basket. He began putting all the food and drink into the basket. And however much he put in, the basket was never full. Then he turned to go.

"Stop!" cried Lludd. "You have robbed me for the last time!"

The giant put down his basket, and they fought. They fought so hard that sparks flew from their weapons. But at last Lludd threw the giant to the ground.

"Mercy!" said the giant. "I will pay you back for everything I have ever taken from you, and I will never steal again. I will serve you faithfully all my life."

And Lludd agreed.

So Lludd freed the Island of Britain from the three plagues. And he ruled Britain in peace and happiness for the rest of his life.
