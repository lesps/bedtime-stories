In the deserts of Arabia there lived a good and generous king called Mirdas, and he had a son, Zahhak, whom he loved. One day Ahriman, the spirit of evil, came to the palace dressed as a nobleman, and tempted Zahhak to leave the path of goodness. "Listen to me," he said, "and make a promise with me, and I will raise your head above the sun."

Zahhak was young and simple, and he swore to obey Ahriman in everything. Then Ahriman told him to get rid of his father. "This old man is in your way," he said, "and as long as he lives, no one will know who you are." Zahhak was horrified, and wanted to break his promise, but Ahriman would not let him. He made Zahhak set a trap for his father, and Zahhak kept quiet, and his father fell into the trap and died. So Zahhak put on his father's crown, and Ahriman taught him magic. And Zahhak ruled his people both well and badly, for he had not yet given himself up completely to evil.

Then Ahriman thought of a trick. He took the shape of a young man, and asked to be the king's cook. Zahhak did not know who he was, and gave him the keys of the kitchen. Until then, people had eaten only plants. But Ahriman cooked meat for Zahhak, new dishes every day, and the meat made the king as strong and fierce as a lion. Zahhak was so pleased that he called for his cook and told him to ask for a reward.

"If the king is pleased with his servant," said the cook, "let me kiss his shoulders."

Zahhak saw no harm in that, and said yes. And Ahriman kissed him on the shoulders. At once the ground opened under the cook's feet and swallowed him up, and everyone was amazed. But where he had kissed Zahhak, black, hissing serpents grew out of his shoulders! Zahhak was terrified, and ordered them to be cut off. But each time they were cut, they grew again, and none of the wise men or doctors could find a cure.

Then Ahriman came again, dressed as a wise man. "This cannot be cured," he said, "and the serpents cannot be rooted out. You must feed them, and give them people to eat. Perhaps that will kill them." But in his heart Ahriman wanted the whole world to be emptied of people. So every day the serpents were fed, and the whole land was afraid of the king. Goodness was forgotten, and wicked people got everything they wanted.

Now the news spread through Iran that there was a mighty and terrible king in Arabia. And the nobles who had turned away from proud Jamshid asked Zahhak to be their king. The armies of Arabia and Persia marched against Jamshid, and he ran away. For a hundred years no one knew where he was hiding. But at last Zahhak's men found him, wandering by the sea in China, and they put him to death. So Jamshid's power withered away like grass, because he had grown proud and tried to raise himself above his Maker.

So Zahhak, the Serpent King, sat on the throne of Iran, and he did more and more evil, until the whole land cried out against him. He reigned for a thousand years, and wickedness walked in the daylight while goodness hid. Every day two men were taken to feed the serpents, and darkness spread over the land.

But God saw it, and took pity on His people. And He let a grandson be born to Jamshid, and his parents called him Fereydun.

On the night Fereydun was born, Zahhak dreamed that a young man, slim as a cypress tree, came at him with a mace shaped like a cow's head, and struck him to the ground. Zahhak woke up shaking, and called his wise men to tell him what the dream meant. They were afraid, and for three days they kept silent. But on the fourth day one brave man spoke. "Someone named Fereydun will come," he said. "He will take your throne, and strike you down with a cow-headed mace."

When Zahhak heard this, he fainted. When he woke, he ordered the whole world to be searched for Fereydun, and from then on he had no rest or joy.

Fereydun's mother was afraid that Zahhak would find her son. So she hid him in a thick forest, where there lived a wonderful cow called Purmayeh, whose hair was as beautiful as a peacock's feathers. She asked the cow's keeper to take care of her son, and for three years Fereydun grew up in the forest, and Purmayeh was his nurse.

Then his mother heard that Zahhak had learned about Purmayeh. So she took Fereydun far away to India, to a holy hermit on Mount Alborz, and begged him to keep the boy safe. And the hermit agreed. Meanwhile Zahhak found the beautiful cow, and learned about Fereydun. When he heard that the boy was gone, he raged like a mad elephant. He killed the wonderful cow, and all the creatures around her, and turned the forest into a desert. But he could not find Fereydun anywhere, and his heart was full of fear.
