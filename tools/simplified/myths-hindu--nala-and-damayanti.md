Long ago there was a king called Nala, who ruled the land of the Nishadhas. He was strong and handsome and truthful, he knew all about horses, and he was the finest archer of all. Everyone loved him.

In another kingdom, Vidarbha, King Bhima had no children. But a wise sage came to visit him, and to thank the king for his kindness, the sage gave him a blessing: a daughter, as precious as a jewel, and three brave sons. The daughter's name was Damayanti, and she grew up so lovely that she was famous all over the world. No one, not even among the gods, had ever seen anyone so beautiful.

The king's heralds praised Nala to Damayanti, and praised Damayanti to Nala. And because they heard so much about each other, Nala and Damayanti fell in love, without ever having met.

Nala began to spend his days alone in his palace gardens, thinking about Damayanti. One day he saw some swans there, with wings of gold, and he caught one in his hands.

"Don't hurt me, King," said the swan. "Let me go, and I will do something for you. I will tell Damayanti about you, so that she will never want to marry anyone else."

So Nala let the swan go, and all the swans flew away to Vidarbha. When they landed in front of Damayanti and her friends, the girls were delighted, and ran after them to catch them. The swans scattered in all directions, and the swan that Damayanti chased led her off to a quiet place. Then it spoke to her.

"Damayanti," said the swan, "there is a king of the Nishadhas called Nala. We have seen gods and spirits and men, but we have never seen anyone like him. You are the best of women, and he is the best of men. You should be his wife."

"Go and say the same to Nala," said Damayanti.

So the swan flew back and told Nala everything.

After that Damayanti could think of nothing but Nala. She grew pale and thin, and sighed, and could not sleep. Her maids told her father, and the king decided it was time for her swayamvara, when she would choose a husband for herself. So he sent word to all the kings of the earth, and they came to his city with their elephants and horses and chariots.

Now the gods heard about it too. The sage Narada told Indra, the king of the gods, that all the kings on earth were going to Damayanti's swayamvara. And Indra, Agni, Varuna and Yama decided to go as well, to try to win her.

On the way they met Nala, and they were amazed at how handsome he was. "Nala," they said, "you always keep your word. Will you help us? Will you be our messenger?"

"I will," said Nala. "But who are you, and what do you want me to do?"

"We are the gods," said Indra. "I am Indra, and this is Agni, and this is Varuna, and this is Yama. Go and tell Damayanti that we are coming to her swayamvara, and that she must choose one of us for her husband."

"But I want to marry her myself!" said Nala. "How can I ask her to marry someone else? Please don't send me."

"You have already promised," said the gods. "Will you break your word?"

"But her palace is well guarded," said Nala. "How can I get in?"

"You will get in," said Indra.

So Nala went to Damayanti's palace, and no one saw him go in. There he found Damayanti among her maids, more beautiful than he had ever imagined. His love for her grew stronger than ever, but he had given his word, so he said nothing about it. The maids jumped up in astonishment. "Who is he?" they wondered. "Is he a god?"

Damayanti smiled at him, and he smiled back. "Who are you?" she asked. "And how did you get in here, when my rooms are so well guarded?"

"I am Nala," he said. "I have come as a messenger from the gods. Indra, Agni, Varuna and Yama all want to marry you. You must choose one of them. It was their power that let me in here without being seen."

Damayanti bowed to the gods. Then she smiled at Nala. "My king," she said, "everything I have is yours. Ever since the swans told me about you, I have been longing for you. I called all the kings here for your sake."

"How can you choose a man when the gods want you?" said Nala. "I am not worth the dust on their feet. Choose one of them."

Damayanti's eyes filled with tears. "I bow to all the gods," she said. "But I choose you for my husband. That is the truth."

"I have made a promise to the gods," said Nala. "How can I ask for you for myself, when I came here as their messenger?"

Then Damayanti thought of a way. "Come to the swayamvara with the gods," she said. "And there, in front of them all, I will choose you. Then no one can blame you."

So Nala went back to the gods, and told them truthfully everything that had happened.

On the day of the swayamvara, all the kings sat in the great hall, decked with garlands and jewels. Then Damayanti came in, and everyone stared at her. But when the names of the kings were called out, Damayanti saw five men who all looked exactly alike! Each of them looked just like Nala. The gods had disguised themselves. How could she tell which was the real Nala?

Damayanti thought hard, but she could not tell. So she folded her hands and prayed to the gods. "Ever since I heard the swans, I have chosen Nala, and I have never stopped loving him, in thought or in word. If that is true, then please, gods, show me which one is Nala."

The gods heard her prayer, and saw how true and how strong her love was. So they showed her their signs. And now Damayanti saw that four of the five were not sweating, and their eyes did not blink. Their garlands were fresh, there was no dust on them, and their feet did not quite touch the ground. But the fifth one cast a shadow. His garland was wilting, he was dusty and hot from the journey, his eyes blinked, and his feet stood firmly on the ground. That was Nala!

So Damayanti shyly took hold of the edge of his robe, and placed a garland of flowers around his neck. The kings cried out in disappointment, but the gods and the sages cried, "Well done! Well done!"

"You chose a man, when you could have chosen a god," said Nala to Damayanti. "As long as I live, I will be yours, and yours alone." And Damayanti promised the same to him.

Then the gods, who were pleased with them, gave Nala eight gifts, two from each god. Agni promised to come whenever Nala called him, and Varuna did too, and Yama gave him a fine taste for food and a love of goodness. Then the gods went back to heaven, and the kings went home.

Nala and Damayanti were married, and they went back to Nala's kingdom. There Nala ruled his people well and wisely, and they were very happy, and in time they had a son and a daughter.
