In the old days in Lithuania, when a storm came rolling over the fields, and the sky went dark, and the thunder crashed, people said, "Perkūnas is out hunting."

Perkūnas was the god of thunder. He rode across the sky in a chariot of fire and stone, and some people said it was pulled by goats, and others said by fiery horses. He carried an axe and a heap of stones, and when he struck with his axe or threw his stones, the lightning flashed and the thunder rumbled. Some even called him the Stone Smith, because he worked with stones in the sky the way a smith works with iron.

And who was Perkūnas hunting? He was hunting his old enemy, the devil, whom the Lithuanians called Velnias.

The devil was a thief and a trickster. He stole the farmers' cattle, and he stole the good things that made their fields grow. Some people even said that, long ago, he had stolen away the bride Perkūnas was going to marry, and that Perkūnas had been hunting him ever since.

But Velnias was very good at hiding. When he heard the thunder coming, he ran away and hid in the hollow of a tree, or under a big stone. Or he turned himself into an animal, a black cat, or a dog, or a goat, or even a pike in the river, and tried to hide among the ordinary animals.

But Perkūnas could always find him. He would hurl down his lightning at the tree, or the stone, where the devil was hiding. That is why, people said, lightning strikes trees and stones. Perkūnas is not trying to hurt the tree. He is trying to hit the devil hiding inside it.

So when a great storm was over, people did not only feel frightened. They felt glad. For the storm had chased away the evil spirits from the land, and brought back the good things the devil had stolen, and the rain made the fields grow green again.

Perkūnas's own tree was the oak, the strongest and tallest tree in the forest. And in Latvia, just across the border, people told the same stories about the thunder god, whom they called Pērkons.

So if you are ever out in a storm, and you hear the thunder crashing over the hills, you can imagine Perkūnas in his fiery chariot, chasing the devil across the sky.
