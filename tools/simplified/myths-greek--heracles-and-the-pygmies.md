Long ago, when the world was full of wonders, there lived a giant named Antaeus. He was so tall that he used a whole pine tree as a walking stick. He had only one eye, right in the middle of his forehead.

Near him lived the Pygmies. They were tiny people, no taller than your hand. Their houses were the size of a bird cage. Their king's palace was as big as a dollhouse. When they cut their wheat, it was like chopping down a forest.

The giant and the Pygmies were brothers. They were all children of Mother Earth. So, even though one was huge and the others were tiny, they loved each other very much.

"Hello, brother Antaeus!" a Pygmy would shout up to him, fifty times a day.

"Hello, brother Pygmy!" the giant would answer, in a voice like thunder.

On holidays, Antaeus lay down on the grass, and he looked like a long hill. The Pygmies climbed all over him. They played hide-and-seek in his hair. They walked from his head to his toes, and it took them an hour. Sometimes they poked him with their tiny swords, just to see how tough his skin was. Antaeus only laughed.

"Ho, ho, ho!" he roared. "What fun it must be to be little!"

The Pygmies had only one trouble. They were always at war with the cranes. Cranes are tall birds with long beaks, and to a Pygmy a crane was a fearsome enemy. When the battle went badly, Antaeus would wave his walking stick, and the cranes would fly away honking.

Antaeus had a secret. Every time he touched the ground, Mother Earth made him stronger. If he fell down, he got up even stronger than before.

One day Antaeus was taking a nap. A Pygmy climbed up onto his shoulder and looked out over the land. Far away, he saw something coming. At first it looked like a blue mountain. Then it looked like a man. It was a very big man, with a golden helmet, a lion's skin over his back, and a huge club on his shoulder.

The Pygmy ran to the giant's ear. "Wake up, brother Antaeus!" he shouted. "Another giant is coming to fight you!"

"Nonsense," mumbled Antaeus. "Let me sleep."

But all the Pygmies were shouting now. "Get up, you lazy giant! His club is bigger than yours. His shoulders are wider. We think he is stronger than you!"

That made Antaeus jump up. No one was stronger than Antaeus! He grabbed his pine tree and stomped off to meet the stranger.

"Who are you?" he roared. "What are you doing in my land?"

The stranger was not afraid. "My name is Heracles," he said. "I am on my way to fetch three golden apples."

Antaeus had heard of Heracles, the strongest man in the world, and he did not like him one bit. "You shall go no farther!" he bellowed. And he swung his pine tree.

Heracles caught the blow on his club. Then he gave Antaeus a bonk on the head, and down went the giant, flat on the ground.

But the moment Antaeus touched the earth, he jumped up again, stronger than ever. He swung again. This time his pine tree broke into a thousand pieces. Heracles knocked him down again, and again Antaeus got up, stronger still.

Heracles was clever as well as strong. He thought, "Every time I knock him down, the earth makes him stronger. I must keep him off the ground."

So Heracles threw down his club. "Let's wrestle," he said.

Antaeus rushed at him. Heracles grabbed the giant around the middle and lifted him high up into the air. Antaeus kicked and wriggled like a baby held up by his father. But he could not reach the ground.

Up in the air, far from Mother Earth, Antaeus grew weaker and weaker. His kicking slowed down. His roaring turned into a grumble, and then into a whisper. At last he had no strength left at all. When Heracles let him go, the giant lay still on the sand, like a sandy hill, and he never got up again.

The Pygmies cried and cried for their brother. But Heracles did not even notice them. He was tired after the fight, so he spread out his lion skin and lay down for a nap.

While he slept, the Pygmies held a meeting. One brave Pygmy stood on top of a toadstool to make a speech.

"That stranger has hurt our brother!" he cried. "We will not let him get away with it!"

"Hooray!" shouted all the Pygmies.

So the whole Pygmy army marched out. Thousands of archers lined up with their tiny bows. Others piled up sticks and dry grass around the sleeping hero's head. Then they lit the pile with a torch.

Heracles woke up with his hair on fire. "What's all this?" he cried, patting out the flames.

The archers let their arrows fly. To Heracles, they felt like mosquito bites.

"You hurt our brother Antaeus!" squeaked a million tiny voices. "We declare war on you!"

Heracles looked around. At first he saw nothing. Then he looked down at the ground and saw the whole Pygmy army at his feet. He picked one up between his finger and thumb and set him on the palm of his hand. It was the very Pygmy who had made the speech on the toadstool.

"What in the world are you?" asked Heracles.

"I am your enemy!" said the Pygmy, as loudly as he could. "I challenge you to a fight!"

Heracles laughed so hard that he nearly dropped him. "I have seen many wonders," he said. "Monsters with nine heads, and dogs with three. But you are the biggest wonder of all! Your body is no bigger than my finger. How big is your heart?"

"As big as yours!" said the Pygmy.

Heracles stopped laughing. He saw that the little man was truly brave, as brave as any hero.

He bowed low to the whole Pygmy nation. "Brave little people," he said, "I would never hurt anyone with hearts as big as yours. I will make peace with you. I will take five steps, and on the sixth I will be gone from your land. I will walk very carefully, so I do not step on anyone. For once in my life, Heracles has been beaten!"

And so he did. The Pygmies stayed in their own little land. Some say they live there still, building their little houses, planting their little fields, and telling their children about the day they beat the mighty Heracles.
