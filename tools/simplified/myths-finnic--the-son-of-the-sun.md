---
title: The Son of the Sun and the Giant's Daughter
basedOn: the Sámi poem of the Son of the Sun, written down by Anders Fjellner and printed by Otto Donner in Lappalaisia lauluja (1876)
workId: sami-son-of-sun
order: 11
---

The Sámi are the reindeer people of the far north, where in summer the sun never sets, and in winter it never rises. And this is a story they used to sing about how their people began.

Long ago, the Son of the Sun grew up into a strong young man. He was lonely, and he wanted a wife: someone to keep him company all his life, to calm him when he was angry, and to have children with him.

But there was no wife for him at home. So he set out on a long voyage across the sea with his best men, to find a wife in the land of the giants. He sailed for a whole year, past the moon and the sun, beyond the farthest star in the east, to a land of mountains of silver and gold.

There, on the shore, he found a young giant girl, the daughter of a giant, washing clothes by the water.

At first the giant girl tried to frighten him away. "Go home, little man," she said. "My father and my brothers are giants. If they find you here, they will destroy you!"

But the Son of the Sun was not afraid. "I am strong too," he said. "And I have come to ask you to be my wife."

The giant girl looked at him, and liked him. So she went to her father, an old blind giant, and asked if she could marry the stranger.

"First I must see if he is strong enough," said the old giant. "Let him shake hands with me, and we'll pull fingers, and see who is stronger."

But the giant girl was clever. She knew her father's grip would crush any ordinary hand. So she secretly gave the Son of the Sun an iron glove to wear. The old giant took hold of his hand and squeezed and pulled with all his might, but he could not hurt him.

"He is strong enough," said the giant at last. And he agreed to the wedding.

The giant gave his daughter a wonderful wedding gift: great blocks of golden rock from the mountains, which were loaded onto the ship. And she took with her three chests, one blue, one red and one white, and a secret key. And she took something else too: a cloth with three knots tied in it. Each knot held a wind. The first knot held a gentle wind, the second knot a stronger wind, and the third knot a mighty storm.

Then the Son of the Sun and his bride set sail for home.

When the giant girl's brothers came home from hunting seals and whales and walruses out on the sea, they found that their sister was gone.

"Where is our sister?" they roared.

"She has gone away with the Son of the Sun," said their father.

The brothers were furious, and they jumped into their boat and rowed after the ship. Soon they were catching up.

So the bride untied the first knot in her cloth. Out blew a fresh breeze, and filled the sails, and the ship sped away. But the giants rowed harder, and caught up again.

So she untied the second knot. Out blew a strong wind, and the ship raced over the waves. But still the giants rowed harder, and caught up a third time.

"Will the boat hold if I untie the last knot?" she asked.

"It will hold," said the Son of the Sun.

So she untied the third knot. And out roared a mighty storm, and the ship flew away over the sea, so fast that the giant brothers lost sight of it altogether.

The brothers rowed to the shore, and climbed up to the top of a high mountain to look out over the sea for the ship. They stood there all night, staring out over the water. But when the morning came, the Sun rose over the edge of the world, and his bright rays fell on the giants. And the giants, and their boat, were turned to stone. And there they stand on the mountain to this day.

The Son of the Sun brought his bride safely home, and they had a second wedding there, on the skin of a bear and the skin of a young reindeer. And the giant's daughter became one of the Sámi.

Then she had children with the Son of the Sun. And their children's children became the Sámi people, the people of the reindeer and the midnight sun.
