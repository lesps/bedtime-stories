Long, long ago, Cornwall was a cold, bare, empty land, where only giants lived. Then two great Trojan chiefs, Brutus and Corineus, came sailing with a big army, and they landed at Plymouth, meaning to make the whole island their own.

They had heard strange stories about the giants, so they sent some of their soldiers ahead to explore. The soldiers marched off laughing, thinking they would only find a few poor, half-starved people. But they came running back, nearly scared out of their wits, chased by a crowd of huge, ugly giants waving clubs as big as oak trees and making the most dreadful noises.

But Brutus and Corineus were not afraid, because they had been expecting giants. They quickly gathered their army and charged at the giants with spears and arrows. The giants had never seen spears or arrows before, and they did not like being poked one bit. They turned and ran away to hide in the hills of Dartmoor. All except one: Gogmagog, the giants' captain, who was too badly hurt to run.

Gogmagog bellowed with rage until the birds nearly fell out of the sky. Then he thought he had better be quiet and hide. So he lay down in a big ditch, hoping the darkness would hide him. But the moon was full, and it was almost as bright as day, and the Trojan soldiers, who wanted to bring home at least one giant, soon found his great big body stretched out in the ditch.

They tied him up and dragged him back to their camp on Plymouth Hoe. But they were kind to him. They fed him well and looked after him until his wounds had healed.

Then Brutus tried to make peace with him. He suggested this plan and that plan for sharing the land. But Gogmagog just sat there and said no to everything. The giant thought he was very clever, and he was trying to think of a way to win without any more of those nasty sharp arrows.

"Clubs and fists I don't mind," he grumbled. "But I don't see no fun in they sharp little things that stick right into you."

Then he had an idea. In Cornwall, even in those days, people loved to wrestle. "These Trojans won't know anything about wrestling," thought Gogmagog. "And they're only little bits of chaps." So he said to Brutus, "Let's settle it with a wrestling match. Whoever wins gets the country."

To Gogmagog's surprise, Brutus agreed at once. The best wrestler among the Trojans was Corineus, and he accepted the challenge straight away.

On the great day, all the giants came to watch, sitting on one side of a ring on Plymouth Hoe, with the Trojans on the other. And the fight began.

What a fight it was! The giant was enormous and tremendously strong, but Corineus was skilful and calm. They wrestled for a long, long time, and nobody could tell who would win.

"The little 'un can't hold out much longer," said one of the giants. "Our Cap'n's only playing with him."

But just then Corineus darted in, grabbed the giant by his belt, and shook him with all his might. Gogmagog swayed backwards and forwards, and then Corineus gave him one tremendous push, and over he went! He fell flat on his back with a thud that shook the ground for miles around. The giantesses at home said, "Oh dear, there must be an earthquake somewhere!"

The other giants sat with their mouths open. They were sure it must be magic, because "a little whippersnapper like that there Corinoos" could never have knocked their captain down by himself.

While Gogmagog lay there, puffing and blowing so hard that the sea grew rough, Corineus gathered all his strength. He rushed at the giant, grabbed him by the legs, dragged him to the edge of the cliff, and pushed him over. Down went Gogmagog, rolling and tumbling over the rocks, with a roar like thunder, into the sea far below. And that was the end of him.

The giants went home very quiet and very frightened, and they never dared to fight the Trojans again. So the land was won.

Later, when Brutus was sharing out the island among his chiefs, Corineus asked for the giants' country, because he loved hunting the great lumbering giants out of their caves. So it was given to him, and he called it Cornwall, after his own name. In time all the giants were gone, and in their place came hard-working people who farmed the land and dug for tin. And their great-great-grandchildren live there still.
