The word of the Lord came to Jonah, the son of Amittai. "Get up," said the Lord, "and go to Nineveh, that great city, and speak out against it, for I have seen how wicked its people are."

But Jonah got up and ran away from the Lord. He went down to the port of Joppa, and found a ship going to Tarshish. He paid the fare, and went on board, to sail away from the Lord.

But the Lord threw a great wind at the sea, and there was such a mighty storm that the ship was about to break apart. The sailors were terrified, and each of them cried out to his own god. They threw the cargo overboard to make the ship lighter. But Jonah had gone down into the bottom of the ship, and he lay there, fast asleep.

The captain came down to him. "How can you sleep?" he said. "Get up, and call on your God! Perhaps your God will think of us, and we won't drown."

The sailors said to each other, "Come, let's cast lots, to find out whose fault this trouble is." So they cast lots, and the lot fell on Jonah.

"Tell us," they said, "why has this trouble come upon us? What work do you do? Where do you come from? What country are you from, and what people?"

"I am a Hebrew," said Jonah, "and I fear the Lord, the God of heaven, who made the sea and the dry land." And he told them that he was running away from the Lord.

The men were even more frightened. "What have you done?" they said. "What shall we do to you, so that the sea will calm down for us?" For the sea was getting wilder and wilder.

"Pick me up and throw me into the sea," said Jonah, "and the sea will calm down. I know that this great storm has come because of me."

But the men rowed hard to get back to land. They could not, for the sea grew wilder and wilder. So they cried out to the Lord, "Please, Lord, don't let us die for taking this man's life. For You, O Lord, have done as You pleased." Then they picked Jonah up and threw him into the sea. And the sea stopped raging. Then the men were filled with awe of the Lord, and they made an offering to the Lord, and made promises to Him.

And the Lord sent a great fish to swallow Jonah. And Jonah was inside the fish for three days and three nights.

From inside the fish, Jonah prayed to the Lord his God. "In my trouble I called to the Lord, and He answered me. You threw me into the deep, into the heart of the seas, and the waters swirled around me. All Your waves and Your breakers rolled over me. The water closed over me, and seaweed was wrapped around my head. I went down to the roots of the mountains. But You brought my life up from the pit, O Lord my God. When my life was fading away, I remembered the Lord, and my prayer came to You, in Your holy temple. I will make offerings to You, with songs of thanks. What I have promised, I will do. It is the Lord who saves."

Then the Lord spoke to the fish, and it spat Jonah out onto the dry land.

The word of the Lord came to Jonah a second time. "Get up, and go to Nineveh, that great city, and tell it what I tell you."

This time Jonah got up and went to Nineveh, as the Lord had said. Nineveh was a huge city; it took three days to walk across it. Jonah went one day's walk into the city, and called out, "Forty more days, and Nineveh will be destroyed!"

And the people of Nineveh believed God. They decided to go without food, and they all dressed in sackcloth, from the greatest of them to the least. When the news reached the king of Nineveh, he got up from his throne, took off his royal robe, put on sackcloth, and sat in the ashes. And he sent out an order through all Nineveh: "No one, person or animal, is to eat or drink anything. Let everyone wear sackcloth, and cry out to God with all their might. Let everyone turn away from their wicked ways, and from the violence of their hands. Who knows? Perhaps God will change His mind, and turn away from His anger, so that we do not die."

When God saw what they did, and how they turned away from their wicked ways, God changed His mind about the harm He had said He would do to them, and He did not do it.

But Jonah was very unhappy about this, and he was angry. He prayed to the Lord, "O Lord, isn't this just what I said, when I was still at home? That is why I ran away to Tarshish in the first place. For I knew that You are a gracious God, and full of mercy, slow to anger, and full of love, and ready to change Your mind about bringing harm. So now, O Lord, take my life. It is better for me to die than to live."

And the Lord said, "Are you so very angry?"

Then Jonah went out of the city, and sat down on the east side of it. He made himself a shelter, and sat in its shade, to see what would happen to the city.

And the Lord God made a gourd plant grow up over Jonah, to shade his head and ease his misery. And Jonah was very glad of the plant.

But at dawn the next day, God sent a worm, and it attacked the plant, so that it withered. And when the sun rose, God sent a hot east wind, and the sun beat down on Jonah's head until he felt faint. He wished he could die. "It is better for me to die than to live," he said.

And God said to Jonah, "Are you so very angry about the plant?"

"Yes," said Jonah, "I am very angry, angry enough to die."

And the Lord said, "You feel sorry for the plant, which you did not work for, and did not make grow. It came up in a night, and it died in a night. And should I not feel sorry for Nineveh, that great city, where there are more than a hundred and twenty thousand people who do not know their right hand from their left, and many animals as well?"
