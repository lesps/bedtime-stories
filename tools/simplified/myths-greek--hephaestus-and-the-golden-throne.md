title: Hephaestus and the Golden Throne

When Hephaestus was born, his mother, Hera, the queen of the gods, took one look at him and frowned. Her new baby had a twisted foot. He would always walk with a limp.

Hera was proud, and she was ashamed of him. She wanted to hide him away where no one would see him. So she picked him up and threw him right off Mount Olympus. Down, down he fell, all the way to the sea.

Luckily, two kind sea goddesses saw him falling. One was Thetis, a sea nymph with lovely hair, and the other was Eurynome, a daughter of the great river Ocean that flows all around the world. They caught the baby in their arms and took him home to a hollow cave beside the sea, where the waves foamed and murmured all day long.

No one else knew he was there, not the gods and not people either. Hephaestus grew up in the cave, and he found that he loved to make things. He lit a fire and built a forge, and he learned to hammer metal. For nine years he made beautiful things for Thetis and Eurynome: brooches and bracelets, curly pins, necklaces and golden flowers. He became the finest craftsman in the world.

But he never forgot what his mother had done.

One day, a wonderful present arrived on Mount Olympus for Queen Hera. It was a throne, made all of gold, shining and splendid, and it came from her son Hephaestus. Hera was delighted. She sat down on her beautiful new throne.

And she could not get up.

Hephaestus had made the throne with secret chains, so fine that no one could see them. As soon as Hera sat down, they wrapped around her and held her tight. She wriggled and shouted, but she was stuck fast.

The gods begged Hephaestus to come up to Olympus and set his mother free. One after another, they went to his forge and asked him. But Hephaestus would not listen to any of them. "She threw me away," he said. "Why should I help her now?" And he kept on hammering.

Then Dionysus, the god of grapes and wine, went to see him. Hephaestus trusted Dionysus more than anyone. They sat and talked, and Dionysus poured him cup after cup of sweet wine, until Hephaestus grew merry and sleepy and forgot to be angry. Then Dionysus led him all the way up to Olympus. And there, at last, Hephaestus let his mother go free.

From then on, Hephaestus lived on Mount Olympus with the other gods, and he became their smith. He was the god of fire and of blacksmiths, and of everyone who makes things with their hands.

And in the end, he became the one who kept the peace in his family. Once, at a feast of the gods, Zeus and Hera had a terrible quarrel, and all the gods sat silent and worried. Then Hephaestus jumped up. He limped over to his mother and put a cup into her hand.

"Mother, be patient," he said gently. "Let's not quarrel about mortals and spoil our feast. Please be kind to Father, and he'll be kind to us. You know how strong he is. Once, when I tried to stand up for you, he grabbed me by the foot and threw me out of heaven! I fell all day long, and when the sun went down I landed on the island of Lemnos, with hardly any breath left in me. The kind people there looked after me."

Hera smiled at her son, and took the cup.

Then Hephaestus went limping all around the hall, pouring sweet nectar for every god in turn. And when the gods saw him bustling about so busily, they all began to laugh, and they laughed and laughed, and the quarrel was forgotten.
