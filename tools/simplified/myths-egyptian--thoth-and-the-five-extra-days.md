Long, long ago, when the world was new, a year had only three hundred and sixty days.

In those days Nut, the goddess of the sky, secretly married Geb, the god of the earth. When the sun god Ra found out, he was angry, and he put a curse on Nut. "You shall not have a baby," he said, "on any day of any month of the year!"

Nut was very sad. But Thoth, the wise god, was her friend, and he wanted to help her. So he went to the Moon, and asked her to play a game of dice with him.

They played, and played, and Thoth won game after game. And for his prize, the Moon had to give him a tiny piece of every single day of the year, one seventieth of each day.

Thoth gathered up all those tiny pieces of time, and put them together. And they made five whole new days. Then he added the five days onto the end of the year, so that now the year had three hundred and sixty-five days.

The five new days did not belong to any month of the year. So Ra's curse could not touch them. And on those five days, Nut had five children, one on each day.

On the first day, Osiris was born. And as he came into the world, a voice was heard, saying, "The Lord of All is born!"

On the second day, Horus the Elder was born.

On the third day, Set was born. He did not come into the world at the right time, or in the right way. And so the Egyptians thought that the third day was unlucky.

On the fourth day, Isis was born.

And on the fifth day, Nephthys was born.

Later, Set married Nephthys. And Osiris and Isis loved each other, and they married too. And the five extra days were kept every year as the birthdays of the gods.
