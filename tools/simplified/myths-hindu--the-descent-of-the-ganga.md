---
title: The Descent of the Ganga
adaptedFrom: dutt-ramayana--the-descent-of-the-ganga
order: 7
namesNote: From the Ramayana. The Ganga is India's holiest river, and Hindus call it Mother Ganga. It is also called Bhagirathi, after Bhagiratha, and Jahnavi, after the sage Jahnu.
---

Long ago, the sixty thousand sons of King Sagara were burned to ashes by the great sage Kapila. Their souls could not rest, and they could not go to heaven, until their ashes were washed by the waters of the river Ganga. But in those days the Ganga flowed only in heaven.

King Sagara could not find a way to bring the Ganga down to earth. Nor could his grandson Anshuman, nor Anshuman's son Dilipa, though they wished it with all their hearts. Then Dilipa's son, Bhagiratha, became king. He gave his kingdom into the care of his ministers, and went to the mountains to pray. For a thousand years he prayed and fasted, eating only once a month, with his arms raised, and with fires burning all around him.

At last Brahma, the grandfather of all creatures, came to him with the gods. "I am pleased with you, Bhagiratha," he said. "Ask me for a gift."

"If you are pleased with me," said Bhagiratha, "let the sons of Sagara be washed by the waters of the Ganga, so that they can go to heaven."

"It shall be so," said Brahma. "But the earth cannot bear the weight of the Ganga as she falls. Only Shiva, who carries the trident, can hold her. Ask him to help you."

So Bhagiratha stood on one toe for a whole year and prayed to Shiva. At the end of the year, Shiva said, "I am pleased with you. I will catch the Ganga on my head."

Then the Ganga, in a huge and mighty shape, threw herself down from the sky onto Shiva's head. She was proud, and she thought, "I will sweep Shiva away with me, down into the world under the earth." But Shiva knew what she was thinking, and he was angry, and he caught her in the tangled locks of his hair. However hard she tried, she could not find her way out to reach the earth, and she wandered about in his hair for many years.

So Bhagiratha prayed again, and Shiva was pleased, and he let the Ganga go into the lake of Bindu. As she flowed out, she split into seven streams. Three flowed to the east, and three flowed to the west, and the seventh followed Bhagiratha. Bhagiratha rode ahead in his chariot, and the Ganga followed him.

Down she came from the sky, onto Shiva's head and then onto the earth, with a sound like thunder. The earth sparkled with leaping fish and tortoises and dolphins. The gods and sages and heavenly spirits came in their chariots and on their elephants and horses to watch, and their shining jewels lit up the sky as if there were a hundred suns. The river rushed on, sometimes fast and sometimes winding, sometimes rising up and sometimes falling, water crashing against water. Everyone who bathed in her was washed clean of their wrongs, and was filled with joy. And wherever Bhagiratha went, the Ganga followed, and all the gods and sages and creatures of the water followed her.

On the way, the Ganga flooded the place where the sage Jahnu was making a sacrifice. Jahnu was angry, and he drank up all her waters. The gods and sages were amazed, and they begged him to let her go, and said that the Ganga would be his daughter. So Jahnu let her out again through his ears. That is why the Ganga is also called Jahnavi, the daughter of Jahnu.

Then the Ganga followed Bhagiratha's chariot again, all the way to the ocean, and down into the world under the earth, to the place where the sons of Sagara lay in ashes. And the Ganga's waters flowed over the ashes, and the souls of the sixty thousand sons were freed, and they rose up to heaven.

Then Brahma said to Bhagiratha, "As long as the waters of the ocean last, the sons of Sagara will live in heaven like the gods. And the Ganga will be your daughter, and she will be called Bhagirathi, after you." So Bhagiratha made his offerings for his ancestors, and went home to his kingdom. He ruled there happily, and his people lived in peace.
