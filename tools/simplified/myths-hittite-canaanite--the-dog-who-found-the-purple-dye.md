---
title: The Dog Who Found the Purple Dye
adaptedFrom: smith-achilles-tatius--the-shepherds-dog-and-the-purple-dye

namesNote: Tyre was the great Phoenician city on the coast of what is now Lebanon, famous for its purple cloth. Achilles Tatius tells the legend as the people of Tyre told it.
---

The people of the city of Tyre, by the sea, made a wonderful purple cloth. It was so splendid that they said even the robe of the goddess of love was dyed with it. And this is how they said the purple was found.

Long ago, nobody knew about the purple dye. It lay hidden inside a little shellfish, shut up in its hard shell.

One day a shepherd was walking by the sea with his dog, and he found one of these shellfish. "I'll have the fish that's inside," he thought. But the shell was so hard that he could not open it. He grumbled at it, and he threw it into the sea, as if it were a useless bit of rubbish.

But his dog found the shell, and cracked it open with his teeth. And when he did, his mouth and his lips were stained a brilliant red, like blood.

The shepherd saw the dog's red mouth, and he was worried. "My poor dog has hurt himself!" he thought. So he took the dog down to the sea and washed his mouth. But the more he washed, the brighter the colour became. And when the shepherd touched it, his own hand turned purple.

Then the shepherd understood. The colour was not blood at all. It came from the shellfish, and it was a dye more beautiful than any other.

So he took some wool and pushed it into the opening of the shell, and the wool turned the same glowing colour as his dog's mouth. Then he broke the shell open with a stone, and inside he found the treasure: the purple dye.

And that is how the people of Tyre came to know the secret of purple.
