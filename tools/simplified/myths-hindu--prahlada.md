namesNote: From the Vishnu Purana, one of the holy books of the Hindus. Hiranyakashipu was king of the daityas, who were demons. Narasimha, the man-lion, is one of the avatars of Vishnu.

Long ago, the demon king Hiranyakashipu was given a gift by Brahma that made him so strong that he conquered all three worlds. He took the place of Indra, the king of the gods, and of the sun and the wind and the moon, and of the gods of water and of fire. All the offerings that people made to the gods came to him instead, and the gods ran away and hid on the earth, disguised as people.

Hiranyakashipu had a son called Prahlada. One day, when Prahlada came home from his teacher's house, his father asked him what he had learned. "I bow to the Great Being who has no beginning, no middle and no end," said Prahlada, "the Lord of the world, who is the cause of everything."

His father's eyes went red with anger, and he turned to the teacher. "You wicked man," he said, "you have taught my son to praise my enemy!"

"I did not teach him that," said the teacher.

"Then who taught you, my boy?" asked Hiranyakashipu.

"Vishnu, Father," said Prahlada. "He is the teacher of the whole world, and he is in all our hearts."

"Who is this Vishnu," shouted the king, "whom you keep talking about in front of me, the lord of the world?"

"He is the great God," said Prahlada, "whose glory no words can tell. Everything comes from him. He is not only my lord, but the lord of all creatures, and of you too, Father. Don't be angry!"

The king sent him back to his teacher. But a long time afterwards, when he called for Prahlada again and asked him to recite a poem, Prahlada sang the praises of Vishnu once more. Then Hiranyakashipu was furious, and he ordered his demons to destroy the boy.

Hundreds of demons attacked Prahlada with their weapons, but they could not hurt him. "Vishnu is in your weapons as well as in my heart," said Prahlada, "so they cannot hurt me." Then the king called for poisonous snakes, and they bit him all over. But Prahlada was thinking of Vishnu, and he felt nothing, and the snakes' fangs broke. The king called the great elephants who stand at the corners of the sky, and they struck him with their tusks, but their tusks broke against his chest. The king had a great fire built around him, but Prahlada said, "The fire does not burn me. All around me it is cool, like a bed of lotus flowers."

Then the king's priests begged him to spare the boy, and promised to teach him better. So the king had Prahlada brought out of the fire. But Prahlada did not stop praising Vishnu. He taught the other demon children to love Vishnu too, and to be kind to every creature.

So the king ordered his cooks to put poison in Prahlada's food. Prahlada said the name of Vishnu and ate it, and the poison did him no harm. Then the priests made a terrible spirit of fire by magic, and sent it against him. But it could not hurt him, and it turned back and burned the priests instead. Then Prahlada prayed to Vishnu. "I have never wished harm to anyone who tried to hurt me," he said. "If that is true, let these priests live again!" And the priests rose up unhurt, and blessed him.

Then the king had Prahlada thrown from the top of his palace onto the rocks far below. But the Earth herself came and caught him in her arms. The king's magician, Samvara, sent thousands of tricks and illusions against him, but Vishnu sent his flaming discus, Sudarshana, and it broke every one. The king sent a cold, withering wind into Prahlada's body, but Vishnu, in Prahlada's heart, drank up the wind. Then the king had Prahlada tied up with ropes and thrown into the sea, and great rocks piled on top of him.

There, at the bottom of the sea, under the rocks, Prahlada praised Vishnu with all his heart. And suddenly Vishnu himself stood before him, dressed in yellow. "I am pleased with you, Prahlada," he said, "because your love for me never wavers. Ask me for whatever you want."

"Let my love for you never grow less," said Prahlada, "in all the lives I may live."

"You have that already," said Vishnu. "Ask for something more."

"My father has done wrong, because he hated me for praising you," said Prahlada. "He attacked me with weapons, and threw me into the fire, and had me bitten by snakes, and poisoned, and thrown into the sea. Please, Lord, free my father from the wrong he has done."

"It shall be so," said Vishnu. "Ask me for one more thing."

"I have everything I want," said Prahlada, "now that my love for you will never fail."

"Then because your love is so strong," said Vishnu, "you will be set free at last from being born again." And Vishnu vanished.

Prahlada went back to his father, and bowed down before him. His father hugged him, and wept, and said, "Are you alive, my child?" And the demon king was sorry for what he had done, and was kind to his son. Long afterwards, when Vishnu took the form of Narasimha, the man-lion, and put an end to Hiranyakashipu, Prahlada became king of the demons, and because he was so good he ruled in glory, and had many children.
