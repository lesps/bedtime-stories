This story sounds very old, but it isn't. A poet made it up about two hundred years ago, and it was told and sung so often that people began to think it had always been there, like the great rock it is named after.

On the river Rhine, in a little town called Bacharach, there once lived a young woman named Lore Lay. She was so beautiful that everyone who saw her fell in love with her. People said she must be a witch, and that she cast spells on them with her eyes. But Lore Lay was not happy at all. The young man she loved had gone away to a faraway land, and he had never come back.

At last the bishop sent for her. He meant to punish her for her witchcraft. But when he saw how beautiful and how sad she was, he could not.

"Poor Lore Lay," he said gently. "Who taught you these wicked spells?"

"I have no spells," said Lore Lay. "My only magic is my eyes and my face. And I don't want anyone else to be unhappy because of me. My own heart is broken, because my love has gone away. Please, send me somewhere far from everyone."

So the bishop called three knights. "Take her to the convent," he said, "where she can live quietly with the nuns."

The three knights rode along the river, with sad Lore Lay riding in the middle. They came to a great tall rock that rose high above the Rhine.

"Please, knights," said Lore Lay, "let me climb up this rock. I want to look out one last time toward my love's castle, and down into the deep Rhine. Then I will go to the convent."

The rock was very steep, but she climbed up and up until she stood on the very top. The knights tied up their horses and climbed up after her.

Down on the river, a little boat was sailing by.

"Look!" cried Lore Lay, and her heart grew glad. "The man standing in that boat, it must be my love! He has come back at last!"

She leaned out over the edge to see him better, and she fell, down, down, into the deep Rhine. And the river carried her away.

The three knights could not get down from the steep rock, and they were never seen again. People still call it the Three Knights' Rock. And the boatmen on the Rhine say that if you call out "Lore Lay!" there, the rock calls back, "Lore Lay! Lore Lay! Lore Lay!"

Years later, another poet named Heinrich Heine wrote a song about the rock, which everyone in Germany knows. In his song, a beautiful maiden called the Lorelei sits on top of the rock in the evening sunshine. Her golden jewels sparkle, and she combs her long golden hair with a golden comb, and she sings a strange and wonderful song.

A boatman sailing down the river hears the song and looks up at her. He looks and looks, and he forgets to watch the river. He doesn't see the sharp rocks ahead of him. And the waves swallow up the boatman and his little boat.

That, says the song, is what the Lorelei does with her singing. So if you ever sail down the Rhine past the great rock, keep your eyes on the water.
